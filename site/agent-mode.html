DialogWright / Agent mode

Agent mode

A model holds the conversation, on a call and in a chat, from the business's own file. What is done stays fixed: every change is read back in the business's words and runs only when the caller confirms it, through the policy gate and into the audit. The model talks, and the runtime decides.

What it is

A runtime of its own beside the scripted engine. It shares the carriers, the chat, the gate, the audit, the traces, the console and the texts, and never imports the scripted turn (a test holds that). An app whose app.yaml does not say mode: agent never meets it.

  1. 1 The business is a file

    agent.yaml holds who the business is, its services and ballpark prices with the caveat said beside any of them, its hours, the questions callers ask with its answers, the lines said word for word, and its actions. A little code runs each action. No intents, forms, slots or prompts.

  2. 2 The model talks

    It words each reply its own way, answers what the caller asks within the business's world, and takes what the caller gives in whatever order it comes. Its replies are streamed a sentence at a time.

  3. 3 The runtime decides

    The model acts only by calling the business's actions. Each call's values are checked by type, put to the policy gate and audited. A change is read back by the line, not the model, and runs only when the caller confirms it.

Each assumption the demo had to make is marked in the file for the shop's owner to correct. A correction is an edit to the file, never to code (the demo's assumptions).

What stays fixed

What a change goes through, every time, whatever the model wrote:

  1. The model calls itWith its values as arguments: the only way a value reaches the runtime.
  2. Checked by typeA real date, a drop-off window only one a lookup returned on this call, and a choice one of its options.
  3. The gate decidesThe business's rules for the action. Allowed or refused, the decision goes to the audit.
  4. Read backThe line says the business's own words with exactly the call's values. The model cannot change them.
  5. ConfirmedThe decision model reads whether the caller confirmed it: confirming read at 0.7 or more, and declining under 0.3. Anything else runs nothing.
  6. Run, with its follow-onsThe follow-ons run with the same confirmation, each through the gate with its own hash: the photo text only where the caller agreed to texts.
  • Never left unsent, unaskedA change the caller has not confirmed is kept. The model's first goodbye is refused, so it asks whether they still want it.
  • A goodbye heldWhere the caller sounds like they are wrapping up while a change could be left unsent, the model's words are held, and a goodbye that would leave it unsent is never said.
  • Honest about what it isAsked whether it is a person, it says it is the business's automated assistant. A business cannot turn this off.
  • The line keeps the recordWhat was done on the call goes with every request to the model, so it never has to work it out.

Five rules, for every business

  1. Claim nothing the business information or a lookup on this call does not support. Prices come only from the information, with its caveat, times only from a lookup, and no callback time it does not give.
  2. Nothing changes until the caller confirms the line's read-back.
  3. Nothing is said to be booked, sent or done unless an action's result on this call says so.
  4. Whatever the caller is told is noted or passed on must be carried by an action they confirm.
  5. What the caller says about themselves is said back as they gave it, nothing added.

No check holds a reply before it is said. Unsupported claims are kept out by these rules and the actions, and measured by simulated callers (below). The model is sent the conversation and what each action returned, and the caller's number goes to it by its last four only. The policy card says all of it.

Journeys: the model sees only the task the caller is on

A line that does more than one thing gives each task its own journey: its goal, instructions, information and actions. Each caller turn, the decision model reads which one the caller wants, and the agent model is shown that journey alone. An action of another journey is refused before the gate.

Guide, don't guess. A turn the line cannot follow is unclear, never guessed at: the model guides the caller to what the line needs, only lookups are offered on that turn, and the third unclear turn in a row ends the call politely.

Measured on 300 simulated calls

  • 83%got what they came for (150 of 180), against 80% without journeys (72 of 90)
  • 4.6gaps per 100 calls, from 14.2: each task's own facts answer more of what callers ask
  • 0.68 to 0.71 sto the first sentence at the median, from 0.74 to 0.76 s
  • $0.0032 to $0.0033for the line's model a call, from $0.0038 to $0.0040

Wrong replies were 12.5 per 100 calls against 11.7 without journeys: the bar was "no worse", missed by 0.8, within the noise of runs this size. Serious ones were level, at 8.3. Later, with one journey for every request and the shop closed, 4% of 140 happy-path calls had a serious wrong reply. Journeys, measured, and after hours, measured.

On the phone

An agent app takes calls on Telnyx and Twilio as it takes chats. On a call:

  • Talk over any replyEach sentence goes to the carrier as it is written. A caller who speaks stops the turn, and their words are the next one, with what they heard.
  • A read-back heardConfirming counts only where the caller heard at least 85% of the read-back.
  • Said again, heldOnce a caller has talked over a change's read-back, every later read-back of it is said whole, and cannot be talked over (the maintainer's decision, 2026-11-26).
  • SilenceNine seconds after the reply has played, the model helps the caller go on. After one more silence, a polite goodbye, and the call ends.
  • After hoursThe model knows whether the business is open. A call that starts while it is closed hears the after-hours greeting, and no callback time is promised that the business does not give.
  • A bridge, only when it helps"One sec." is said once, only when a reply has said nothing after 2.5 seconds.

It's a fine line between time to think and "did it hang up on me?"

Measured

A line in agent mode is measured by simulated callers: a model plays each caller against the app's own server, and each reply is labelled or judged against the business information and the actions the gate decided. All the figures below are the wheel shop's. Its model is Muse Ember 1.0.

On the voice path

60 simulated calls over the voice path, the carrier played in text and replies at speaking pace, then the same 60 over the chat (2026-11-25). No speech recognition, speech or real carrier.

Callers who neither interrupt nor break their turns were done 20 of 20 on voice. The gap was callers who cut in on every long reply, the read-back too, so their confirmation never counted: 8 of 20 done. The held read-back (above) came from that finding. The voice run.

Wrong replies, kept from being said

Every reply of 120 simulated calls a set, labelled against the business information, the call's actions and what the caller said. What the runtime and the lookup know, told to the model plainly, halved the wrong replies.

The model, chosen by measurement

Eight models ran on the same simulated callers, five calls each, against the same bars, judged again under the material-claims rubric. No model cleared every bar. Three were fast enough, and of those Muse Ember 1.0 did best on both quality bars, and it is the wheel shop's.

What a call costs

The model's share of a call's cost is small in both modes, so the difference between them is a few percent of a call's total. Model costs are measured from the calls' tokens at the models' list prices. The carriers' are what each billed for the wheel shop's test calls, read from their own records on 2026-11-26. On both, the conversation relay's price includes speech recognition and the voice.

  • $0.0084a call for agent mode's models, at most: the agent model measured, the decision model an upper bound
  • $0.002a call for scripted mode's decision model, measured on one call
  • $0.19 and $0.236a call for everything else, as billed on Telnyx (the median) and on Twilio, the same in both modes
  • 2.6% to 3.2%of a call's total is the difference between the modes, about $0.006

Krisp is an option. It costs $0.005 a minute, about 8% of a Telnyx call: $0.015 to $0.02 on the wheel shop's calls, billed by the call's whole minutes. It is one setting, TELNYX_NOISE_SUPPRESSION=krisp (the guide's 13.1), and leaving it out saves it. What it bought is not yet measured alone: the loud-room calls compared Telnyx with flux and Krisp against Twilio (10 speech events against 135, no reply cut off against 7), and the same room has not yet been called on Telnyx without Krisp. So that result is the Telnyx path with Krisp, not Krisp alone. That measurement is to come.

The mode and the model for a line are chosen by what the job needs, not by their price.

Start

The wheel shop is the example: a repair request with a drop-off window, a dealership booking recognized by caller ID, and a callback note. Its tests run with no key, on a scripted model: 

pnpm --filter @dialogwright/example-wheelshop-agent test

Not every line suits it: keep a line scripted where every word must be its own, where callers prove who they are, or where it needs a knowledge base or more than one language (how to choose).
