DialogWright / Agent mode

Agent mode

A model holds the conversation, on a call and in a chat, from a file that describes the business. What gets done stays fixed. Before anything is booked, sent or changed, the line reads the details back in the business's own words, and it runs only when the caller confirms. Every action goes through the policy gate (the business's rules, checked in code) and into the audit log. The model talks, and the runtime decides.

What it is

Agent mode is a runtime of its own (the part that runs each call), beside the scripted one. It shares the rest of DialogWright: the phone carriers and the web chat, the policy gate and the audit log, the call records and the console, and texts. An app chooses it with mode: agent in its app.yaml, and a scripted app never meets it.

  1. 1 The business is a file

    A file, agent.yaml, holds who the business is, its services and ballpark prices (with a caveat said beside any price), its hours, the questions callers ask with the business's answers, the few lines said word for word (such as the greeting), and its actions. A little code runs each action. There is no call flow to design.

  2. 2 The model talks

    It words each reply its own way, answers what the caller asks within the business's world, and takes what the caller gives in whatever order it comes. Its replies are sent a sentence at a time, as they are written.

  3. 3 The runtime decides

    The model acts only by calling the business's actions (look up free times, send a request). Each call's details are checked by type, put to the policy gate and logged. Before anything is booked or sent, the line, not the model, reads the details back, and it runs only when the caller confirms.

The wheel shop is a demo, so each answer only its owner could give is marked in the file as an assumption to correct. A correction is an edit to the file, never to code (the demo's assumptions).

What stays fixed

What every booking, request or other change goes through, whatever the model wrote:

  1. The model calls the actionWith the details as arguments, the only way a value reaches the runtime.
  2. Checked by typeA real date, a time slot only one a lookup returned on this call, and a choice one of its options.
  3. The gate decidesThe business's rules for the action. Allowed or refused, the decision goes to the audit log.
  4. Read backThe line says the details in the business's own words, with exactly the values that will be sent. The model cannot change them.
  5. ConfirmedThe decision model reads whether the caller confirmed. It must read confirming at 0.7 or more and declining under 0.3, or nothing runs.
  6. Then it runsActions that follow it run with the same confirmation, each through the gate. In the wheel shop example, a text asking for a photo goes only to a caller who agreed to texts.
  • Never left unsent, unaskedA request the caller has not confirmed is kept. When the model first tries to end the call, it is stopped, so it asks whether they still want it.
  • A goodbye heldWhere the caller sounds like they are wrapping up while a request could be left unsent, the model's words wait, and a goodbye that would leave it unsent is never said.
  • Honest about what it isAsked whether it is a person, it says it is the business's automated assistant. A business cannot turn this off.
  • The line keeps the recordWhat was done on the call goes with every request to the model, so it never has to work it out.

Five rules, for every business

  1. Claim nothing the business information or a lookup on this call does not support. Prices come only from the information, with its caveat, times only from a lookup, and no callback time it does not give.
  2. Nothing changes until the caller confirms the line's read-back.
  3. Nothing is said to be booked, sent or done unless an action's result on this call says so.
  4. Whatever the caller is told is noted or passed on must be carried by an action they confirm.
  5. What the caller says about themselves is said back as they gave it, nothing added.

No check holds a reply before it is said. Unsupported claims are kept out by these rules and the actions, and measured by simulated callers (below). The model is sent the conversation and what each action returned, and the caller's number goes to it by its last four digits only. The app's policy card (a generated page in plain English) says all of it.

Journeys: the model sees only the task the caller is on

A line that does more than one thing (take a repair request, book a dealership's cars) gives each task its own journey: its goal, instructions, information and actions. Each time the caller speaks, the decision model reads which task they want, and the agent model is shown that task alone. An action from another task is refused before it reaches the gate.

Guide, don't guess. When the line cannot follow what the caller said, it does not guess. The model guides the caller to what the line needs, only lookups are allowed on that turn, and after the third such turn in a row the line ends the call politely.

On the phone

An agent app takes calls on Telnyx and Twilio as well as chats. On a call:

  • Talk over any replyEach sentence goes to the carrier as it is written. A caller who speaks stops the turn, and their words are the next one, with what they heard.
  • A read-back heardThe caller's confirmation counts only if they heard at least 85% of the read-back.
  • Said again, heldOnce a caller has talked over the read-back of a booking or request, every later read-back of it is said whole and cannot be talked over (since 2026-11-26).
  • SilenceWhen the caller says nothing for nine seconds after a reply has played, the line checks they are still there and helps them go on. After one more silence, it says goodbye politely and ends the call.
  • After hoursGiven the business's opening hours, the model knows whether it is open. A call that starts while it is closed hears the after-hours greeting, and no callback time is promised that the business does not give.
  • A bridge, only when it helps"One sec." is said once, only when a reply has said nothing after 2.5 seconds.

It's a fine line between time to think and "did it hang up on me?"

Jason Stiles, on how long the line should wait when a caller goes quiet before it checks they are still there

Measured

Agent mode is measured with simulated callers: a model plays each caller against the app's own server, and each reply is labelled or judged against the business information and the actions the gate decided. Every figure below comes from the wheel shop example, whose model is Muse Ember 1.0.

Wrong replies, kept from being said

Each set is 120 simulated calls, every reply labelled against the business information, the call's actions and what the caller said. Telling the model plainly what the runtime and the lookup know halved the wrong replies.

The model, chosen by measurement

Eight models ran on the same simulated callers, five calls each, against the same bars, judged again counting only claims that matter (a price, a time, a policy, something said to be done). No model cleared every bar. Three were fast enough, and of those Muse Ember 1.0 did best on both quality bars, so the wheel shop uses it.

What a call costs

The model's share of a call's cost is small in both modes, so the difference between them is a few percent of a call's total. Model costs are measured from the calls' tokens at the models' list prices. The carriers' are what each billed for the wheel shop example's test calls, read from their own records on 2026-11-26. On both, the conversation relay (the carrier's service that hears the caller and speaks the line's words) includes speech recognition and the voice in its price.

  • $0.0084a call for agent mode's models, at most: the agent model measured, the decision model an upper bound
  • $0.002a call for scripted mode's decision model, measured on one call
  • $0.19 and $0.236a call for everything else, as billed on Telnyx (the median) and on Twilio, the same in both modes
  • 2.6% to 3.2%of a call's total is the difference between the modes, about $0.006

Krisp is an option. It costs $0.005 a minute, about 8% of a Telnyx call: $0.015 to $0.02 on the wheel shop's calls, billed by the call's whole minutes. It is one setting, TELNYX_NOISE_SUPPRESSION=krisp (the guide's 13.1), and leaving it out saves it. What it bought is not yet measured alone: the loud-room calls compared Telnyx, with Deepgram's flux recognizer and Krisp, against Twilio (10 speech events against 135, no reply cut off against 7), and the same room has not yet been called on Telnyx without Krisp. So that result is the Telnyx path with Krisp, not Krisp alone. That measurement is to come.

Start

The wheel shop example takes a repair request with a drop-off window, books a dealership's cars (recognizing the dealership by the number it calls from) and leaves callback notes. Its tests run with no key, on a scripted stand-in for the model: 

pnpm --filter @dialogwright/example-wheelshop-agent test

Not every line suits it. Keep a line scripted where every word must be its own, where callers must prove who they are, or where it needs a knowledge base or more than one language (how to choose).
