Two ways to build a phone line

By Jason Stiles, November 26, 2026

DialogWright now has two ways to build a line. Both understand callers in their own words, and both hold what is done to the same policy gate and the same hash-chained audit. They differ in who words the replies.

  • ScriptedA decision model reads what the caller means through typed questions, code decides what happens, and every line is said word for word, generated only where the app opts in, and checked. For a business that needs every word predictable: regulated lines, high volume, identity checks.
  • Agent modeA model holds the conversation from the business's own file, agent.yaml: its services, prices, hours, questions and actions. For a small business that wants a natural conversation, with what gets done still fixed. The example is the wheel shop agent.

What stays fixed in agent mode

  • Every change is read back in the business's own words, with exactly the values it will carry.
  • It runs only when the caller confirms it, which the decision model reads.
  • Every action goes through the policy gate and into the audit, allowed or refused.
  • Follow-on actions run with the same confirmation, each through the gate.
  • A change the caller has not confirmed is never left unsent at the end of a call without asking.

How long to wait

The agentic IVRs can make people need to think a little more, since we aren't directing them what to say. It's a fine line between time to think and "did it hang up on me?" So we can't go too long.

So once its reply has played, the line waits nine seconds. Then the model helps the caller go on, and after one more silence the line says a polite goodbye and the call ends. A business can change the wait, or turn it off.
