What a call costs, and why it should not decide your design

By Jason Stiles, November 26, 2026

We've got two cheap but effective models: Jev, the decision model, and Muse Ember, the model that holds the conversation in agent mode. Ember costs more, but it's a rounding error on the telephony costs.

That means the right approach is using the model and the call type that's best for the job, not one dictated by price. Telnyx as the carrier, and the work we did so it could handle barge-in, is a bigger money saver against Twilio's fees than decision model against agent mode ever could be.

The numbers

  • Under 1¢a call for the models, in either mode
  • $0.19a call on Telnyx for the phone line, recognition and voice, as billed
  • $0.24a call on Twilio, as billed
  • About 3%of a call's cost is the difference between agent mode and scripted

The phone line is most of the bill. Choosing Telnyx over Twilio saves about six cents on a typical call, ten times what separates the two modes.

A Telnyx call, by partA minute
Conversation relay (hears the caller and speaks the replies)$0.050
The phone call itself$0.005
Krisp noise suppression, optional$0.005
Total$0.060 with Krisp, $0.055 without

Krisp is the one part you choose. It costs about two cents on a typical call, and in my loud-room test it was part of what let the line hear only me. I haven't yet measured it on its own, and will.
