# 16. Generated wording

[Contents](README.md) · Previous: [15. The adversarial suite](15-the-adversarial-suite.md) · Next: [17. Tuning with live calls](17-tuning-with-live-calls.md)

Every line an app says is fixed unless the app asks otherwise. Generated wording lets an app have chosen lines worded by a model for the moment (acknowledging what the caller just said, warmer when they are frustrated, a short summary of a message rather than the whole of it), held to the line's meaning and data by checks, with the fixed line said whenever a check fails or the model is slow. The core has decided before a line is worded: generated wording changes how a line is said, never what is decided, asked, refused or done. The design and the maintainer's decisions are [designs/2026-10-13-phase9-generated-wording.md](../designs/2026-10-13-phase9-generated-wording.md); the engine's part is `packages/dialogwright/src/render/`.

An app that marks no line generated never meets any of this: its turns, traces, audit rows, model requests, cassettes and policy card are exactly as they were.

## 16.1 When to use it, and when not

Which lines may be generated is the app's choice, never the framework's. Some uses:

- **A warmer re-ask.** A question asked again after a miss is where a fixed line sounds most like a machine. Generate it only then: `when: { situations: [re-ask] }`.
- **An acknowledgement that fits.** "Thanks, and what's the best number to call you back on?" after a name, rather than the same line every time.
- **Reading back a summary of what was collected.** A secretary reads back the gist of a message, not every word; the exact message is still what is kept, and read word for word when the caller asks. The pattern is a framed line: the line is fixed and says whose words follow, and only the summary inside it is generated (16.3).
- **An entirely generated experience.** A form's switch (`wording: generative`) generates every one of its lines, and an app may set it on every form.

What a line must say exactly (a legal notice, a disclosure, a figure the caller relies on, a keypad instruction) is the designer's to keep fixed. Mark such a line `prescribed: true`: it is said word for word whatever a form says, and `pnpm check` refuses `mode: generative` on it. The framework does not decide what is regulated; the policy card (16.8) lists every generated line and every prescribed one, so a reviewer sees exactly which lines a model words. A knowledge base's answers stay word for word ([12](12-the-knowledge-base.md)): a line that says one (it names `{answer}`) is never generated, `pnpm check` refuses `mode: generative` on it, and a form's switch leaves it fixed.

## 16.2 Turning it on

On a line, in prompts.yaml:

```yaml
# prompts.yaml, under prompts:
  ask_outageStart:
    text: When did the power go out?
    interruptible: true
    mode: generative
    goal: Ask when the outage started; briefly acknowledge what they just said.
    when:
      situations: [re-ask, frustrated]
      channels: [voice]
```

- `mode: generative` generates the line; it needs a `goal`, what the line must do, which the model is told. The `text` stays: it is the meaning the wording is held to, the fallback, and what a repeat of the line says.
- `when` (optional) limits where it is generated; everywhere else the fixed line is said. `situations`, any of: `first-ask` (the line has not been said before on this call), `re-ask` (it has: asked again after a miss, a no or a change), `after-correction` (on this turn the caller changed a value they had given, or said no to a read-back), `frustrated` (the caller has been frustrated on this call). `channels`: `voice`, `chat`. Both parts must hold.
- `prescribed: true` keeps the line word for word, always.
- `mode: fixed` keeps one line of a generated form fixed.
- `acknowledge: false` (optional) says the line's wording adds no acknowledgement of what the caller just said: the model is told to reword the fixed line alone, and is not sent the caller's words (16.5). Being told is not a guarantee: what holds it is that a figure only the caller said is refused in such a line (`foreign`, 16.6), and the drift check, which is not sent the caller's words either, so a name only they said is one of the line's own to it; a thanks passes the checks. The default, `true`, lets it begin with a brief acknowledgement ("Thanks.", "Got it."). Whatever this says, a line the turn says after an acknowledgement of the core's (`ack_intent`, `read_back_exact`, any ack before the line, or a line of the engine's own such as a language switch's) is generated as with `acknowledge: false`: two acknowledgements never stack ("Sure, I can help you take a message. Sure, I can take that down. Who is..."). An acknowledgement is where a model most often goes past its line (16.6, `drift`): use it on a line where that matters more than sounding warm. `pnpm check` refuses it on a line that is not generated, on a framed line (a span acknowledges nothing) and in a locale's prompts.yaml.

On a form, in forms.yaml:

```yaml
forms:
  take_message:
    slots: [recipient, caller, phone, message]
    summaryPromptId: confirm_take_message
    hooks: [confirmedParams, complete]
    calls: [leaveMessage]
    wording: generative
```

`wording: generative` generates the form's lines: each of its slots' questions (`ask_<slot>` and every `ask_<slot>_<anything>`: its retry, no-input and partial lines), their read-backs (`confirm_<slot>` and its variants) and its summary, unless a line says `mode: fixed` or is prescribed. A line with no `goal` of its own is worded to say what its fixed text says. A form line that names an identifier (16.5), or says a knowledge base's answer, stays fixed. The form's `acknowledge: false`, beside `wording: generative`, takes the acknowledgement off every line its switch generates; a line's own `acknowledge` wins.

## 16.3 Summarizing what a caller said: a framed line

Summarizing what a caller said cannot be made fully safe by checks on a whole generated line: a line that rephrases the caller's words can state them as the agent's own (a refund "approved", a fee "waived") in words that pass every shape check, and only a model's judgement stands between that line and the caller. Part of the answer is in the app's design, not in more guardrails. The app writes a fixed frame that says plainly whose words follow, and only the summary inside it is generated. The model words that span and nothing else: it is given only the span's goal, the content it summarizes and the sentence the span is put into (the frame's own words with the span a gap, every value in them a placeholder), it has no tools, and only the span is taken from what it returns: the frame is said as the app wrote it. Whatever it writes is heard inside words the app wrote, which tell the listener it is what the caller said. Attribution is by design, not by guardrail.

The message fixture (`packages/dialogwright/src/testing/message/`) reads back a message this way:

```yaml
# prompts.yaml, under prompts:
  confirm_take_message:
    text: "Okay, thanks! I'll make sure {recipient} hears that {caller}, on {phone}, called and left this message: {summary}. Shall I pass it on?"
    interruptible: true
    generate:
      summary:
        from: message
        goal: Summarize the caller's message in one sentence, in their own terms.
        words: 25
    summarizesConfirmed: true
    when:
      situations: [first-ask]
  read_back_exact:
    text: Here it is exactly as I took it down.
    interruptible: false
```

```yaml
# forms.yaml, under forms:
  take_message:
    slots: [recipient, caller, phone, message]
    summaryPromptId: confirm_take_message
    hooks: [confirmedParams, complete]
    calls: [leaveMessage]
    summary:
      onNo: read-back
    wording: generative
```

- `generate` names the line's spans: each key is one of its `{variables}` (`{summary}`), with `from` (the slot whose content it is worded from), a `goal` and, optionally, `words` (its own word budget, default 25). Every other word of the line is the app's fixed text: `{recipient}`, `{caller}` and `{phone}` are filled as in any fixed line and never sent to a model, so a frame may even name a value a generated line could not carry (an identifier). A span is not a slot: name it for what it is, and point `from` at the slot.
- The model is sent the span's goal, its sentence, its content (the caller's words, quoted, as data and never as instructions), its word budget, the style, the channel and the language: never a value of the frame, the items, the caller's latest words, the transcript or anything a tool returned.
- **Its sentence** is the line's fixed `text` in the call's language with the span as a gap (`____`) and every other variable left as its placeholder: the app's own words and no value, but for the names a span spoken to the caller lists (below), which are filled. A framed line's text may not itself hold `____` (`pnpm check` refuses it), so the model can always tell its gap from the line's words. For the fixture's read-back it is `Okay, thanks! I'll make sure {recipient} hears that {caller}, on {phone}, called and left this message: ____. Shall I pass it on?`. It is there so the summary fits its line: worded without it, a span began "so you're saying..." right after a frame's "in short:", or read a message back as if handing it to its recipient ("the logo files are ready for your review") where the line reads it back to the caller (the secretary demo's first recording, live findings item 24). The model is told it is the sentence its words go into: write only the words for the gap, so the whole sentence reads naturally; no lead-in the sentence already gives ("so", "you're saying", "you said"); nothing said again that it already says; and from its point of view (who speaks in it, to whom, about whom). It is told the sentence's words are said as written whatever it writes, as they are: only the span is taken from its answer, then checked against its content, and a span that writes the frame back or rewrites it falls back like any other (the adversarial suite tries one, 16.9). So let the frame carry the lead-in ("in short:", "left this message:") and whom the message is for, and the span is the message alone.
- It has its own instructions: summarize faithfully, in the caller's own terms, one sentence or less, no question, no closing punctuation (the frame has its own), beginning in lowercase unless the first word is a name (the span follows the frame's words mid-sentence; a summary verb such as "Asks" or "Wants" capitalized anyway passes the checks; a name the caller's words do not have is the judge's to refuse).
- `from` names a slot of the line's form (for a line of a form: its questions, read-backs and summary are given the form's slots); `pnpm check` refuses another. A span worded from a slot the records mask (a callback number kept by its last four) sends the value to the model whole: `pnpm check` warns of it (the app's choice), and the policy card says so on the span. Say such a value in the frame instead.
- Said with recorded clips (app.yaml's prompts audio), a framed line keeps its frame's clips when its span is generated: the span is spoken by TTS in its place, as a variable is.
- The span is checked by code before anyone hears it, for its data and shape (16.6): it asks nothing (the frame's question is fixed); it names no other value of the frame's own items (another recipient); it keeps to its budget and the phrases style.yaml avoids; and it names no figure, amount or date its content does not. What it means, a name its content does not have among it, is the drift judge's: the decision model, asked whether the span, heard in its sentence, is a faithful summary of the quoted words (`renderSpanDrift`, given the sentence with the span's words in its gap; what the sentence already says, such as whom the message is for, the span need not say again), refuses one that adds to them, turns them around, leaves out a negated statement, a refusal, a condition, a time, an amount or who is to act, speaks as the agent or changes whose words they are, even where what it keeps is accurate. Before the span is worded, the injection screen reads the content again, alone.
- **More caution, or less.** The judge refuses a wording it holds 0.5 likely or more to be unfaithful; style.yaml's `judge: { refuseAt: 0.3 }` makes it refuse more (more callers hear their exact words). A cutoff above 0.5 is less cautious than the default (more of what the judge doubts is said): the app's choice, which `pnpm check` warns of and the policy card names. For a line whose words must never be a model's, mark it `prescribed: true`. (A span's `checks: standard | strict` was removed, live findings item 25: `pnpm check` refuses it with the fix to delete it.)
- A span the model words as its content itself (the same words, letter case, punctuation, spacing and the kind of apostrophe aside) is said unchanged: as the exact content, with no drift check asked, so a frame for the exact words (`exactText`, below) is said, and a frame written for a summary never comes before the caller's own words. It is no fallback (no check failed): the trace's span entry says `unchanged` (not to be confused with the `exact` skip, a line the core asks for word for word), no `render_fallback` row is written, and the regression's line, `render:bakeoff` and the console's Insights count it apart ("said unchanged"), never as a fallback.
- Any failure, a timeout or a provider's error fills the span with the exact content, word for word, and the audit records `render_fallback` with the prompt and the span. The frame is said as written either way: a renderer cannot reach it.
- Word the frame so it reads true whether the span is a summary or the exact content ("left this message:", not "summarized:"): the exact content is what is said on a fallback, on a read-back word for word and on a repeat.
- Or give the exact content a frame of its own. `exactText` beside `text` is the frame said whenever every span is its exact content (no model set up, a check that fails, a slow model or an error, a span the model words as its content word for word, a call in the fixed group, a reading `when` leaves fixed, a read-back after a no, a repeat); `text` is then said only with a generated span. So a frame may say what is true of a summary alone:

  ```yaml
  confirm_take_message:
    text: "Thanks, I'll make sure {recipient} gets your message: {caller} called and said, in short, {summary}. Shall I pass it on?"
    exactText: "Thanks, I'll make sure {recipient} gets your message: {caller} called and said: {summary}. Shall I pass it on?"
    interruptible: true
    generate:
      summary: { from: message, goal: Summarize the caller's message briefly, in their own terms. }
  ```

  It names the same variables as `text` (`pnpm check` holds it to that, and refuses it on a line that is not framed or is prescribed; an app built in code is refused it the same way by `validateApp`, and on a line that is not framed it is never said). It is the line's fixed text: what the session keeps as the line just played, what the decision model is told was asked, and what a repeat says. A line with two spans says `text` when either is generated, and `exactText` only when both are exact, so put wording that is true of a summary only right next to the span it describes ("in short, {summary}"), where it reads as that span's. With recorded clips, its fixed words have clips of their own, `<id>.exact.0` and on. Without `exactText`, `text` is said either way, as before. The policy card lists both frames.
- **Speaking to the caller: `speaks: to-caller`.** By default a span is worded in the caller's own terms, as they said it ("call me back"), under a frame that gives their words ("{caller} called and left this message: {summary}"). A receptionist reads a message back the other way, to the caller and about their own message: "The project is complete and I just need him to give his approval" read back as "the project is complete and you need Sam's approval" (not "your project": the caller did not say "my project"). A span that says `speaks: to-caller` is worded so:

  ```yaml
  confirm_take_message:
    text: "Okay, thanks! I'll make sure {recipient} gets your message, {caller}, with your number, {phone}. In short, {summary}. Shall I pass it on?"
    exactText: "Okay, thanks! I'll make sure {recipient} gets your message, {caller}, with your number, {phone}. Here it is as you said it: {summary}. Shall I pass it on?"
    interruptible: true
    generate:
      summary:
        from: message
        goal: Give the caller their message back in one sentence, speaking to them.
        speaks: to-caller
        names: [recipient]
  ```

  - The model is given the goal and the full context, never a procedure for the words: the line is spoken to the caller, the person who left the message, and gives them their own message back before it is passed on; the sentence with its gap; the caller's words; the names it may use (`names`); and the style. It is asked for a span that, heard by the caller in that sentence, gives them their own message, shorter or plainer, with nothing added and nothing changed or left out that matters (what is asked or said, who is to do what for whom, when, how much, any no or condition). How a "my" or a "me" reads back to the person who said it is the model's to word and the judge's to read, not a rule the engine writes: an earlier version told the model to say the caller's "I" and "my" as "you" and "your", and the secretary demo's third recording heard the read-backs that gave (live findings item 27): "please call me back about the proofs" read back as "asking you to call back", "the logo files are ready" as "your logo files are ready". These are instructions of its own (`SPAN_INSTRUCTIONS_TO_CALLER`), sent only for such a span.
  - **`names`** lists, explicitly, the line's variables whose value the span may use (to name whom the message is for, where the message means them): who the message is for, each a `choice` slot of the line (`[recipient]`). They are the app's own option text, not the caller's words, so they are not screened for an injection (the content is, as for any span); they are sent to the model and to the drift check, each under its variable, as the line says them, and filled in the span's sentence (`Thanks! I'll make sure Sam Okafor gets your message, in short: ____. I have you as {caller}, on {phone}. ...`). A name the app wrote with a capital ("Sam Ortiz") is read in the message whatever its case; one it wrote lowercase ("the team") only as written, so a common word is not taken for the name by luck. No other value of the frame is sent (not the caller's name, not the number): its words are, as the span's sentence, with those values as placeholders. `pnpm check` refuses a `name` slot there (the caller's own name is never put in place of a pronoun of their message), an identity factor, a slot with a redact setting, a choice whose options say a digit, and `names` on a span that does not speak to the caller. Without `names`, the model is told to name no one the message does not. Code does not read names (16.6): a name the span says that neither the message nor `names` has is the drift judge's to refuse. The policy card lists the names such a span is sent.
  - Its checks (16.6) are any span's, of its data and shape. The names it lists are given to the model, which may use them, and to the drift judge as whom the message is for (`frame`); a name, listed or not, is the judge's to read, as every name is. Whose words it gives, who does what to whom, a "you" of the message taken for the caller, a "your" for no "my", a "no" about a person: all of these are meaning, the drift judge's.
  - Whether a name stands for the one meant, and whether who does what to whom is kept, is the drift judge's: its question for such a span (`renderSpanDrift`, asked the to-caller way) gives it the context and nothing on how a read-back should be worded: the line is spoken to the caller, the person who left the message; the sentence as the caller will hear it, with the span in its gap; the caller's words, quoted; and the listed names as `frame`. It is asked whether the span, as the caller hears it, is anything other than a faithful summary of their message: one that changes who does what to whom or whose something is, or names someone the message does not make clear is meant, is not ("asking you to call back" for "please call me back"; "you need Sam to call the printer" for "tell Riley I need him to call the printer"). The engine's labelled cases hold such read-backs beside their faithful twins (16.13). The stub regression's judge is a simple test double (16.9): it reads no persons.
  - **The frame must read right with it.** A span that speaks to the caller wants a frame that speaks to them ("{recipient} gets your message, in short: {summary}"), not one that gives their words in the third person: "{caller} called and said, in short, you need Sam's approval" reads wrong. `pnpm check` warns of a to-caller span after, in its sentence of the frame, "said", "says" or "told", "left ... message", "wants ... to know", or a `name` slot's variable ("{caller}"): a cheap reading of the frame's words. The exact words are the caller's own, in the first person, so give them a frame of their own that says so (`exactText`: "Here it is as you said it: {summary}"); `pnpm check` warns of a to-caller span on a line without one, whose `text` would be said with the exact words too ("...in short, The project is complete and I just need him to...").
  - English only, for now: its read-backs and their judge are measured on English cases only (16.13). In another language (a call in a locale that is not English, or an app whose prompts.yaml is not English) the span is worded in the caller's own terms, as without the option, and `pnpm check` warns where that applies (a locale that gives the span its goal, or prompts.yaml's own). `speaks` and `names` are read from prompts.yaml, for every locale (`pnpm check` refuses them in a locale's file).
  - Off by default (`as-caller`, or no `speaks`): every other span's request and its drift question are the caller's-own-terms ones.
- The exact content is always kept. The slot holds the message as the caller said it, the summary's yes arms exactly what the slots hold, and the tool is sent the message word for word, whatever the span said.
- The slot a span is worded from is said whole, so for a `text` slot it is `say: null`. With `redact: none` the message is in the trace and on the console as said (the action's param can still be recorded by its length, policy.yaml's `audit: length`). To keep it out of the records, give the slot `redact: length-summarized`, the one named exception to "a model is never sent a value the records hide whole" (16.5):
  - **Sent to a model.** The words go whole to the render model only for the span that summarizes them: a framed line's span worded from the slot. `pnpm check` (and `validateApp`, for an app in code) refuses a line generated whole from it, whose wording would be the line itself, and a line that names it as an item stays fixed. They also go to the decision model's checks of that span: the content screened again for an injection, and the drift check, which is given the words as quoted. In such an app no other generated line is sent the caller's words (`heard`): an acknowledgement is worded without them. The decision model reads every turn's words, as it always does.
  - **Kept, and said.** The words are kept in the session for the call (the session store holds them while the call is open, as it holds every slot's value), the frame's fallback, the read-back word for word and a repeat say them, and the tool is sent them.
  - **Masked by their length, in every record.** In the trace and on the console: the slot, the parts of our lines that say the words or the summary a model gave (the line kept, that part `<n chars>`: the actions, the decision, a repeat's words, the line just played, the span's render entry and its error), and every caller's words, on every turn, since the words that give the slot may come on any (asked for, at another question, in one breath with the request, on a retry, as a correction): the event's words, what an interruption heard of our line, joined fragments, the turn state the model saw (`asr.text`, `candidateSpans`), and the parts of the words the model was asked about (each span question's options and their answers, a pick's candidates); the questions themselves, the app's options and the answers stay whole and readable. In the frame log, written as the words arrive and before any turn has read them: every caller's words (each prompt, each typed text) and what an interruption heard, by their length, and our lines sent with the words and the summary masked, a line said again on a new socket among them; so a call of such an app cannot be replayed from its frame log (`--replay` refuses it, saying why). The console's live view of what the model is asked masks the same. The audit records the write's param by its length; a handoff hands the slot over by its length, whatever app.yaml's `handoff.data` says (intended: the person taking the call does not hear the message from the transfer). The server says at start, for each such app, that its frame log and trace keep no caller words.
  - **What stays elsewhere.** The recognizer and the decision model hear every caller's words, as for every app; a recording's cassettes hold the corpus's words, which are the app's own examples, not callers'.
  - `pnpm check` refuses it on an identity factor and on a slot no framed span summarizes (`redact: length` masks the same way there); the policy card lists the slot as "sent whole to the render model to summarize; masked in the records".
- A `text` slot's value is the whole turn's words: a caller who gives the message in one breath with their name and number ("it's Alex Rivera for Riley, the logo files are ready, call me on ...") gets that whole sentence as the message, read back with its name and number when it is said exact (the summary usually leaves them out). `pick` (the text type's option) chooses a part of the words, which could cut a real message short; for a message this is a known limit, best written down and scripted so the baseline shows it.
- A span that summarizes a value a `confirmed` rule holds the caller to needs `summarizesConfirmed: true` on the line, the app's acceptance; `pnpm check` asks for it, and the policy card says so on the action.
- Reading it back word for word is always there. A bare no to the summary, with the form's `summary: { onNo: read-back }`, says `read_back_exact` and the line again with the exact content, whatever its `when` says, with no attempt counted, on a call as in a chat; a second bare no asks what to change (`ask_change`), and the ladder goes on as always. Asking to hear it again at "what should I change?" reads it again, exact. A caller who asks to hear it again (`repeat_prompt`) hears the line with the exact content, on a call as in a chat, never the summary again: a repeat says the line's fixed text as the session keeps it, never the words a renderer gave, and a framed line's fixed text is its frame with each span its content (a line generated whole from collected content, `from`, is repeated the same way: its fixed text, the content word for word). A repeat is not a read-back on a no: `read_back_exact` is not said before it, and a bare no after it still reads the line back once, after `read_back_exact`, before asking what to change. With `when: { situations: [first-ask] }`, every later reading is exact too.
- `when`, `RENDER_SHARE` and the screen apply to a framed line as to a line generated whole: where the line would not be generated, each span is its exact content. A framed line is generated by its own `generate`, whatever its form's `wording` says; `prescribed: true` forbids it (`pnpm check` refuses `generate` on a prescribed line), and a framed line takes no `mode`, `goal` or `from` of its own.
- The same pattern summarizes a long transaction or a note: a fixed line that says what it is ("Here is what you asked us to change: {summary}."), the span worded from the slot that holds the caller's description.

**Generating a whole line from collected content** is still possible, as the app's choice: a line with `mode: generative` names in `from` the variables whose content it may summarize, and the checks of 16.6 and the drift check hold it to saying that content as the caller's (the drift check is given the fixed line with the content left as placeholders and the content apart, as quoted, and asks whether the line states any of it as the agent's own). Whether a line does is then a model's judgement, not the line's design, so `pnpm check` warns of every such line and points to the framed form; it never refuses it. The engine's own tests keep one (the message fixture's variant `WHOLE_LINE`, `src/testing/message/variant.ts`).

## 16.4 style.yaml

How a generated line sounds, optional, beside the other files:

```yaml
# yaml-language-server: $schema=../../packages/dialogwright/schemas/style.schema.json
persona: a warm, brief office receptionist
words:
  voice: 40
  chat: 50
avoid:
  - unfortunately
  - I apologize for the inconvenience
judge:
  refuseAt: 0.5
model:
  provider: openrouter
  id: google/gemini-3.1-flash-lite-preview
```

`persona` is told to the model (default: a calm, plain-spoken agent). `words` is the most words a generated line may use on each channel (default 25 on a call, 40 in a chat): a longer one is not said. `avoid` lists phrases the model is told to avoid, and a line that uses one anyway is not said. `judge.refuseAt` is where the drift judge refuses a wording: its probability that the wording is unfaithful, or does more than its goal, at or above which the fixed text is said (default 0.5; lower is more cautious; higher is less cautious than the default, which `pnpm check` warns of and the policy card names). `model` names the model that words the app's lines, the author's choice (measured with `render:bakeoff`, 16.12): `provider` (`openrouter` or `anthropic`) and `id` (the model id as the provider names it; default the provider's default model). It is what the server words with and what `regress --client recorded` replays (the cassette `fixtures/recorded/render/<model>.jsonl` of that model), as `RENDER_PROVIDER` and `RENDER_MODEL` would name it, so an app's tests replay the model its author chose without a setting on each machine. The environment overrides it (16.7): `RENDER_PROVIDER` naming another provider takes that one, with `RENDER_MODEL` or its default model; `RENDER_PROVIDER` naming the app's provider keeps the app's id unless `RENDER_MODEL` is set beside it. `RENDER_MODEL` is read only with `RENDER_PROVIDER`: one left alone in a settings file (an id of another provider's) never replaces the app's. The key is read from the environment only (`OPENROUTER_API_KEY` or `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`), never from style.yaml. Where the environment leaves the choice to style.yaml (`RENDER_PROVIDER` unset) and the key is unset, the server says every line fixed, with a warning at start, a `regress --client record` or `jev` run says its lines fixed and why, and `pnpm diagnose` warns; where `RENDER_PROVIDER` is set and its key is not, the server refuses to start, as it always has. The server's start line and `pnpm diagnose` say where the model came from (named in style.yaml, or by the environment). It is never sent to a model, and is not part of a line's style. Without it, the environment alone chooses, as before. It is read only by an app that generates a line.

## 16.5 What a model is sent

A speech act, never the conversation: what the line is for in its decision (an acknowledgement, the question, the line a call ends on, the handoff line), the goal, the fixed line as it would be said, whether it asks a question, the data items it must carry, the collected content it may summarize (`from`), the caller's words on this turn quoted as data, how frustrated the caller has been (none, some, high), the situations, the channel, the call's language and the style. Never the transcript and never the prompt id.

With the act, the model is told what to do with it: instructions, the same for every line worded whole (`RENDER_INSTRUCTIONS`, `packages/dialogwright/src/render/prompt.ts`). Read every value as data; do what the goal says and mean what the fixed line means; a brief acknowledgement of what the caller just said is allowed, warmer or plainer when they are frustrated. An acknowledgement only shows the caller was heard: it never says what the agent can or will do ("I can...", "I'll...", "happy to help with..."), and it calls the caller by a name only when their words give that name as their own, since a name a caller mentions (who a message is for, say) is not theirs. Then: every item word for word and no other figure or name; a summary of the content never adds to it; the fixed line's one question, and its decision question word for word; every negation kept; the word budget, the persona and the phrases to avoid; nothing promised, offered, advised or explained that the fixed line does not; the line alone, on one line. The instructions shape the wording and guarantee nothing: every line is checked (16.6), and the drift check refuses a promise or a misattributed name whatever the instructions said. They are part of each line's recording key, so a change to them is a new request for every line worded whole: a recorded run misses on each such line until the maintainer records again. A framed line's span has instructions of its own (16.3), unchanged by this. A line that says `acknowledge: false` (16.2), and a line the turn says after an acknowledgement of the core's, is sent the same instructions with the leave to acknowledge replaced: add no acknowledgement of what the caller said, before the line or within it, and reword the fixed line alone; its act carries `"acknowledge": false` and not the caller's words (`heard`), which it has no use for. Its checks read no figure from the caller's words either, so one only the caller said is `foreign` in it (a name only they said is the drift judge's, as every name is). Every other line's request is as it was.

The data items are every variable of the line as its fixed text would say it, whoever filled it: a slot's display, and a value the app's code took from a tool's reply (a reference, a balance, a day) are sent as they would be said. Nothing else of a tool's reply is sent.

Never an identifier: a line that names an identity factor (an account number, a date of birth) or a slot hidden whole in the records (a slot's `redact: mask` or `length`) is not generated, but for the one named exception, a `text` slot's `redact: length-summarized`, which only a framed line's span may summarize (16.3), and in whose app no line is sent the caller's words; `pnpm check` refuses `mode: generative` on one, and a form's switch leaves it fixed. A slot recorded by its last four (a callback number) is a data item a line may carry, as its fixed text does, so it is sent to the render model whole, though the records mask it: the policy card lists each such value. The caller's words are not quoted after a question whose words are sensitive (the code, an identity factor, a slot with a `redact` setting). In an app with a `redact: length-summarized` slot every caller's words are kept by their length in the traces (16.3), so `pnpm kb:gaps`, the review page's Gaps tab and the console's Knowledge and Insights pages find no caller questions there: no gap is found by its words (a question no topic was nominated for), and one read from the answers alone (a topic question answered none, a passage not said, a close call) shows its words by their length.

A turn whose words the injection screen flagged, or did not read (it failed), generates nothing: every line of it is said fixed, and nothing of it is sent to the model.

A knowledge answer worded for the caller's question (a span `from: answer`, 16.14) is sent its goal, its sentence, the approved passage (as its content, the only facts there are) and the caller's question in their words, quoted; never the topic's account line, a record, another passage or the transcript.

A framed line's span (16.3) is sent far less: the span's goal, its sentence (the line's fixed words in the call's language, the span a gap and every other value a placeholder: the app's own words, so that the span fits them), the content it is worded from (quoted), its word budget, the style, the channel and the language. Never a value of the frame, the caller's latest words or the situation; but a span that speaks to the caller (`speaks: to-caller`, 16.3) is also sent the names it lists (`names`: who the message is for, choice slots of the line, the app's own text and so not screened; never the caller's name nor a value the records hide), filled in its sentence too, and that it speaks to the caller. A span's instructions and its act are its recording key, and its drift check's state and question are that check's: giving the span its sentence (live findings item 24) re-keyed every recorded span and every recorded span drift check, so a recorded run misses on each until the maintainer records again.

## 16.6 The checks, the judge, and the fallback

Every generated line, and every span of a framed line, passes two kinds of check before anyone hears it. The first that fails is recorded, and the fixed text is said.

**Data and shape, exactly, by code.** These are rules about what was given, not about language, so they are exact:

| Reason | What failed |
|---|---|
| `empty` | the model said nothing |
| `format` | markup, a link, braces, a line break, or more than 600 characters (a knowledge answer: or than its passage, whichever is more); a span of more than one sentence (a stop and a capital after it: a line of its own written after the summary), but a knowledge answer's, which may have as many as its passage |
| `words` | more words than the budget (style.yaml, or a span's own `words`) |
| `question` | a line whose fixed text asks must end in exactly one question, one that does not ask must ask none, and a decision question (one a yes or a no settles: a summary, a read-back, a confirmation, an offer) is kept word for word, the line ending with the fixed text's own question; a span asks nothing |
| `frame` | a span copies four words running of its line's own fixed words (its sentence's, a run broken by the gap and each placeholder) that its content does not have: the frame written back, or rewritten, into the span, whatever its budget |
| `conflict` | another value of an item's own slot named (another option of a choice: the message for someone else), which the fixed text does not name; a value the collected content names may be said back as the content's, never as the one the line is for |
| `avoid` | a phrase style.yaml lists |
| `items` | a data item left out (each word for word, letter case aside) |
| `foreign` | a figure (a digit, a number word, an ordinal, an amount, a month, a weekday or a relative day such as today, or a currency sign) that is in none of what it was given: the fixed text, the goal, the items and the content (a span: its content; a knowledge answer: its passage alone, never the caller's question); the caller's words count for a figure only in an acknowledgement, which says back what was heard. Code reads no name: one found by its capital letter is found with every sentence's first word ("However, ...", "Professional sports ..." were refused on live calls as names), and a name a wording was not given is a fact of its own, which the drift judge refuses (the maintainer's decision, 2026-10-17) |
| `content-flagged` | the collected content (`from`), read again by the injection screen before the line was worded, was flagged, or could not be read |

**Meaning, by the drift judge.** Whether a line does more than its goal, states the caller's words as the agent's own, keeps a "no", a condition, a time, an amount and who is to act, gives the caller's words as theirs and keeps who does what to whom: that is language, and a model reads it. The judge is the decision model, on its own request, given everything the line was given: for a line worded whole, the line, its goal and its fixed text with the caller's words apart as quoted (`renderDrift`); for a span, the span heard in its sentence, its goal, the caller's words as quoted and, for a span spoken to the caller, the names it lists (`renderSpanDrift`); for a knowledge answer, the wording heard in its line, its goal, the approved passage and the caller's question (`renderAnswerDrift`: does it say anything other than what the passage says for that question). It answers how likely the wording is to be unfaithful; at 0.5 or more (style.yaml's `judge.refuseAt`) the wording falls back as `drift`. A summary adds no content (the maintainer, 2026-10-17): the span judge's criteria count as unfaithful a summary that names a person, a company or anything else the caller's words do not (who the message is from, whom it is for, what it is about), as the answer judge's count a fact of its own; for a span spoken to the caller, the names it lists are theirs to use. The judge's reading is kept on the line in the trace (`judge: { score, refuseAt }`), whether it passed the wording or refused it.

| Reason | What failed |
|---|---|
| `drift` | the judge refused the wording |
| `drift-error` | the judge could not be asked |
| `timeout` | the wording, or the checks, took longer than their budget |
| `error` | the model's request failed |
| `cassette-miss` | a replay had no recording of the line |

**Why there are no grammar rules.** The engine once held spans to hand-written English rules as well: a negation added or dropped, a "no" or a condition left out, whose person a pronoun was, the order of persons, a he or a she of another gender. Measured on labelled read-backs (16.13), the rules refused good summaries (one everyday read-back in six at their strictest, mostly spoken to the caller, each a caller hearing their exact words) and still missed what a judge reads (a time or an amount dropped, an instruction followed); they were English only, and each fix for one reviewer's case made the next case worse. Live findings item 25 removed them: deterministic rules belong to decisions and to data, and meaning is the judge's, given the full context. The judge is a judgement, not a guarantee: measure it with `render:judge-eval` (16.13) before changing its question, lower `judge.refuseAt` for more caution, and prescribe (`prescribed: true`) a line whose words must never be a model's. (Traces from before may hold `negation`, `person` or `omitted`; the console still words them.)

**The fallback.** A line that fails is said as its fixed text; a span, as its exact content inside the frame, which no wording reaches. A span the model words as its content itself is said unchanged (16.3), which is no fallback. Each fallback is a `render_fallback` row in the audit (the prompt and the reason, and a framed line's span) and is in the trace. A line that fell back as `error` carries in the trace why the request failed: the provider, the HTTP status and the API's error type (`anthropic: HTTP 400 invalid_request_error`; OpenRouter's code where it is a name, the status alone where it is a number), and the API's own message apart, short and on one line; never the key or the request.

In the stub regression the judge is a test double (`stubDrift`, `stubSpanDrift`), as the renderer is (the fake, 16.9): deterministic, conservative and simple, it reads no grammar, and a stub run's results say what the engine does with a wording, not how well a model reads one. On a live call, meaning rests on the real judge (measured in 16.13). It refuses words of an injection or an instruction, a figure or a name of the caller's words left out or one of the wording's own, and a wording with two words or more traceable to nothing it was given. A real judge reads the rest, so a recorded run may differ from the stub there.

## 16.7 Running it

| Setting | What it is | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `RENDER_PROVIDER` | `openrouter` (any model OpenRouter serves) or `anthropic`; over style.yaml's `model` (16.4) | style.yaml's `model`'s provider; with none, unset: no line is generated, every line is said fixed, and the server says so at start |
| `RENDER_MODEL` | the model id, read only with `RENDER_PROVIDER` set; over style.yaml's `model` | style.yaml's `model`'s id where its provider is in force; else a current Claude Haiku: `claude-haiku-5-5` on Anthropic, `anthropic/claude-haiku-5.5` on OpenRouter |
| `OPENROUTER_API_KEY`, `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` | the provider's key, from its own variable, never a flag; never logged, never in a trace | none |
| `RENDER_SHARE` | the share of calls (0 to 100) served generated; the rest are served fixed | `100` |
| `RENDER_TIMEOUT_VOICE_MS`, `RENDER_TIMEOUT_CHAT_MS` | how long a line's wording may take, each channel on its own (`RENDER_TIMEOUT_MS` sets both at once; a channel's own setting wins) | `900` on a call, `1500` in a chat |
| `RENDER_CHECK_TIMEOUT_VOICE_MS`, `RENDER_CHECK_TIMEOUT_CHAT_MS` | how long the decision model's checks of a line may take together (the content screened again, the drift check), each channel on its own (`RENDER_CHECK_TIMEOUT_MS` sets both at once; a channel's own setting wins) | `500` on a call, `1000` in a chat |
| `RENDER_ANSWER_TIMEOUT_VOICE_MS`, `RENDER_ANSWER_TIMEOUT_CHAT_MS` | how long a knowledge answer worded for the caller's question (16.14) may take, its wording and its checks together, from when the turn's rendering begins; past the two budgets above the caller first hears the hold line, past this the passage word for word (`RENDER_ANSWER_TIMEOUT_MS` sets both at once; a channel's own setting wins; never less than the two budgets above together) | `4000` on a call, `6000` in a chat |
| `RENDER_PRICE_INPUT_PER_MTOK`, `RENDER_PRICE_OUTPUT_PER_MTOK` | the model's price in dollars a million tokens, for the cost | the default model's: 0.10 and 0.50 |
| `RENDER_THINKING` | how the model thinks (reasons) while it words a line: `disabled`, `between-tools` (Anthropic only), `adaptive` or `omit` (below) | on Anthropic, the model's own least thinking, by the table below; on OpenRouter, reasoning off |
| `RENDER_EFFORT` | the effort the model is asked for: `low`, `medium` or `high` (Anthropic's `output_config.effort`, OpenRouter's `reasoning.effort`) | on Anthropic the table's (`low` for Claude Opus 5.5), else the model's own default; on OpenRouter none |

The Anthropic adapter calls the Messages API with thinking off where the model can turn it off (a line, at most 200 tokens; a line cut off at the limit is an `error`). Models turn it off differently, so the adapter asks each in its own way, by its id:

| Model | Sent | Thinks |
|---|---|---|
| Claude Haiku 5.5 (`claude-haiku-5-5`), and any model the table does not name | `thinking: { type: 'disabled' }` | no |
| Claude Sonnet 5.5 (`claude-sonnet-5-5`) | `thinking: { type: 'between_tools' }` (it refuses `disabled`) | no |
| Claude Opus 5.5 (`claude-opus-5-5`) | no `thinking`, `output_config: { effort: 'low' }` (it refuses `disabled` at every effort) | yes, as little as it can |
| Claude Fable and Claude Mythos (`claude-fable-…`, `claude-mythos-…`) | no `thinking` (they refuse `disabled`) | yes |

`RENDER_THINKING` overrides the table: `disabled`, `between-tools`, `adaptive` (`thinking: { type: 'adaptive', display: 'omitted' }`: the model thinks as much as it judges it needs) or `omit` (no `thinking`, the model's own default); `RENDER_EFFORT` (`low`, `medium`, `high`) sends `output_config: { effort }`. Both are read with the other settings, and apply to OpenRouter too (below). With thinking on, a request may take up to 4,000 tokens rather than 200, since the thinking counts against that limit (the line is still held to its checks); only the line's text is read, the thinking never (with `display: 'omitted'` it comes back empty), and its tokens are counted in the line's usage and cost. `pnpm diagnose` and the server refuse a setting the model would refuse: `disabled` for Claude Sonnet 5.5, Opus 5.5, Fable or Mythos; `between-tools` for any model but Claude Sonnet 5.5; `adaptive` for a model older than adaptive thinking (Claude 3, and the 4 family before 4.6, such as `claude-haiku-4-5`). Thinking takes time: a model that must think (Claude Opus 5.5, Fable, Mythos) is slow for a line on a call, where `RENDER_TIMEOUT_VOICE_MS` (900 ms by default) is all a line's wording gets; a late wording is dropped and the fixed line said, so a model that thinks will mostly fall back as `timeout` unless the budget is raised, and a caller waits that much longer. To find out whether thinking helps a model follow its instructions, and what it costs in time, record the same app with it off and on (`RENDER_THINKING=adaptive RENDER_EFFORT=low`): a run with the table's setting changed records into a cassette of its own (16.9), and the run's summary gives each line's latency. `render:bakeoff` (16.12) records both in one run and tables them side by side. OpenRouter is called through its chat completions endpoint, with reasoning off by default (`reasoning: { effort: 'none' }`, the way OpenRouter's documentation gives to turn it off; that each model honours it is unconfirmed), and its default model id is unconfirmed against OpenRouter's list: name the model (style.yaml's `model`, or `RENDER_MODEL`). The same two settings choose an OpenRouter model's reasoning, sent as OpenRouter's `reasoning` object as its documentation describes it: `disabled` `{ effort: 'none' }` (the default); an effort, alone or with `adaptive` or `omit`, `{ effort, exclude: true }` (an effort alone turns reasoning on); `adaptive` alone is OpenRouter's own default effort when reasoning is on, medium, and is sent and named so (`{ effort: 'medium', exclude: true }`, `thinking adaptive, effort medium`); `omit` alone no `reasoning` field, the model's own default. The request's body is not part of a recording's key, so recordings made with the earlier off form (`{ enabled: false }`) replay as they are. `between-tools` is Anthropic's, and `disabled` with an effort asks for reasoning and turns it off at once: both are refused at start. With reasoning on (or left to the model), a request may take up to 4,000 tokens rather than 200; `exclude: true` asks that the reasoning not be sent back, only the line (`message.content`) is read, and a reasoning text that comes back anyway (`message.reasoning`) is never read or kept. Which models honour which setting is OpenRouter's and the model's, unconfirmed per model: a model that always reasons (some open-weight reasoning models) refuses reasoning off with an HTTP 400 ("Reasoning is mandatory for this endpoint and cannot be disabled.", as GPT-OSS-120B answered), and needs an effort (`RENDER_EFFORT=low`) or `omit`. A failed OpenRouter request keeps what OpenRouter passes on of the upstream provider's error (`error.metadata`) in a few fields only: the provider's name, its error type and code, and its own message when it is short (160 characters); never the rest of its payload, which may quote the caller's words, and never a moderation's flagged input. Every error text kept is one line, at most 200 characters, with the key and any long unbroken token (another provider's key) masked. Both are plain `fetch`, the key only in a header. One model call per generated line, started once the core has decided, and the decision model's checks around it (a line from collected content: the screen before, then the wording, then the drift check, in turn); a turn's lines are worded at once, the wording within its budget and the checks within theirs, so a caller waits at most both. A late wording is dropped, and its request is counted at an estimate of its input (it was paid for). The worst case a caller waits on a call is `RENDER_TIMEOUT_VOICE_MS` and `RENDER_CHECK_TIMEOUT_VOICE_MS` together (1.4 s at the defaults), the content screen of a line from collected content inside the checks' budget; a line that runs out is said fixed. On a host of several apps, each app reads its own settings. `pnpm diagnose` says which model words an app's lines (with its key's variable, never the key), the share and the budget, or that every line is said fixed, without asking the model anything.

## 16.8 Measuring it

The point is to find out whether it helps, so it is measured:

- **The call's group.** `RENDER_SHARE` chooses each call's group, generated or fixed, from its id (the same call is always in the same group), recorded on its first trace record and as a `render_group` audit row.
- **The trace.** Each turn that says a line the app generates has a `render` record: each such line's mode, its fixed text, the wording said or why it fell back (and the wording a check refused, for whoever reads the trace), the drift judge's reading where it was asked (`judge`: its `score`, how likely it held the wording to be unfaithful, and the `refuseAt` it was held to; on a line said generated the score it passed with, on a `drift` fallback the score it was refused at; absent where a check failed first, the wording was late or the judge could not be asked; two numbers, never words), how long it took and what it cost. The actions hold the words as said; the decision, the session's last line and what the decision model is told was asked are the fixed lines, so the decision model's requests (and their cassettes) are the same whatever was worded. The trade-off: on the next turn the decision model reads the fixed line as the prompt just played (a summary whole, say) while the caller heard the generated one (its gist); what a caller answers about the line is read against its meaning, which the two share.
- **The console's call detail** shows under each agent line each line the app generates: generated, with the fixed line it stands for beside it, or fixed and why; and, where the judge was asked, its score beside the cutoff ("the judge passed it at 0.12 (cutoff 0.5)", muted, or "refused at 0.62 (cutoff 0.5)" beside a `drift` fallback, [14.9](14-running-it.md#149-looking-at-calls-from-your-phone)).
- **Insights** compares the calls served generated with those served fixed (containment, re-asks a call, the share with a frustrated caller, call length, and the models' cost a call), lists each generated line's fallback rate and latency, and counts what the lines cost, in the cost with the decision model's.
- **The policy card** (`POLICY.md`) lists every line a model words, with its fixed text, its goal, where it is generated, the data it carries word for word and what it may summarize; beside them, every span of a framed line, with its line's fixed text (and its frame for the exact content, `exactText`, where it has one), its goal, where it is generated, the content it is worded from and its word budget; and every prescribed line.
- **A framed line's spans** are recorded one by one: each in the trace's `render` record as an entry of its own (`span`, its exact content as `fixed`, its wording as `said`), in the console under its line by name, and in Insights under its line.

## 16.9 Testing it

No test and no CI run calls a model. The stub regression words an app's generated lines with the fake renderer (`FakeRenderClient`, from `'dialogwright/testing'`): a short acknowledgement before the fixed line where the moment calls for one (none on a line that says `acknowledge: false`), for a line from collected content, each content value cut to its first few words; and for a framed line's span, its content's first clause (to its first comma or stop; a content of one clause comes back whole, and is said unchanged), but the whole content where a later clause has a no, not, never, n't, unless, until, except or only if, so a stub run never shows a read-back that drops a "no" as generated. That word list is the fake renderer's alone, a test double's: no check reads it. The stub regression's judge is a test double too (16.6). Both stand in for a model so the engine's part is tested; on a live call, what a wording means rests on the real judge. It runs every check on every line, the drift check asked of the stub, and prints a line with how many were tried, said generated and fell back, and why: a request's failures grouped by why it failed (`error 36 (anthropic: HTTP 400 invalid_request_error)`), so a failed run says why; and how long a line tried took, its wording and its checks, at the median and the 95th percentile (`latency ms p50 … p95 …`; a replay's wording takes no time, so it is a recording run's figure that compares models; `render:bakeoff` keeps each wording's own time in the cassette and tables it, 16.12). `--render fixed` says every line fixed.

An app's own test of its scripted calls (the scaffold's `src/fixtures.test.ts`) runs them with the same renderer, `stubRenderer()` from `'dialogwright/testing'` (the fake renderer, with `RENDER_SHARE` and the budgets read from the environment as `regress` reads them; null for an app that generates nothing), so a call that says a generated line, or its frame for the exact words, says it the same in the test as in the stub regression, and a scripted call's `expect.text` on such a line passes both or neither.

`--client recorded` replays the render cassette (`fixtures/recorded/render/<model>.jsonl`, of the model style.yaml's `model` names under `RENDER_PROVIDER` and `RENDER_MODEL`, Anthropic's default model with neither; keyed by the act and its line; a run whose `RENDER_THINKING` or `RENDER_EFFORT` changes the model's default, a cassette of its own beside it, such as `claude-haiku-5-5.thinking-adaptive.effort-low.jsonl`) and runs every check again on every recorded line; a line with no recording falls back as `cassette-miss`. `--client record` records real generations with the author's key: the maintainer's step, never a test's. When only the decision model's questions changed, `--client record --render recorded` records the decision model again and replays the generated lines from the render cassette, so no render model is asked and no render key is needed (a line the cassette does not hold falls back as `cassette-miss`).

The adversarial suite (chapter 15) adds a `wording` family for an app that generates lines: an injection where a generated question answers it, the screen catching it (nothing is put to the renderer) or fooled along with the renderer, which echoes the words into its line (never said); collected content that asks a line worded from it to state something as the agent's own (an amount and a day, and a claim with no word an injection screen knows), the screen fooled and the renderer obeying it (never said); a read-back changed by one word in meaning: its decision question turned around (`question`), a negation of the content dropped, the message read back as for another of the people it could be for (`conflict`); and renderers that write instructions, or an amount and a number, before every line (never said, and each call ends as with a well-behaved renderer). For a framed line (16.3), the family's span cases put the same attacks to the span alone: a message that asks to be stated as the agent's own (a refund, a waived fee) with the renderer obeying it, a negation of the message dropped, a message whose last clause says "no" summarized with that clause left out, the message summarized as for the other person it could be for, a renderer that writes words of a line of its own into the span, one that writes the sentence it is shown back whole, the message in its gap and its decision turned, and, spoken to the caller, the message turned toward the caller wrongly (persons swapped, the message's own "you", a "your" for no "my", a "no" about a person); each span falls back to the message word for word, and a property of its own, `frame-kept`, holds that every framed line is said as its frame with each span its wording or its exact content, whatever the renderer returned. A case whose attack changes data or shape is held by those checks (`conflict`, `question`, `foreign`, `words`; the frame written back as `frame`, a line of its own after the span as `format`, both whatever the budget and whatever the judge); one whose attack changes meaning is held by the drift judge, as on a live call: each hostile renderer registers what it wrote with the case's judge (`refusingJudge`, over the run's own), which refuses it as a real judge would. What the suite tests is then the engine's part: a refusal falls back, the frame is kept, nothing is acted on. A run whose renderer names its own judge (`drift`) has that judge tested as it is, and `render:judge-eval` (16.13) measures the real judge on meaning.

## 16.10 Languages

A line is generated in the call's language. In another locale it is generated only where that locale's prompts.yaml gives the line its own `goal`; without one, the locale's fixed line is said. `mode`, `prescribed`, `when` and `from` are read from prompts.yaml, for every locale.

A framed line's span is the same: in another locale it is generated only where that locale's prompts.yaml gives the span its own goal (`generate: { summary: { goal: ... } }`, its goal and nothing else: `from` and `words` are prompts.yaml's); elsewhere the span is its exact content. The locale's text is its own frame, and must say every span prompts.yaml frames (`pnpm check`). Where prompts.yaml gives the line a frame for the exact content (`exactText`), the locale gives its own too, with the same variables as its text; one where prompts.yaml gives none does nothing (`pnpm check` refuses both).

## 16.11 Cost and latency

Each generated line is one call to a small model, and one decision-model question for its drift check (two for a line from collected content and for a framed line's span, whose content is screened again first); a framed line costs one call per span, each a few words. All are counted: the line's tokens and cost in the trace's `render` (a wording the turn stopped waiting for at an estimate of its input), the checks' in the record's own usage, and both in Insights' cost and the console's cost of each turn. Latency is the line's wording and checks, each within its budget; Insights shows it per line. If timeouts dominate a line's fallbacks, raise the budget the trace shows it ran out of, or keep the line fixed: on a call, a wording and two checks in turn rarely fit in well under a second.

## 16.12 Choosing a model

Which model words an app's lines is a measurement on the app's own lines, not a guess: `render:bakeoff` runs the app's corpus and scripted calls once for each model named, that model wording every line the app generates, records each into its own render cassette, and prints one table.

```sh
pnpm render:bakeoff apps/<name> --models anthropic:claude-haiku-5-5,anthropic:claude-sonnet-5-5,openrouter:<vendor/model>
pnpm render:bakeoff apps/<name> --replay --models anthropic:claude-haiku-5-5,anthropic:claude-sonnet-5-5 --by-line
```

In a project of its own it is `npx dialogwright render:bakeoff` (or `npm run render:bakeoff --`), with no folder in the app's own. The folder is any app folder, an engine fixture's among them (`pnpm render:bakeoff packages/dialogwright/src/testing/message --replay --models anthropic:claude-haiku-5-5` tables the message fixture's committed recording); the run happens in the app's package, where app.yaml's `fixtures.dir` is read from, and an app that generates no line is refused (there is nothing to compare).

- `--models`: comma-separated (or repeated), each `anthropic:<model>` or `openrouter:<vendor/model>`. A model's thinking and its effort go after it, either or both, in `RENDER_THINKING`'s and `RENDER_EFFORT`'s words (16.7): `anthropic:claude-haiku-5-5:adaptive:low`, `openrouter:openai/gpt-oss-120b:low` (an OpenRouter model reasoning at low effort), so one run compares the same model thinking and not; without them, the model's own default (the table in 16.7 on Anthropic, reasoning off on OpenRouter), whatever `RENDER_THINKING` says. An OpenRouter id may hold a colon of its own (`vendor/model:free`): only trailing words of that vocabulary are read as its thinking and effort. A thinking the model refuses (16.7) is reported for that model, with why, and nothing is asked of it. Models run one after another. One whose first lines all fail as requests (a wrong id, a key it refuses: 5 tried, every one an `error`) is stopped and reported with why, and the run goes on to the next.
- Recording (the default) asks the models with your own keys, read as the regression reads them (the environment, then `ENV_FILE` or the app's `.env`): `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` or `OPENROUTER_API_KEY` for each model's provider, and the decision model's key (`TYPESAFE_API_KEY`, or `JEV_PROVIDER`'s) for its drift checks. It names every key missing before it asks anything, never prints a key, and refuses in CI: it is the author's step on their own machine, as `kb:draft` is, and no test or CI run records.
- `--replay` tables the cassettes alone, with no key and no request: a model with no cassette shows as not recorded. A comparison recorded once is tabled again for nothing, with `--by-line` or another `--out`.
- Recording asks a model only for the lines its cassette does not have, so a model recorded before is not timed again. `--fresh` asks every line again. The cassette is never moved: the run is recorded beside it, into `<model>.jsonl.fresh`, started empty, and put over the cassette only when it completes with every line answered (no `error` and no `timeout`); otherwise the fresh recording is discarded, the cassette is as it was, and the notes say why (the table is still the fresh run's). On Ctrl-C or a SIGTERM the partial file is deleted before the process exits; one a hard kill leaves behind is said under the table, and the next `--fresh` starts it again. A cassette that is the app's own (one git tracks, or the one the app's `regress --client recorded` replays: style.yaml's `model`, under `RENDER_PROVIDER` and `RENDER_MODEL`) is kept: its fresh run is recorded under the comparison's own name, `<model>.bakeoff.jsonl`, which the bake-off tables for that model from then on. Replacing it takes `--replace-app-cassette`, said in so many words because the app's committed regression replays that cassette and the drift checks of its wordings are in the app's recording: replaced, its new wordings have no checks there, and `regress --client recorded` falls back on them (`drift-error`) until the owner records again (`regress --client record`). After a recording that completes (and, fresh, is kept), the model's checks cassette keeps only the answers this run asked for.
- `--by-line`: each model's lines (by prompt id, a framed line's span by its name), the most fallbacks first, with one wording a check refused for the most common reason (scrubbed of the redacted values as the trace is), or a failed request's reason with the API's own words.
- `--price <model>=<input>,<output>`: a model's price in US dollars per million tokens, for its cost; the default model's is known. `--out <file.md>` writes the table as a Markdown page too; `--json` prints the results as JSON alone. `--screen` is the regression's, for a recording made with the screen separate.

What each column means:

| Column | What it is |
|---|---|
| tried | the lines the app generates that the model was asked to word, over the corpus lines and the scripted calls (a framed line's span counts as one) |
| said generated | of those, the ones whose wording passed every check and would be said |
| fell back | the ones said fixed instead (16.6) |
| why | the fallbacks by reason; `error` with why the requests failed (`error 36 (anthropic: HTTP 400 invalid_request_error)`), and under the table the API's own words for each reason (as the trace keeps them: one line, short, no key); `cassette-miss` when a replay finds a line the model was never asked |
| wording ms p50 / p95 | how long the model took to word a line, at the median and the 95th percentile, each distinct request once: measured only by the bake-off's own recordings (from the request to its answer, on your machine's network), kept in the cassette with the budget it was recorded under (`latencyMs`, `budgetMs`; a regression's recording writes neither), so a replay tables it again; `not measured` for a cassette without them |
| over the call budget | of the wordings measured, how many took longer than the app's own `RENDER_TIMEOUT_MS` for their channel (900 ms on a call by default); a wording that ran past even the bake-off's 30 s and never answered counts as measured and over, at 30 s in the percentiles, and the cell says how many (`10 of 11 (1 past 30 s)`) |
| cost: wording | what the run's wordings cost at the model's price (`no price` without one, with the tokens under the table) |
| cost: checks | what the decision model's checks of those wordings cost (the drift check, and collected content screened again) |

**The 900 ms reality.** While recording, a line's wording and its checks get 30 seconds each, so a slow model is measured rather than cut off: its `said generated` is what it would say given the time. On a call a wording gets `RENDER_TIMEOUT_VOICE_MS` and no more, and every wording counted over the call budget would have been the fixed line (`timeout`), the caller waiting the budget first. A model with many over budget is one a call cannot use at that budget, however well it words: raise the budget (the caller waits longer) or choose a faster model. The checks' time comes on top (`RENDER_CHECK_TIMEOUT_MS`), and is the decision model's, the same for every model. A small model with thinking off is the one that fits. In the maintainer's comparison of the message fixture, Claude Haiku 5.5 with thinking off fell back least of the Claude models, and Sonnet 5.5 (p95 1.8 s) and Haiku with thinking (p95 1.1 s) were over a call's budget ([the design's item 18](../designs/2026-10-13-live-findings.md#as-built-18)); in the first runs of this command, Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite through OpenRouter was the only model both accurate (35 of 36) and inside 700 ms (p95 551 ms), while Haiku 5.5 was accurate but mostly over a call's budget (p50 837 ms on Anthropic), well inside a chat's 1,500 ms ([the design's item 19](../designs/2026-10-13-live-findings.md#as-built-19)).

**The same judge.** Only the renderer changes from model to model. The decision model's answers to every turn are the app's own recording (`regress --client recorded`'s cassette), so each model words the same lines at the same moments, and the drift check is the decision model's, with the same question, for each: a strict judge raises every model's fallbacks alike, and a difference between two rows is the models'. The app's recording is only read: the decision model's answers about one model's wordings (and any turn the app's recording lacks) are recorded beside its render cassette, in `fixtures/recorded/render/checks/<model>/<decision model>.jsonl`, so a comparison never adds to the app's recording and `cassette:trim` of it never loses one. The one exception is the app's own model: the checks of its committed wordings are the app's recording's, which the trim keeps since the regression asks them; a fresh run of it records its own checks beside it, as for any model.

**Then.** A model's render cassette is the one `regress --client recorded` replays with that model named (style.yaml's `model`, or `RENDER_PROVIDER` and `RENDER_MODEL`; and `RENDER_THINKING`). To make a model the app's, name it in style.yaml (`model: { provider: openrouter, id: <vendor/model> }`, 16.4), so every machine's tests replay it and the server words with it, and record the app once (`regress --client record`): the wordings the comparison recorded replay from its cassette, so what is asked is only what it did not ask (the decision model's drift checks, into the app's own recording, and the adversarial suite's lines).

## 16.13 Measuring the judge

The drift judge decides what a generated line means (16.6), so how well it reads is a measurement, not a guess: each faithful summary it refuses costs the caller the summary (they hear their exact words), and each unfaithful one it passes is a wrong message said. `render:judge-eval` puts a set of labelled cases, each a caller's message and a summary of it marked faithful or unfaithful by a person, to three readers, each as a call puts a span to it: the data checks (16.6), the stub regression's judge, and the real drift judge, the decision model asked `renderSpanDrift` with the state a call sends and nothing else, and tables the data checks and the judge together, as a call runs them. A case has no line of its own, so each is heard in a representative sentence, as on a call: spoken to the caller, the secretary demo's read-back (`Thanks! I'll make sure {recipient} gets your message, in short: ____. I have you as {caller}, on {phone}. Shall I pass it on?`, a case's names filled); in the caller's terms, the message fixture's. Measure here before changing the judge's question or `judge.refuseAt`.

```sh
ENV_FILE=apps/<name>/.env pnpm render:judge-eval --out judge.md   # record what the cassette lacks, with the decision model's key
pnpm render:judge-eval --replay                                      # table the cassette alone, no key
```

The engine's cases are `packages/dialogwright/src/render/judgeCases/spans.jsonl` (`--cases <file>` names others: one JSON object a line, `id`, `family`, `expected` faithful or unfaithful, `speaks` as-caller or to-caller, `names` for a to-caller span, `content`, `span`, `note`, and optionally `goal`, `words` and `conflicts`). The judge's answers are kept in `recorded/<decision model>.jsonl` beside the case file, so a recording asks only for the cases it lacks and `--replay` tables them again for nothing. The report gives the everyday messages first (the `realistic` family: how often a good read-back would be said with the data checks and the judge together, the judge alone and the data checks alone, and how many everyday slips each catches), then the hard cases by family (dropped negations, conditions and details, persons swapped, a gender changed, a claim added, a value changed, an instruction followed), each case's verdicts with the judge's probability (it refuses at 0.5), and every unfaithful case the judge passed and every faithful case it refused, with their words. Recording refuses in CI, needs the decision model's key (`TYPESAFE_API_KEY`, or `JEV_PROVIDER`'s), read as the regression reads it, and never prints it; `--json` prints the results as JSON, `--out` writes the report as Markdown.

**What it found.** The maintainer recorded the judge (jev-1.13.0) on the engine's 170 cases on 2026-10-14, when spans were also held to hand-written grammar rules at two levels. Of the 62 good read-backs of everyday messages, 46 (74%) would have been said with the strictest rules and the judge, 55 (89%) with the lighter rules and the judge, and 56 (90%) with the judge alone; the judge alone caught all 10 everyday slips and 46 of the 49 hard unfaithful cases (94%), the rules adding one. That measurement is why the rules were removed (live findings item 25). The judge refused 17 of the 111 faithful cases, six of the eight to-caller read-backs that keep the persons right among them: its question for a to-caller span is where the next measurement starts.

**Since then (live findings items 24, 25 and 27).** The span and its judge are now given the sentence the span is heard in, the report's code column is the data checks alone, and a to-caller span and its judge are given the goal and the context only (the line is spoken to the person who left the message), with no procedure for the words: no "I" said as "you", no whom a he or a she most likely means (item 27, which added ten cases from the secretary demo's third recording, each "you" that changes who does what beside its faithful twin). The maintainer recorded the judge again on all 180 cases on 2026-10-14, after item 27. On the cases of the first measurement it catches the same 56 of 59 unfaithful spans (95%) and refuses 14 of 111 faithful ones (13%); the data checks refuse no faithful span. **A known limit:** on a span spoken to the caller the judge does not follow who "you" is: it passes the five misplaced "you"s of the secretary's third recording (0.15 to 0.42) and refuses faithful turns of person ("he sent me the contract" read back "he sent you the contract", 0.71). The render model's own wording is the protection there, and the message delivered is the caller's own words.

**Names (the maintainer's decision, 2026-10-17).** The data checks once refused a capitalized word a wording was not given as a name, and so refused sentences that open with an ordinary word ("However, ...", "Professional sports are not covered ...", on live calls); in every labelled case where the data checks caught an unfaithful wording the judge refused it too. The name check was removed, and a name a wording was not given is the judge's alone: the families `span-invented-name` (the engine's cases: a summary naming a person the message does not, or the wrong recipient) and `answer-invented-name` (Harbor's answer cases: a payer, a hospital, a clinic or a broker the passage does not name), each beside faithful wordings whose sentences open with an ordinary capitalized word (`span-capital-opener`, `answer-capital-opener`), measure it. On their first recording two invented names in the caller's terms passed the span judge (0.38, "Jordan would like a call back about the invoice" for "please call me back about the invoice"), so its criteria now say that a summary adds no content: a summary naming a person, a company or anything else the caller's words do not is unfaithful. Recorded again with those criteria, the span judge refused all eight invented names (the two it had passed at 0.74 and 0.76) and passed all six ordinary openers; over the 194 span cases it caught 63 of the 72 unfaithful (62 before) and refused 12 of the 122 faithful (14 before), with one fewer role swap caught (11 of 18) and one more everyday read-back refused (58 of 62 said), each a single case near the bar.

**The judge's latency.** A recording times each request it sends the judge (the connection opened first; an answer the cassette already has is not timed) and the report gives its p50, p95 and slowest beside the call's budget for it, `RENDER_CHECK_TIMEOUT_MS` (500 ms by voice, 1000 ms in a chat by default): a judge that answers later on a call leaves the fixed text said, whatever it would have found.

**Another decision model as the judge.** Any decision model that speaks the same API (`POST <base>/v1/systemone`: a state, typed questions, typed answers) can be measured on the same cases, through the decision model's settings (14.12): on a provider the engine knows, its name and the model (`JEV_PROVIDER=openrouter` and `JEV_MODEL=microsoft/microsoft-decision-1`, with `OPENROUTER_API_KEY`); elsewhere `JEV_PROVIDER=custom`, `JEV_BASE_URL` its base (the part before `/v1/systemone`), `JEV_MODEL` its model or deployment name, and `JEV_API_KEY` its key, sent as a bearer token. Its answers are kept in their own `recorded/<model>.jsonl` beside the cases, so the reports compare the two judges case by case, and their latency, on the same 180 cases.

## 16.14 A knowledge answer worded for the caller's question

A knowledge base's answers are approved passages, said word for word ([12](12-the-knowledge-base.md)). Where the app marks a topic `risk: low` and frames its answer line's `{answer}` as a span worded from the answer (`generate: { answer: { from: answer, goal: ... } }`), a model may word the approved passage for the question the caller asked; both switches are needed, and every other answer is said word for word. [12.13](12-the-knowledge-base.md#1213-answers-worded-for-the-callers-question) is the how-to; here is what is generated wording's.

- **Its act** is a span's (16.3) whose content is the passage, under `answer`, with the caller's question beside it (`answer.question`, the words the topic slot was filled from, quoted, screened again before it is sent) and instructions of its own (`ANSWER_INSTRUCTIONS`): the context (an automated agent answering a caller from an approved passage, the passage being everything there is to say) and the goal, never a procedure for the words.
- **Its budget** is the passage's word count and a quarter more (ten more at least) unless the span sets `words` (`answerWordBudget`, the maintainer's decision of 2026-10-16): room for a long passage's answer, and for a faithful wording that takes a few more words than the passage. What it may say is held by the checks and the judge, not by the number; set `words` to ask for something shorter, never longer than 100.
- **Its checks** are 16.6's with the passage as what it was given (`checkAnswer`): no figure the passage does not have (the question is no source of facts; a name, a company or a place the passage does not have is the drift judge's), no question, no four words running of the line's own fixed words, the budget and `avoid`. A wording that is the passage's own words is said as the passage, with nothing more asked (`unchanged`). Its closing stop is kept where the answer ends its line.
- **Its judge** is `renderAnswerDrift` (16.6), at style.yaml's `judge.refuseAt`; its labelled cases are measured with `render:judge-eval` (16.13) from a file of their own (`speaks: answer`, with the passage as `content`, the `question` and the wording as `span`; Harbor's are `apps/harbor/fixtures/judge/answers.jsonl`).
- **Its time.** A long passage takes longer to word, so it is given room: it has `RENDER_ANSWER_TIMEOUT_MS` (16.7) for its wording and checks together. When they are not done within a line's own budgets (the wording's and the checks', 1.4 s on a call by default), the hold line is put before it (`kb_hold`, or the engine's "One moment while I gather that information."), and a channel that can say a line before its turn has ended says it then, with the turn's lines before it: the phone relay (on both carriers, each reply ending on its own `last`) and the chat socket. The answer follows when it is ready, or the passage when it is not ready in time or a check fails. A channel that answers a request with one reply (the harness, the HTTP chat) says the hold line in its place with the rest. The hold line is the call's language's own `kb_hold`, or the engine's words in English; in a language with neither, none is said and the answer has a line's budget only (`pnpm check` warns). Only fixed lines before the answer go early; a server that stops while the answer is worded resumes the call at the question before it, as for any turn in progress (the hold line heard, nothing else of the turn done or saved).
- **The fake renderer** words an answer from the passage's sentences that bear on the question and every sentence with a figure, a name or a limit (a test double; no model is told it), and the stub's judge (`stubAnswerDrift`) refuses a figure, a name or words of its own, or a figure of the passage left out. A scripted call's `renderDelayMs` makes the renderer slow, to hear the hold line in a regression.
- **The adversarial suite** tries three hostile wordings over the scripted calls that word an answer (`wording/answer/figure`, `/turned`, `/promise`): another figure, what is covered turned around, and a promise the passage does not make; none may be said ([15](15-the-adversarial-suite.md)).
