# Journeys: task-scoped instructions for the converse runtime

Status: draft for the maintainer's approval, 2026-11-12. It builds on the converse runtime of
[2026-11-11-agent-runtime.md](2026-11-11-agent-runtime.md) and changes nothing in the scripted engine.

## Why

The converse runtime gives the model everything at once on every turn:
- the whole business: its services, every fact and every question callers ask;
- every action, for every task the line handles.

The wheel shop's agent.yaml holds 16 facts and 17 questions. They serve three different tasks: a repair
request, a dealership booking and a callback note. A caller sending a repair request gets a model that
also reads how dealerships are billed and how many cars a dealer window takes.

The measured runs of 2026-11-12 ([2026-11-prevention.md](eval/2026-11-prevention.md)) point the same way:
- rules added to the prompt moved the kinds of wrong reply without reducing them;
- what halved them was telling the model plainly and briefly what the runtime knows: the lookup's reasons,
  and the line's record of what has been done;
- the wrong replies left are mostly about one task, worded with another task's facts. A callback note was
  said to carry what it does not, and a request was said to be booked when only a dealership booking is.

The maintainer brought the idea from Sierra's agent platform, with its lesson, in the maintainer's words: "the easiest way to
confuse a model is by giving it more information than it needs to do a job." Sierra's **journeys** work like
this:
- each task the agent handles is a journey;
- a journey has a trigger in plain words ("when the caller is asking about booking a service");
- it has its own information, on top of what it inherits from the app;
- it has its own instructions: what to collect, what to confirm, what not to mention;
- it names the integrations it uses;
- the app's root holds the persona, the general information and the guardrails, and answers whatever no
  journey covers, steering back to what the line is for.

Journeys keep the model's context to the task in hand, and give the business a place to formalise a
flow without turning it into a script.

## What it is for

A business describes its line as:
- one **base**: who it is, how it sounds, what every caller may ask about;
- a few **journeys**: the tasks the line carries out.

For each caller turn, the decision model (Jev) says which task the caller is on. The agent model is then
told only what the base and that task need.

**Success looks like this:**
- fewer wrong replies than the prevention runs, with no new kinds;
- a caller who switches task partway ("actually, can someone just call me?") is followed at once;
- a caller who goes back to the first task finds what they already gave still there;
- a side question ("what time do you close?") is answered without leaving the task;
- a question nothing covers gets a short, honest answer and is steered back;
- the first sentence comes as fast as it does today.

## Goals

- Journeys in agent.yaml: a trigger in plain words, a goal, instructions, the journey's own facts and
  questions, and the actions it may use.
- The decision model routes each caller turn. It can carry on with the current journey, switch to
  another, go back to the one set aside, start one, or stay at the root.
- Per turn, the agent model is sent:
  - the base information;
  - the active journey's goal, instructions and information;
  - the active journey's actions only.
- At the root, the model answers from the base information, with the business's own steering
  instructions, and offers what the line can do.
- The runtime refuses any action the turn did not offer, and audits the refusal.
- Traces record the route of every turn, and the console shows it.
- An app with no journeys behaves exactly as today.
- The wheel shop rebuilt with three journeys, measured against the prevention baseline on the same 120
  simulated calls and labelling method, plus new personas that switch task and go off topic.
- A routing evaluation: Jev's reading of the journey for labelled caller turns, recorded into a cassette,
  with accuracy and time added.

## Non-goals (for now)

- Steps or an order inside a journey. A journey is a goal with guidance, never a form.
- Nested journeys, or more than one journey set aside at a time.
- A model, persona or voice of a journey's own.
- Trimming the conversation by journey. The whole conversation is still sent (see "What carries
  across a switch").
- The phone. Journeys are the same on chat and voice; the converse runtime takes calls in plan 2 of the
  runtime's design.
- The scripted engine and the old agent mode: neither changes.
- An admin page for editing journeys.

## Anti-goals (a failure even if it works)

- **A journey becomes a script.** Instructions say what to collect and what to avoid, never the words to
  say or the order to say them in.
- **A switch loses what the caller said.** A caller who comes back to a task is not asked again for
  something they already gave.
- **A caller is trapped in a journey.** A caller who wants something else gets it on that turn, not
  after the journey's questions run out.
- **An action is reachable outside its journeys.** Neither the caller nor the model can talk the line
  into an action the turn did not offer.
- **Routing makes the line slower** in a way a caller notices.
- **An app without journeys changes.**
- **Read-backs and confirmation move into journeys.** Confirmation stays the runtime's job. No journey
  can loosen it, and a journey's instructions never need to mention it.

## Constraints

- Everything in the root CLAUDE.md and the agent runtime's design holds. In particular:
  - every change is read back in the business's words and runs only on the caller's yes;
  - every action goes through the gate and into the audit;
  - the model never sees identity factors, and sees the caller's number by its last four only;
  - lines speak as the business ("we").
- The converse runtime never imports the scripted engine (`src/boundary.test.ts`). Routing uses the Jev
  client the runtime already has (`ConverseDeps.jev`).
- Engine code and fixtures use neutral words (task, request, appointment, callback); wheel-shop words
  stay in the app.
- A model is never called in a test or in CI. The routing evaluation records with the author's own key
  and refuses in CI, as the other evaluations do.
- A saved state from before journeys loads as "no journey active". A journey field that is not in its
  shape loads the same way, as `done` does today.

## The shape

### The base and the journeys in agent.yaml

agent.yaml keeps everything it has today. Its top level is the **base**: the business, persona, lines,
model, sources, services, price caveat, what it does not offer, facts and questions. All of it is
information every journey sees. A new optional `root` section and a new optional `journeys` map sit beside
it.

```yaml
root:
  # How the line answers when no journey is active: the business's own guidance, never a script.
  instructions: >-
    Answer what you can from the business information. For anything else, say plainly that it isn't
    something we can help with, and offer what we can do.
  actions: []          # the actions offered at the root (lookups only; none by default)

journeys:
  repair_request:
    when: The caller wants a wheel repaired, a price, or to bring a car in.
    for: { account: none }           # not offered to a dealership account
    goal: Send the shop a repair request with a drop-off window, read back and confirmed by the caller.
    instructions: >-
      Collect what send_request needs, in whatever order the caller gives it. Check the day they want
      before offering a window. Don't talk about dealership accounts or their rates.
    facts: [...]       # this journey's own facts, on top of the base's
    questions: [...]   # this journey's own questions callers ask, with the business's answers
    actions: [check_availability, send_request]
  dealer_booking:
    when: The caller wants to book cars in for repair.
    for: { account: recognised }     # offered only where the number calling is on an account
    start: { account: recognised }   # and started at the call's start there
    goal: Book the account's cars into a drop-off window, read back and confirmed by the caller.
    instructions: ...
    facts: [...]
    actions: [recognise_caller, check_availability, book_dealer]
  callback:
    when: The caller wants to speak with someone, or wants the shop to call them.
    goal: Leave the shop a callback note, read back and confirmed by the caller.
    instructions: ...
    questions: [...]
    actions: [leave_callback]
```

The fields:
- `when`: the trigger, in plain words. It is what Jev reads, and what the root model is told the line can
  help with.
- `goal`: what the journey achieves, in one sentence.
- `instructions`: what to collect, what to check first, what not to mention. These are goals and limits,
  never wording (the rule for generated wording in the root CLAUDE.md applies here too).
- `facts` and `questions`: the same shapes as the base's. They are added to the base's information for
  this journey only. A journey cannot remove or override a base fact.
- `actions`: the actions it may use, named from the top-level `actions`. Each action is defined once and
  may be named by several journeys.
- `start` (optional): a fact of the call that starts the journey before the caller speaks. In this
  version that means `account: recognised`, so caller ID starts the dealership journey. This works only
  because a recognised account is a fact the runtime already has. It is not an identity.
- `for` (optional): which callers the journey is open to, by a fact of the call. In this version that
  means `account: recognised` or `account: none`. A journey closed to the caller is never one of Jev's
  options, so the dealership journey is not offered to a caller whose number is on no account, and the
  repair request is not offered to a dealership. This is the business's choice, made in its own file. The
  `when` texts then say only what the caller wants, never who they are, since Jev is not told the account.
- `completes` (optional): the change actions whose confirmed run ends the journey. By default these are
  all the change actions it names that are not follow-ons.
- `after` (optional): what the line helps with once the journey is done, in one sentence. By default it is
  "<goal>: done on this call. Answer the caller's questions about it." See "Ending a journey".

`pnpm check` adds these checks:
- every action a journey or the root names exists;
- the root names lookups only;
- no follow-on is named, since follow-ons are never offered to the model;
- every change action is named by at least one journey, so none is unreachable;
- `when` and `goal` are not empty;
- a journey id is a plain identifier.

The JSON schema is regenerated from the zod schema.

**My call:** journeys live in agent.yaml, not a file each. The wheel shop's three are short, and one file
keeps the business readable as a whole. Splitting into files is left until an app needs it.

### Routing: the decision model reads each caller turn

Before the agent model is asked, the runtime asks Jev one typed question about the caller's turn. Jev is
given:
- the caller's words;
- the line's last reply;
- the active journey's `when`, if there is one;
- the paused journey's `when`, if there is one.

The questions:
- **`task`**: a choice. "Which of these does the caller want to do now?" The options are the `when` of each
  journey open to the caller (its `for`), plus "none of these: a question, small talk, or something else".
  The goal Jev is given is what the caller wants done, not what they ask about. A question about another
  task's subject ("what kind of photo do you need?" during a callback) is not a switch, and the labelling
  of the routing turns follows the same rule. The active journey's model, which does not have the answer,
  says so and offers the other task. Questions callers ask whatever their task go in the base.
- **`drops`**: a yes/no, asked only when a journey is active. "Has the caller said they no longer want
  to go on with <active journey's when>?"

The runtime decides from the answers. This is deterministic code, as in the scripted engine:

| Active journey | Jev's `task` | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| none | a journey, above the bar | **start** it |
| none | none of these, or below the bar | **root** |
| A | A, or none of these | **carry on** with A (a side question is answered inside A, from the base information) |
| A | B (not paused), above the bar | **switch**: A is paused, B starts |
| A | the paused journey B, above the bar | **return**: A is paused, B resumes |
| A | `drops` yes, and `task` none of these | A ends, not done; **root** |
| A | `drops` yes, and `task` another journey B | A ends, not done (not paused); B starts or resumes |

The details:
- **The bar.** A switch needs Jev's probability for the new journey above a bar, `routing.switchAt`
  (default 0.6, chosen on the routing evaluation's labelled turns, never by re-recording). Below it, the
  current journey carries on.
- **One paused journey at most.** Switching away from a journey while another is paused drops the older
  one, which is recorded in the trace.
- **A journey that ended done** stays as the turn's context (see "Ending a journey"). If Jev's `task`
  reads that journey again, a second yes/no, `again`, asks: "Does the caller want another <goal>, a new
  one?" Yes starts it fresh, and the record of the call says the first one ran. Otherwise the turn carries
  on in the finished journey's context.
- **Routing's own time limit** is `routing.timeoutMs` (default 400 ms), well under `jevTimeoutMs`. It is
  the most a held first sentence waits for Jev.
- **A read-back waiting for a yes** is answered first, as today. If the answer is not a clear yes, routing
  runs on the same words, so "actually, just have someone call me" both declines the read-back and switches
  to the callback.
- **Jev fails or times out** (`routing.timeoutMs`): the turn carries on with the active journey, or stays at
  the root. Routing never fails a turn.

**My call:** Jev routes, not the agent model. The maintainer suggested Jev for it. It is fast and typed, and its
reading is a judgement the runtime can record and evaluate. If the agent model routed itself (for
example by calling a `start_task` tool), every journey's trigger would be in its context, and every switch
would cost an extra model round.

### What the agent model is sent

The system prompt keeps its order: persona, information, the five rules, honesty, now, the caller, the
record of the call. The information part changes:
- **In a journey:**
  - the base information;
  - then "What you are helping the caller with now: <goal>";
  - then its instructions and its own facts and questions.
  
  The tools are the journey's actions and `end_call`.
- **At the root:**
  - the base information;
  - the root's instructions;
  - "What this line can help with:", followed by each journey's `when`, so the model can offer them. It
    is never given their facts or actions.
  
  The tools are the root's actions, if it names any, and `end_call`.
- **A paused journey** is named in one line: "Set aside earlier on this call: <when>; offer to pick it up
  once this is done." Its information is not sent.
- **The record of the call** (`callSoFar`) stays whole across journeys. What has been done on the call is
  true whatever the task, and it is what stops "your request is in" being said wrongly.

The base information and the five rules come first and do not change between journeys. The prompt's
stable prefix stays cacheable where a provider caches.

### What carries across a switch

**The conversation is sent whole.** What the caller gave for a paused journey is in the conversation, so
a resumed journey picks it up and does not ask again. What is confined is the business information and
the actions, which is where the measured confusion came from. The wheel shop's calls are a few minutes
long, so the whole conversation is small.

**My call:** whole for this version. A journey's view of the conversation (only its own turns, with a
summary of the rest) is a later step, if the measured runs show the earlier conversation misleading a
journey.

### The gate and offered actions

- The model is only given the turn's actions as tools.
- A call to any other action, whether a model's slip or prompted by a caller, is refused by the runtime
  before the gate, with the reason `not-offered`. The refusal is audited as a `converse_refused` row
  naming the active journey, and the model is told the action is not available for this task. It does
  not count toward the action's refusals, since nothing about the action itself was wrong.
- Follow-ons still run after their action's confirmed yes, whatever journey is active.

**My call:** this is a runtime check, not a new policy rule. Which actions a task offers is the
business's design of the line, held by the runtime as it holds a slot to its lookup. The gate's rules
stay what the business decides about each action.

### Ending a journey

- **Done:** an action in its `completes` runs on the caller's yes. The journey is finished, but its
  information stays as the turn's context until another journey starts or the call ends. The questions
  that follow a request ("is my drop-off booked?", "when do I send the photo?") are where the remaining
  wrong replies were, and the facts that answer them are the finished journey's. In that context the model
  is sent:
  - the base information and the journey's own facts and questions;
  - its `after` line in place of its goal and instructions;
  - its lookups, but none of its change actions.
  
  A paused journey, if any, is offered as above. A new request on the same call goes through `again`
  (see "Routing").
- **Dropped:** Jev reads `drops` yes with no other task.
- **The call ends:** the journey ends with it.

### State, trace and console

- **State.** `ConverseState.journey` holds:
  - `{ active: string | null, paused: string | null }`;
  - the journeys done on the call, by id.
  
  It is loaded tolerantly, as `done` is, and needs no session schema bump.
- **Trace.** Each turn's record gains `route`:
  - the journey before and after the turn;
  - the outcome (start, carry, switch, return, root, ended-done, dropped);
  - Jev's probabilities for each option and for `drops`;
  - Jev's time.
- **Console.** The call view shows the active journey beside each turn, and marks each switch.

### Policy pages

- **POLICY.md** lists, for each action, the journeys that offer it, and the root's actions.
- **APP-MAP.md** gains a diagram: root → journeys (each with its `when`) → their actions → follow-ons.
- After the change, run the three read-back commands. Read each diff as a change in what the agent may
  do.

## Measuring it

The measured runs of 2026-11-12 are in [2026-11-journeys.md](eval/2026-11-journeys.md): worse than the baseline on the raw
rate of wrong replies, and a wrong first route traps a caller in the wrong task.

**The baseline** is the prevention runs on Ember:
- 120 calls: 12 personas, 10 runs each;
- labelled by the same brief and method;
- 14 wrong replies, 10 at severity 2 or more, 17 gaps.

The calls, their labels and the labelling brief are kept outside the repository with the runs, in the
maintainer's evaluations folder (`2026-11-12-status`: the Ember runs `ember-g` and `ember-h`,
`labels-ember.jsonl` and the brief), as the prevention page says. Steps 6 and 7 work from there, and the
results page publishes only the figures.

**Three new personas** are added. Their calls count apart from the baseline set:
- `switches-to-callback`: starts a request, asks for a callback halfway, then comes back to finish the
  request;
- `side-question`: asks the hours and the parking partway through a request;
- `wanders-off`: asks about tyres, the weather and a car wash, then wants a price.

**The routing evaluation.** Each caller turn of a sample of the calls is labelled with the journey it
belongs to. Then:
- `pnpm converse:route-eval --cases <file> --labels <file> [--replay]` scores Jev's reading;
- it reports accuracy, wrong switches, missed switches, accuracy by bar, and Jev's time (p50 and p95);
- the decision model is recorded into a cassette beside the labels, with the author's key, and refuses in
  CI;
- `--replay` runs from the cassette, with no key.

**Noise.** The prevention page found a difference of a few wrong replies in 120 calls to be noise. The
journeys runs are therefore 240 calls (20 per persona), compared with the baseline as rates per 100
calls. The labelling uses the same brief, by the same kind of labeller.

**The bars (my call, for the maintainer to check; the first is "no worse" by the maintainer's decision under "How it lands"):**
- wrong replies per 100 calls fall below the baseline's 11.7 (14 in 120), with severity 2+ no higher than
  8.3 (10 in 120);
- no new kind of wrong reply;
- routing: at least 95% of labelled turns right, and wrong switches under 2%;
- the first sentence's median is no more than 100 ms slower than the prevention runs (0.75 s);
- every new persona's switch is followed on the turn it is asked for, and nothing given is asked again.

**Keeping latency flat.** Routing runs alongside the active journey's model request (or the root's, with
no journey). In the common case, carry on, nothing waits.

The reply's first sentence is held until Jev answers. Jev's reading of a short turn usually comes back
well before the model's first sentence. On a switch or a start, the request is cancelled before anything
is said and asked again with the new journey. That costs the one model round already begun. The trace
counts these.

## Amendment (2026-11-13): guide the caller, don't guess

Status: draft for the maintainer's approval.

### Why

The measured runs ([2026-11-journeys.md](eval/2026-11-journeys.md)) found a caller trapped in the wrong task by a wrong
first route. A simulated caller opened with only a phone number and a day ("555 555 0112. Thursday afternoon"). The
decision model read that as wanting a callback, and the request never happened.

The maintainer's direction, in their words:
- "Our goal can't be perfect results from confusing input, we need to detect confusing input and provide guidance."
- "If you called a person and said '[a number], Thursday' a real person wouldn't know what to do with that either."

A good receptionist does three things:
- opens by saying what the business does;
- when a caller says something that fits nothing, tells them what they can help with;
- when the caller still misses, tells them exactly what to say.

Inside a task, a receptionist who gets a life story in place of a day says so: "I just need the day you want to bring
it in."

### What changes

**1. A greeting that says what the line does.**
- The wheel shop's `lines.greeting` becomes a receptionist's opening, in the business's words. For example: "Thanks
  for calling Example Wheel Works, where we make damaged wheels look new again. We can price a repair and set up a
  drop-off, or have someone from the shop call you back. How can we help?"
- A journey started at the call's start (`start`) may have a `greeting` of its own. The dealership journey would
  greet a recognised account with a booking question.
- Both are fixed lines, said word for word.

**2. Unclear input gets guidance, wherever it comes.**

One mechanism serves the root and every task. The decision model reads each caller turn:
- At the root, the `task` question's options become each open journey, "a question about the business" (hours,
  prices, where we are, what we fix), and "none of these" (unclear, unrelated, or something the line does not do).
- Inside a task, a `fits` reading asks whether the turn answers the line's last question or carries on with the
  task.

A turn is **unclear** when, at the root, it is none of these (or a journey below the start bar), or, inside a task,
it does not fit and is no switch and no question about the business.

On an unclear turn:
- The model is told the turn was unclear, with the caller's words. Its goal: give the caller direction for the
  input it needs at that point, never a guess at what they meant.
  - At the root, that means what the line can help with, from the open journeys' triggers.
  - Inside a task, it means what it needs from them now ("I just need the day you'd like to drop it off").
- On a second unclear turn in a row, the goal adds: say exactly what to say ("You can say 'repair' to price a
  repair, or 'call me back'"; "Just the day, like 'Tuesday'").
- On that turn the model is offered no action that changes anything, so confusing input is never acted on.
  Lookups stay.
- On the third unclear turn in a row, the runtime says the business's fixed line for it (`lines.unclear`, for
  example "Sorry we couldn't sort that out today. Call us back any time.") and ends the call.
- The count starts again on any turn that is not unclear.

The guidance is the model's own words, held to the same rules as every reply. The maintainer chose this over a fixed
menu line for each state (2026-11-13): a fixed line would be needed for every state a caller can go astray in, and
the model already knows what that state needs.

**3. A stricter start.**
- A journey starts from the root only at or above `routing.startAt`.
- `routing.switchAt` keeps its meaning for moving between journeys.
- startAt's default comes from the routing evaluation's labelled turns. My guess is 0.8.

**4. A way out of a task.**
- Inside a journey, the model is also sent the `when` of the other journeys open to the caller ("Other things this
  line can help with").
- Its goal: when the caller wants one of those, say so and ask whether to switch, never fit it into this task's
  action.
- The caller's answer is routed with that reply as the line's last reply, so "yes, let's do that" moves them.

**5. The wheel shop's lines** are in the business's words, each marked as an assumption for the owner:
- the greeting;
- the dealership greeting;
- `lines.unclear`.

### What is measured

- **The routing evaluation's labels** gain the split between "a question about the business" and "none of these",
  and a `fits` label inside tasks. The 59 turns labelled none are labelled again.
- **The same 300 simulated calls are run again**, labelled by the same brief and compared with the baseline and the
  first journeys run.
- **One rule of the evaluation changes.** A persona whose opening a receptionist couldn't act on (the number-and-day
  caller) is held to being guided to the task and then completing it, not to being understood at once.
- **The bars stay as they are:** no worse than the baseline on wrong replies, plus requests done at least as often
  as the baseline (80%).

### Engine and app

**In the engine** (neutral, for any app):
- the `question` and `none` options, and the `fits` reading;
- the unclear count and the guidance goals;
- withholding change actions on an unclear turn;
- `lines.unclear` and the end after the third unclear turn;
- `routing.startAt`;
- the other journeys' triggers sent inside a journey;
- a journey's `greeting`.

All of it applies only to an app with journeys. An app without them is unchanged.

**In the wheel shop:** the lines in item 5.
