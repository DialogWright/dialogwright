# Texting: a sender and a webhook for any app, consent through the gate, and a photo sent back

Drafted and built on 2026-11-08 from the maintainer's decisions below. The engine gains texts (SMS and MMS) as a capability, neutral to any app; the wheelshop demo uses it to ask a caller for a photo of the damage once their request is confirmed, and to send that photo on to the shop.

## The maintainer's decisions (2026-11-08)

1. **The wheelshop texts from its own Twilio number.** A test text from that number was delivered, so the number is registered and can send.
2. **The caller gives consent at the request.** A required yes or no, asked after the callback number ("Is it OK if we text you at that number?"), read back with the request.
3. **After the request is confirmed, the caller gets a text:** reply with a photo of the damage.
4. **Their reply's photo is forwarded to the shop's phone** with the request's summary.
5. **Reply with a photo, not an upload link.** No page to host, nothing for the caller to open: they answer the text the way they answer any text.

## The shape

### The engine: a capability for any app

**Sending.** The server gives app code a sender: `ctx.texts?.send({ to, body, media? })` on the tool context (and the incoming hook's; since call 46 not on any other hook's, which texts through a gated tool), absent where no sender is configured. The provider is Twilio's Messages API by fetch (no SDK), with `TWILIO_ACCOUNT_SID` and `TWILIO_AUTH_TOKEN`; the number texts are sent from is `TEXT_FROM`, which under a host an app's own settings file may give its own (a business's own number). `TEXT_FROM` unset is no sender, the default: no text is sent, as before. A send never throws: a failure is a receipt with `status: failed` and the provider's code and message (numbers masked). A text sent with a key (`key: ctx.idempotencyKey`) is not sent twice on the server; the first receipt comes back, marked `repeat`.

Each text is recorded in the audit (`text_sent`: the number by its last four, the body by its length, the media by their count, the provider's message id and status) and, on a call or chat, in the turn's trace (`texts`). The words are never recorded: they are the app's fixed lines, and the caller's own number is the one thing a text carries that the records mask.

The provider is one adapter, `TextProvider` (`texts/types.ts`): `{ id, send({ to, from, body, media }) }`. A Telnyx sender is a second adapter of the same shape, chosen by a setting when it is added; nothing an app writes changes.

**Receiving.** `POST /messages/twilio`, Twilio's signature checked exactly as the voice webhook's is (the same HMAC over the public URL and the form), refused (403) when invalid. Its fields (`From`, `To`, `Body`, `MessageSid`, `NumMedia`, `MediaUrlN`, `MediaContentTypeN`) become an `IncomingText`; the number texted (`To`) picks the app, as a call's number picks it under a host. Twilio is answered at once with an empty `<Response/>` (no auto-reply from the engine), the message is recorded (`text_received`, under its message id on the `sms` channel: the sender by their last four, the media by their count), and it is handed to the app's new optional hook, `AppCode.onText(message, ctx)`, whose context has the sender (`ctx.texts`), the app's settings (`ctx.env`) and its systems (`ctx.sys`). The engine downloads no media: the URLs are Twilio's, and forwarding passes them as `MediaUrl` on the outgoing message, which Twilio fetches.

**Consent through the gate.** A form's `then` entry in business.yaml may carry a condition: `{ tool: send_photo_link, when: { field: textOk, is: "yes", reason: no-consent } }`. The business compiler writes it as a `oneOf` rule on the tool's param, after the read-back's `confirmed`, so with a no the gate refuses the text, the refusal is audited under its reason, and the form goes on without it (the write made, the done line said). `pnpm check` holds the condition to a choice field of the same form and one of its options, and refuses it on the form's write (its first tool, which a yes always makes). The policy card lists each such action under "Done only on the caller's answer", with the field, the option and the reason.

**A choice said one way in each line.** A choice field's `says` may give an option `{ readBack, done }`, so the consent reads back as "and we'll text you at {callbackNumber}" and the done line says "Reply to our text with a photo ...", or, for a no, "and we'll call you at" and "We'll call you ...".

**The business's text lines.** business.yaml gains `texts:`, the texts the app's own code sends, by name, in fixed words with the placeholders its code fills: never a generated line, never said on a call. And `owner.number` is optional, since a real number belongs in the app's settings.

### The wheelshop

- The request gains a required `textOk` (yes or no), asked after the callback number, read back by its `says`; the price line and the done lines say "reply to our text with a photo", still promising nothing before the read-back's yes (the agent-mode design's decision 25).
- `send_photo_link` is a real text from business.yaml's `texts.photoRequest`, held to the consent by the gate, idempotent on `ctx.idempotencyKey`, and printed where there is no sender.
- `notify_owner_request` and `notify_owner_booking` text the summary to `SHOP_PHONE`, the app's own setting, printed where it is unset or there is no sender. A text to the business is not a text to the caller, so no caller's consent applies.
- `onText`: a text from a number with a recent request (the app's systems keep each request by its callback number, in memory: the demo's, said so in DESIGN.md) is forwarded to `SHOP_PHONE` with the photos ("Photo from {name}, ...{last4}, for their {vehicle} request."), and the caller gets the fixed thank-you ("Got it, thanks. We'll text you a price soon."); a text from an unknown number is forwarded as "Text from ...{last4} (no request on file)"; a text with no photo is forwarded too.

## Call 46: the review (2026-11-08, decided by the maintainer)

1. **Routing under a host.** A text goes to the app whose `TEXT_FROM` it was sent to, else the app host.yaml's `numbers` list the number for. A text to any other number is recorded in the server's own audit (`_server`, `text_received` with `unrouted`) and handed to no app: unlike a call, it does not go to the default app. A server of one hands every text to its app.
2. **Abuse limits** (new settings with defaults; texts are new, so nothing changes for an app that sent none): the sender refuses a text over `TEXT_LIMIT_PER_NUMBER` texts to one number in 24 hours (default 3) or `TEXT_LIMIT_PER_HOUR` texts in all in an hour (default 20), each per app. A refused send is recorded (`text_sent`, status `limited`) and returns as a failure, which the tool reports; it is never retried, and its key is not kept. The guide also tells operators to restrict Twilio's messaging geographic permissions to the countries they serve and to turn on Twilio's SMS pumping protection. The wheelshop keeps texting on a chat too (a chat caller gives the number), under the same limits.
   - **Built beside it, for the maintainer to check:** `TEXT_LIMIT_EXEMPT`, the business's own phones (E.164, comma-separated), which the per-number limit does not hold; they still count toward the hour's. Without it, the wheelshop's fourth request of a day could not reach the shop's phone (each request, booking, callback note and photo is a text to it). The wheelshop's `.env.example` says to put `SHOP_PHONE` there.
3. **No sender on the app context.** `texts` is on the tool context and the incoming hook's context only; any other hook texts through `ctx.callTool` of a tool, so the gate decides.
4. **A message sent again** (the same `MessageSid`; the latest thousand kept per app) gets the empty `<Response/>` and is not handed to `onText` again.
5. **Send errors scrubbed:** each `http(s)://...` becomes `[url]`, and each run of digits with spaces, dots, dashes or brackets between them that holds seven digits or more becomes `[number]`.
6. **The wheelshop's thank-you** goes only to a caller whose request said yes to texts: the request on file keeps its `textOk`.
7. **`pnpm check` refuses `when` on an optional field** (the tool would be refused for an answer the caller was never asked), and the schema refuses a `when.reason` the gate gives of its own (`confirmation`, `identity`, `scope`, `value-missing`, ...).
8. **The webhook is mounted only where some app has `onText` or a sender** (404 otherwise), and only media at `https://api.twilio.com/` are kept; any other URL is dropped, never handed on, and the drop counted in the record (`mediaDropped`).
9. Smaller: the wheelshop's `photoRequest` ends "Reply STOP to opt out." (a demo assumption the owner may change; Twilio answers STOP itself); a text forwarded to the shop is cut to 1,500 characters; the done line is unchanged.

## Settings

| Setting | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `TEXT_FROM` | unset: no sender | The number texts are sent from (E.164); on a host, an app's own settings file may give its own. |
| `TWILIO_ACCOUNT_SID` | unset | The Twilio account texts are sent through; needed with `TEXT_FROM`. |
| `TWILIO_AUTH_TOKEN` | (the voice webhook's) | Sends the texts, and checks the incoming webhook's signature. |
| `TEXT_LIMIT_PER_NUMBER` | `3` | Texts to one number in 24 hours, per app (call 46). |
| `TEXT_LIMIT_PER_HOUR` | `20` | Texts in all in an hour, per app (call 46). |
| `TEXT_LIMIT_EXEMPT` | none | The business's own phones, which the per-number limit does not hold (call 46). |
| `SHOP_PHONE` (the wheelshop's own) | unset: the shop's summaries and forwards are printed | The shop's phone the summaries and photos go to. |

The Twilio console's number takes `https://<PUBLIC_HOST>/messages/twilio` as its "A message comes in" webhook (HTTP POST).

## Calls made for the maintainer to check

1. **The incoming hook runs after Twilio is answered,** so a slow forward never holds Twilio's webhook (its timeout is 15 s); a hook that throws is logged, and what it sent before it threw is still recorded.
2. **Twilio's media URLs are passed as they come.** Where the account enforces HTTP basic auth on media URLs, Twilio cannot fetch them for the forward; the default does not, and the engine never fetches them itself.
3. **The keys of texts sent, the limits' counts and the message ids seen are kept in memory** (the latest 2000 keys and 1000 ids per app): a server restarted between a send and its repeat sends again, and counts its limits afresh. The wheelshop's stand-in systems keep theirs in memory too.
4. **The recent request a text is matched to** is the wheelshop's, kept in its systems in memory for seven days; a restart forgets it, and the photo is then forwarded as from an unknown number, which still reaches the shop.
5. **The thank-you goes only with a photo** from a number with a recent request that said yes to texts (call 46): a text with no photo is forwarded, and the caller gets no reply, so a word like STOP is never answered with "We'll text you a price soon" (Twilio answers STOP and HELP itself).

## Call 52: quote and confirm (2026-11-08, decided by the maintainer)

The wheelshop's retail request becomes a booking only once the customer accepts the shop's quote ([its DESIGN.md](../../apps/wheelshop/DESIGN.md#quote-and-confirm); the agent-mode design's call 52). For texting, it adds two engine capabilities, each neutral to any app:

1. **`TextContext.decide(questions, about?)`**: the incoming hook asks the decision model typed questions about the text (a yes or no, a choice, a score), of the same client the app's calls ask (`JEV_CLIENT`; a stub or a recording in a test, never a model), within `JEV_TIMEOUT_MS`, the model sent `{ message: { words, media }, about }`. It returns the answers, or null where the model gave none. Each asking is recorded in the app's audit beside the message (`text_decision`: the questions by id, each answer, the client's source and model, the time it took; never the words, only the names of `about`). A text has no trace, so the audit row is its record. So a reply is understood as a turn is, never by matching its words: the wheelshop's CONFIRM is read by the model ("confirm", "yes confirm", "sounds good, book it" count). The heuristic stub reads a text's words as a turn's. `texts/decide.ts`; tests `texts/decide.test.ts`, `server/messages.e2e.test.ts`.
2. **`AppPageDeps.texts`**: an app's own pages are given the app's sender where the server has one (its provider, number, keys and abuse limits, as a tool's), each text recorded in the app's audit (`text_sent` on the `sms` channel, under the text's key, or `page`). This widens call 46's "no sender on the app context" for pages alone, which a person works behind the console's sign-in: nothing a page does goes through the gate, so whether a caller may be texted is the page's code to decide, as in the incoming hook (the wheelshop texts a quote only to a request whose caller said yes to texts). Test: `server/appPages.e2e.test.ts`.

The wheelshop texts a customer up to four times in a day (the photo request, the thank-you, the quote, the booking), over the default `TEXT_LIMIT_PER_NUMBER` of 3: its `.env.example` suggests 5, and `SHOP_PHONE` stays in `TEXT_LIMIT_EXEMPT`.

## Call 53: the review of quote and confirm (2026-11-08)

**(2026-11-08, the review of quote and confirm) Decided by the maintainer** ([the wheelshop's DESIGN.md](../../apps/wheelshop/DESIGN.md#quote-and-confirm)):
- **A superseded quote is never booked, nor a request twice.** After the decision model answers, the reply is read against the quote as it then stands, on the clock of that moment: a quote no longer its request's latest, superseded, withdrawn or booked sends the reply to the shop (`texts.quoteReply`). `bookQuote` books only a waiting quote, its request's latest, whose request has no booking, inside its hold.
- **A lapsed quote never books.** A reply to one is not read by the model and goes to the shop to call (`texts.quoteLate`); the customer gets no text. Only a waiting quote is read and booked.
- **Receipts are checked.** Where the customer's booked text does not go (`limited`, `failed`, `refused`, `printed`), the shop's `texts.quoteBooked` adds `texts.customerNotTexted` ("Customer not texted (limited): call them."); a quote whose text did not go says so on its card, with why and the number to call.
- **Numbers are matched exactly.** A reply, and a photo's request on file, only from a `+1` number that is the request's (`sameNumber`, now exported, against `+1` and its ten digits); the booked text and the thank-you go to the number that consented, never the number the text came from.
- **`acceptsQuote` is the goal only** (does the reply accept the quote as offered and ask to book it?), the quote and the reply word as context, no instruction to match a word; a reply books only with `acceptsQuote` at 0.7 or more, above `asksChange`, and `asksChange` under 0.5.
- **`ctx.decide` is costed.** The `text_decision` row adds `inputTokens`, `outputTokens` and `estimated`. The engine puts no limit on `decide` per text that comes in, and guide 13.17 says an app asking on every text lets anyone run up model calls; the wheelshop asks only for a number with a waiting quote.
- **Page texts.** Guide 13.6 says every app page is given `AppPageDeps.texts`, public ones too, that a public page that texts is an SMS-pumping risk, and that a text's `key` is the audit's id, so it carries no personal data. The wheelshop's page texts through an allow-list (`allowListTexts`): the shop's phone, or a request's callback number that said yes to texts; any other refused and logged.
- **The forms check Origin and Sec-Fetch-Site** beside their token: the console's sign-in's check, now the engine's `formFromOwnPage` (exported; the console uses it too).
- **Withdraw.** A waiting quote's Withdraw releases the hold and shows the card "Withdrawn"; the form asks whether to text the customer (`texts.quoteWithdrawn`), unticked by default (the shop calls if it wants).
- **The texts in a day:** four (the photo request, the thank-you, the quote, the booking), one more for each new quote and for a withdrawal the shop texts. The engine's `TEXT_LIMIT_PER_NUMBER` default stays 3; the wheelshop's `.env.example` suggests 5, and the app notes at start when the limit is under 5. The lapse is still written before the shop is texted.
- Smaller: the booking uses the clock after the model's answer; a text's reference is taken as it is sent (`Systems.nextRef`), so two texts in flight never share one.
