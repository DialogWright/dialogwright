# The cost of a call in each mode (2026-11-25)

> Plan 3, Task 7 of [the agent runtime](../2026-11-25-agent-runtime-plan-3.md), the spec's "Cost comparison" ([2026-11-11-agent-runtime.md](../2026-11-11-agent-runtime.md)). The models' per-call figures are measured from traces and recorded runs. The carriers' figures are measured from their own billing records, read on 2026-11-26 ([What the carriers billed](#what-the-carriers-billed-2026-11-26)); they replace the estimates of 2026-11-25, which priced a Telnyx path "from parts" that never applied: DialogWright reaches both carriers only through their conversation relay (Twilio's ConversationRelay, Telnyx's `<ConversationRelay>` in TeXML). Sharing this page is the maintainer's decision.

## The answer

**The model's share of a call's cost is small in both modes, so the difference between them is a few percent of a call's total at any volume.**

- **Per call, models only:** agent mode about $0.0084 at most (the agent model $0.0063 measured, plus the decision model's routing requests at an upper bound of $0.0021); scripted mode about $0.002 (measured on one call).
- **Per call, everything else (the carrier, with speech recognition and the voice in its conversation relay), as billed:** on Telnyx a median of $0.19 a call (12 calls, $0.15 to $0.24); on Twilio $0.236 a call (its 9 calls' median length, billed as 3 minutes). The same for both modes.
- **The difference** is about $0.006 a call: 2.6% of agent mode's total a call on Twilio ($0.236) and 3.2% on Telnyx ($0.19); 2.5% to 4.0% across the Telnyx calls' range. At 2,000 calls a month it is about $12.
- **Where it starts to matter:** it takes about 16,000 calls a month for the difference to reach $100 a month, and about 160,000 for $1,000. Below tens of thousands of calls a month, cost is not a reason to choose either mode.
- **The carrier is the larger saving:** Telnyx with Krisp is billed $0.060 a minute against Twilio's $0.0785, 23.6% less. On a call of three and a half minutes that is about $0.065, about ten times the difference in model cost between the modes. (The calls billed differ in length: the medians, $0.19 against $0.236, differ by $0.046, about seven times.)
- **One billed minute of carrier time costs more than the whole difference:** a minute is $0.0785 on Twilio and $0.060 on Telnyx, 12.5 and 9.5 times the difference in model cost per call. A mode whose calls run a minute longer costs more than a mode whose model costs more.

### What it means (the maintainer's reading, 2026-11-26)

Both models are cheap and do their jobs well: the decision model for scripted mode's typed questions and agent mode's routing, the agent model for agent mode's conversation. The agent model costs more, but that is a rounding error beside the call's carrier, recognition and speech. So the mode and the model for a line are chosen by what the job needs, not by their price. The larger saving is in the carrier path: Telnyx, with the work that lets a caller talk over its lines, costs $0.10 to $0.20 a call against Twilio's $0.26, many times more than the difference between the modes could ever save.

(The reading is kept as written. Its per-call figures were the estimates of 2026-11-25; the carriers' bills, below, put Telnyx at a median of $0.19 a call and Twilio at $0.236, $0.060 a minute against $0.0785. The reading still holds: the carrier's saving is about ten times the difference between the modes.)

**Twilio,** from its usage records (the account's October and November): ConversationRelay $0.07 a minute and the inbound call on a local number $0.0085 a minute, both billed in whole minutes, so **$0.0785 a minute**: the list price this page used before, now confirmed by the bill. **How a request is counted.**
- **Agent mode:** the agent model is one request per model round, as the trace's `usage` holds it. The decision model is asked once per caller turn: a routing reading, or on the turn that answers a read-back, the reading of the yes. - **Scripted mode:** the decision model gets one request a turn, with all the turn's questions in it (25 on average on Harbor). The injection screen is one of the questions, so it adds no request.

## Shared costs, per minute

The carrier, speech recognition and speech are the same for both modes, since a call's minutes and the words said are the carrier's whatever decides what to say. The figures are the carriers' bills ([above](#what-the-carriers-billed-2026-11-26)).

**Superseded: the list prices of 2026-11-25.** This section first priced three Telnyx paths from list prices: "from parts" (the Voice API with a recognizer and speech billed apart, $0.030 to $0.040 a minute, $0.099 or $0.135 a call), and Telnyx's Conversation Relay as an add-on ($0.055 a minute, $0.196 a call). The "from parts" paths never applied: DialogWright reaches Telnyx only through its conversation relay, which the bill shows includes recognition and the voice. The add-on's list price, $0.05 a minute with call control and the SIP leg, is what Telnyx billed, plus Krisp. Twilio's $0.0785 a minute was right, and its question (whether ConversationRelay includes recognition and speech) is answered by the bill: it does, with no separate charge.

- **Which figures are measured and which are list prices.** Measured: the agent model's tokens and cost, the decision-model request counts, the call lengths and the characters spoken, the scripted call's decision-model cost, and what each carrier billed. Prices: the agent model's and the decision model's, each a list price. Not measured: the decision model's cost in agent mode (an upper bound) and anything about calls longer or shorter than the sample.
- **The difference is the same dollars on both carriers,** since the shared cost is the same for both modes; it is a larger share where the shared cost is smaller.
- **It is a share, not a threshold.** The difference grows in step with calls, so no volume turns it from small to large. It reaches $100 a month at about 15,900 calls and $1,000 at about 159,000. Where it matters is a business that runs a very large volume, or one whose margin is in cents a call.
- **The carrier matters more than the mode.** At the same length, a call on Telnyx with Krisp costs $0.0185 a minute less than on Twilio: about $0.065 on a call of three and a half minutes, about ten times the difference between the modes.
- **A mode's own call length matters more too.** One more billed minute is $0.0785 on Twilio and about $0.060 on Telnyx, 12.5 and 9.5 times the difference in model cost. The one scripted phone call ran 2 min 31 s (3 billed minutes), about as long as the wheel shop's Twilio calls; the sample is one call, so this shows how much length counts, not that either mode's calls run shorter.

## Limits

- **Sample sizes.** Agent mode: 17 live calls, all placed by the maintainer to test the line, and 120 simulated ones. Scripted mode: one phone call with substance and three chat sessions, so its per-call figures are one observation; the per-request figures rest on 452 and 429 recorded requests, which are not calls. The second voice run was not in, so no figure here comes from it.
- **The bills' samples.** Telnyx's per-call figures are the wheel shop's 12 calls in the week Telnyx keeps its detail records; Twilio's per-call figure is the wheel shop's 9 calls' median. These are not the same calls as the 17 traced above (other windows), and all were the maintainer's test calls. Telnyx's per call includes Krisp, the recommended setting.
- **Demo call shapes.** The wheel shop's calls are a booking of about eleven turns; Harbor's one live call asked about coverage and reported a claim. A real shop's calls (more or fewer turns, longer replies, a caller who calls back) cost differently, and call length drives the shared cost much more than it drives the model's.
- **One account's rates.** The carriers' figures are what one pay-as-you-go account was billed for US calls in November 2026. Providers publish volume tiers and committed rates; Telnyx says its rates vary by destination and volume tier, and Twilio's volume discounts apply to the carrier part. The model prices are list prices and change; the agent model's is read from its page into `price.ts` with a date.
- **Recognition and speech.** Settled by the bills: on both carriers the conversation relay's price includes them, with no separate charge (this page first left it open, from the providers' pages alone).
- **The decision model in agent mode** has no token count in its trace, so its cost is an upper bound from scripted mode's per-request tokens. It is under a quarter of agent mode's model cost, so the bound does not change the picture.
- **Not counted:** hosting and storage, the number's monthly rental, taxes, text messages sent by a call, the simulated callers' and judges' costs, and the carrier's recording or transcription add-ons if used.
- **The agent model's price** is Muse Ember's Standard mode up to 200,000 input tokens; calls here sent about 57,000 tokens at most in a call.
