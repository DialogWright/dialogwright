# The cost of a call in each mode (2026-11-25)

> Plan 3, Task 7 of [the agent runtime](../2026-11-25-agent-runtime-plan-3.md), the spec's "Cost comparison" ([2026-11-11-agent-runtime.md](../2026-11-11-agent-runtime.md)). Per-call figures are measured from traces and recorded runs; the carrier, speech recognition and speech prices are list prices read from the providers' pages on 2026-11-25. Sharing this page is the maintainer's decision.

## The answer

**The model's share of a call's cost is small in both modes, so the difference between them is a few percent of a call's total at any volume.**

- **Per call, models only:** agent mode about $0.0084 at most (the agent model $0.0063 measured, plus the decision model's routing requests at an upper bound of $0.0021); scripted mode about $0.002 (measured on one call).
- **Per call, everything else:** $0.10 to $0.26 for a call of about three and a half billed minutes, from the carrier, speech recognition and speech, at list prices, the same for both modes.
- **The difference** is about $0.006 a call: 2.3% to 5.9% of a call's total, depending on the carrier path. At 2,000 calls a month it is about $12.
- **Where it starts to matter:** it takes about 16,000 calls a month for the difference to reach $100 a month, and about 160,000 for $1,000. Below tens of thousands of calls a month, cost is not a reason to choose either mode.
- **One billed minute of carrier time costs more than the whole difference:** a minute is $0.0785 on the Twilio path, 12.5 times the difference in model cost per call. A mode whose calls run a minute longer costs more than a mode whose model costs more.

### What it means (the maintainer's reading, 2026-11-26)

Both models are cheap and do their jobs well: the decision model for scripted mode's typed questions and agent mode's routing, the agent model for agent mode's conversation. The agent model costs more, but that is a rounding error beside the call's carrier, recognition and speech. So the mode and the model for a line are chosen by what the job needs, not by their price. The larger saving is in the carrier path: Telnyx, with the work that lets a caller talk over its lines, costs $0.10 to $0.20 a call against Twilio's $0.26, many times more than the difference between the modes could ever save.

**How a request is counted.**
- **Agent mode:** the agent model is one request per model round, as the trace's `usage` holds it. The decision model is asked once per caller turn: a routing reading, or on the turn that answers a read-back, the reading of the yes. The live count equals the caller turns in 16 of the 17 calls (one call had two more readings than turns). For the simulated calls the count is the caller turns.
- **Scripted mode:** the decision model gets one request a turn, with all the turn's questions in it (25 on average on Harbor). The injection screen is one of the questions, so it adds no request.

Agent mode asks the decision model fewer questions a turn (the routing reading, three task questions and two more), not 25, so a request is smaller than these. **How the per-minute prices are put together for a call.** Two paths, each priced as its own parts:
- **Twilio:** $0.0085 + $0.07 = **$0.0785 a minute**. If ConversationRelay does not include speech recognition and speech, a call costs more than this; Twilio's pages do not say.
- **Telnyx, built from parts** (how the wheel shop's Telnyx calls run, its own voice and recognizer): $0.002 + $0.0032 + recognition, $0.005 (Deepgram) to $0.015 (Telnyx's own), plus speech at $0.032 per 1,000 characters. Characters are measured: the live calls' replies came to 626 characters a minute at the median, so speech is $0.020 a minute. The total is **$0.030 to $0.040 a minute**.
- **Telnyx with its Conversation Relay add-on:** $0.002 + $0.0032 + $0.05 = **$0.055 a minute**, if the add-on includes recognition and speech, which its page does not say.

Calls are billed by the minute, rounded up, by both carriers (Twilio's usual practice; Telnyx's voice AI page says so). Each live call is rounded up to whole minutes, and the cost below is the mean over that carrier's own live calls.

- **Which figures are measured and which are list prices.** Measured: the agent model's tokens and cost, the decision-model request counts, the call lengths and the characters spoken, and the scripted call's decision-model cost. Prices: the agent model's, the decision model's, and every carrier, recognition and speech price, each a list price. Not measured: the decision model's cost in agent mode (an upper bound) and anything about calls longer or shorter than the sample.
- **The difference is the same dollars on every path,** since the shared cost is the same; it is a larger share where the shared cost is smaller.
- **It is a share, not a threshold.** The difference grows in step with calls, so no volume turns it from small to large. It reaches $100 a month at about 15,900 calls and $1,000 at about 159,000. Where it matters is a business that runs a very large volume on the cheapest path, or one whose margin is in cents a call.
- **A mode's own call length matters more.** The one scripted phone call ran 2 min 31 s (3 billed minutes) against the Twilio live calls' mean of 3.33 billed minutes. At its own length scripted mode's shared cost on the Twilio path is $0.236 a call against $0.262: a difference of $0.026, 4 times the difference in model cost. On Telnyx the scripted call's longer replies (2,217 characters) cost as much in speech as the minute saved. The sample is one scripted call, so this shows how much length counts, not that scripted calls run shorter.

## Limits

- **Sample sizes.** Agent mode: 17 live calls, all placed by the maintainer to test the line, and 120 simulated ones. Scripted mode: one phone call with substance and three chat sessions, so its per-call figures are one observation; the per-request figures rest on 452 and 429 recorded requests, which are not calls. The second voice run was not in, so no figure here comes from it.
- **Demo call shapes.** The wheel shop's calls are a booking of about eleven turns; Harbor's one live call asked about coverage and reported a claim. A real shop's calls (more or fewer turns, longer replies, a caller who calls back) cost differently, and call length drives the shared cost much more than it drives the model's.
- **List prices, not negotiated ones.** Every price is a pay-as-you-go list price on the day it was read. Providers publish volume tiers and committed rates; Telnyx says its rates vary by destination and volume tier, and Twilio's volume discounts apply to the carrier part. The model prices change; the agent model's is read from its page into `price.ts` with a date.
- **Recognition and speech under Twilio.** Twilio's pages do not say whether the ConversationRelay price includes speech recognition and speech, so the Twilio figure may be low. Telnyx's Conversation Relay add-on page is likewise silent on what it includes. The Telnyx from-parts path is the one whose components are named.
- **The decision model in agent mode** has no token count in its trace, so its cost is an upper bound from scripted mode's per-request tokens. It is under a quarter of agent mode's model cost, so the bound does not change the picture.
- **Not counted:** hosting and storage, the number's monthly rental, taxes, text messages sent by a call, the simulated callers' and judges' costs, and the carrier's recording or transcription add-ons if used.
- **The agent model's price** is Muse Ember's Standard mode up to 200,000 input tokens; calls here sent about 57,000 tokens at most in a call.
