# The converse runtime on the voice path, measured (2026-11-25)

Plan 2 ([its plan](../2026-11-24-agent-runtime-plan-2.md)) put the converse runtime on the phone. This page measures it with simulated callers over the voice path.

**What is simulated.**
- The harness plays Twilio's carrier over the relay wire, in text: `pnpm eval --channel voice`, src/eval/voiceDriver.ts.
- Each reply is played at 2.7 words a second.
- A caller with `voice.interrupts` cuts in once 40% of a reply of more than 30 words has played.
- A caller with `voice.fragments` sends a turn of more than 8 words in two parts, 600 ms apart, as a recognizer that ended the turn early.

**What is not simulated:** speech recognition, speech and a real carrier.

**The run.**
- The wheel shop on the converse runtime (`apps/wheelshop-agent`, Muse Ember 1.0 at minimal effort).
- 15 personas, 4 runs each, over the voice path. The same 60 calls were then made over the chat the same night.
- Personas: `apps/wheelshop-agent/eval/voice-personas.yaml`. A third of them interrupt, a third fragment, a third do neither.
- Callers: Claude Haiku 5.5.
- Judged by Claude Sonnet 5.5 subagents in the maintainer's session, from each call's judge input (`--judge none`, then `eval:rejudge --verdicts`), under the material-claims rubric.
- **Spend:** $0.68 in all ($0.40 voice, $0.28 chat), against a $15 cap.

## The bars

| Bar (the design's) | Voice | Chat |
|---|---|---|
| First sentence, median (at most about 1.2 s) | 736 ms | 677 ms |
| First sentence, p95 (at most 2.5 s) | 1,204 ms heard; 1,381 ms the reply's own | 1,247 ms |
| Timeouts (at most 1% of turns) | 0.0% of 622 | 0.3% of 336 |
| Cooperative calls done correctly (at least 95%) | **67%** of 39 | **88%** of 40 |
| Contradicted material claims (none) | **4** | **6** |
| Agent cost per call | $0.0049 | $0.0034 |

- **The speed bars are met on both channels.** The bridge ("One sec.") was said on 50 of 498 voice turns. It covers the slow ones, so the first thing heard is never late; the reply's own p95 is 1.4 s.
- **The task bar is missed on both channels.** On voice, the whole gap is one kind of caller (below).

## By how the caller speaks

| Voice manner (5 personas, 20 calls each) | Voice: done | Voice: correct | Chat: done | Chat: correct |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Neither | **20 of 20** | 20 of 20 | 19 of 20 | 19 of 20 |
| Fragments (the recognizer ends a turn early) | 17 of 20 | 18 of 20 | 19 of 20 | 18 of 20 |
| Interrupts (cuts in on long replies) | **8 of 20** | 18 of 20 | 19 of 20 | 20 of 20 |

- **Callers who neither interrupt nor fragment are flawless on voice.**
- **Fragmented turns cost little.** The second part reaches the model with the first, in one reply (172 cut-ins and 125 two-part turns over the run).
- **Interrupting callers rarely finish.** They cut in over the read-back too, since it is a reply of more than 30 words. Their "yes" then never counts, because a read-back counts only when the caller heard 85% of it (the design: "a yes counts only if the read-back played to its end, or nearly"). The model reads it back again, the caller cuts in again, and the call runs out of turns or the caller hangs up.
- **That is the safe side of the rule, working as built.** But the harness caller is harsher than a person: it cuts in on every long reply, the read-back included, where a person usually lets a read-back finish, or says "yes, yes" over its last words.

## The contradicted claims

Four on voice, six on chat. Every one but one is a "done" claim: the line said something was sent, noted or confirmed when the call's actions do not show it.
- **Voice, interrupting callers:** twice, after repeated read-backs that were not heard, the model told the caller the request was with the shop when nothing had been sent.
- **Voice, other:**
  - a contact name said to be "noted on the call", with no action recording it;
  - a caller's date said to be noted in what the shop is passed, with the callback note carrying no notes.
- **Chat:**
  - a confirmation step described as a send that "didn't go through" (twice);
  - a drop-off said to be confirmed before the caller's yes to the price text;
  - a preference said to be on a callback note whose notes were empty.

## What follows

1. **Bounding unheard read-backs** (plan 3, Task 1, in progress).
   - A read-back not heard counts as a refusal of its action, so the business's `limits.refusalsPerAction` brings the offer of a person rather than a loop.
   - This also removes the loop in which the model claimed a send that never happened.
2. **The read-back rule against real callers.**
   - The 85% rule is safe, but a real caller saying "yes, that's right" over the last words of a read-back is common.
   - Plan 3's live calls should count how often a yes over a read-back is refused, before the rule is tuned (the design's anti-goal: no rule changed for one call).
   - The harness's interrupting caller could also be made truer to people (letting a question that asks for a yes play out). That is a change to the measure, and is the maintainer's call.
3. **"Noted" claims.** The model says a detail is noted when the action it called carried no such field.
   - The prevention work (the call record told to the model) covers what ran, not what each action's params held.
   - A next step is to tell the model, in the record, what each action carried. Done after this run: each change in the line's record now lists the values it carried (a phone number by its last four, an empty one as nothing).

## Limits

- **Text only.** No recognizer, no speech, no carrier; Telnyx's event order is not simulated (Twilio's frames only).
- **One night, 60 calls a channel.**
- **The judges** read each call's judge input; their verdicts are a reading, not a guarantee.
