# Example Wheel Works on the converse runtime

Example Wheel Works is invented: a small wheel repair and refinishing shop inside a detailing shop at 400 Commerce Drive, Rivergate. This app is the shop's line rebuilt on the converse runtime ([the design](../../docs/designs/2026-11-11-agent-runtime.md)): a model holds the conversation from the shop's file, `agent.yaml`, and the runtime owns what is done. It takes calls on its numbers as well as chat ([plan 2](../../docs/designs/2026-11-24-agent-runtime-plan-2.md)): a caller can cut in, the model is told what the caller heard of a cut reply, and a bridge (`lines.bridge`) is said when the model is slow; `pnpm eval --channel voice` runs the simulated callers over that path. It replaced the agent-mode app (`apps/wheelshop`, removed in plan 3); its business facts, its assumptions and its shop code were copied from it.

No real business, place, person or number is in this folder: the dealerships are invented and the numbers are in the 555 range. The shop's phone and the number texts come from are settings (`SHOP_PHONE`, `TEXT_FROM`), never in the folder, and so is a tester's phone recognized as Rivergate Motors (`env.DEALER_TEST_LINE` among the account's numbers, set privately in the app's settings; unset, left out).

## The calls it takes

- **A dealership regular**, recognized by the number it calls from (+15555550142 is Rivergate Motors), books its cars into a drop-off window. The model asks who the number is on file for (`recognise_caller`), looks up the day (`check_availability`, each window with how many cars it can still take), and calls `book_dealer` with the account `recognise_caller` returned, the number of cars, which cars and wheels, and a window the lookup returned; the runtime refuses any other account before anything is read back, reads the booking back ("So that's Rivergate Motors, three in all: a 2021 BMW 3 Series, ..., dropping off Thursday, November 12, morning, by 8. Shall we book it?") and books it on the yes, the shop texted a summary (`notify_shop_booking`, a follow-on). A window holds three cars at most, so `cars` offers one to three; more go in another window as a booking of their own. The calendar still refuses a booking whose window filled between the read-back and the yes.
- **A retail caller** says what happened to which car, hears the shop's ballpark for the work with its caveat when they ask, picks a drop-off window from the lookup, gives a number and says whether we may text them there. The runtime reads the request back in the shop's words; on the yes it is sent (`send_request`, taking a place in the window), the caller is texted asking for a photo (`send_photo_request`, only where they said yes to texts: the gate's `oneOf` rule, `no-consent`), and the shop gets a summary (`notify_shop_request`). A photo the caller texts back is sent on to the shop's phone (`onText`).
- **A caller who wants a person** leaves a callback note (`leave_callback`: their name, a number, what it is about), read back and texted to the shop, as part of their request: a retail caller may send a request and leave a note on the same call, and a dealership may leave one too. There is no live transfer: the handoff line, said only when the model has failed past the call's budget, asks the caller to get in touch again.

## The voice

The line speaks as the business ("we", "the shop", "Example Wheel Works"), never as a named person, as the old app does (its call 44). The persona is "a friendly, plain-spoken auto-shop voice; brief on the phone; speaks for the shop as 'we'": it shapes how the model sounds, never what it claims. Asked whether it is a person, the model says it is an automated assistant (the runtime's own rule, not the business's).

## The greeting, the unclear lines and the start bar

For the owner to correct, each marked `# assumption: the owner corrects these` in `agent.yaml`:

- **The greeting** is a receptionist's opening: what the shop does ("where we make damaged wheels look new again"), then the two things the line can do (price a repair and set up a drop-off, or have someone from the shop call back). A recognised dealership hears a shorter one from `dealer_booking` ("Example Wheel Works, hi. Booking some cars in today?"); it names no account, because a number on file recognises an account and does not prove who is calling.
- **A turn the line cannot follow** (the decision model reads none of the offered tasks) is not guessed at: the model says what the line can do, with only lookups on offer, and `business.line` ("This line is how we price repairs and set up drop-offs while we're busy in the shop fixing wheels.") gives it the reason. On the third such turn in a row the line says `lines.unclear` ("Sorry, this line can only help with a wheel repair or a callback. Call us back any time.") and ends the call.
- **A read-back turned down** with nothing said of what to change (a bare "no") hears `lines.notConfirmed` ("No problem. What would you like to change, or would you rather cancel the request?"), and the model answers what the caller says next. A caller who says what to change is answered by the model, which reads the request back again.
- **The start bar** (`routing.startAt: 0.7`) is how sure the decision model must be that a caller wants a task before the line starts it. It was chosen on labelled turns: 0.7 kept the best accuracy with fewer wrong starts than 0.6, and 0.8 missed a third of starts. Raise it to start fewer tasks wrongly, lower it to miss fewer.

## How the facts were split among the journeys

The line has two journeys (`request` and `dealer_booking`) and a root ([the design](../../docs/designs/2026-11-12-journeys.md)). The owner corrects the split by moving an item between `agent.yaml`'s top level and a journey; every item keeps its assumption marker.

**One request journey** (2026-11-13, the maintainer's direction: "A call back is really just a request that didn't let you text", and "a single journey with happy paths working"). The spec's table had three journeys, the third a `callback` journey of its own. The after-hours runs ([the measured calls](../../docs/designs/eval/2026-11-after-hours.md)) found its cost: a caller in the repair request who wanted a call back had to be switched to another journey, and the model in the request said there was no way to leave a callback. A callback note is now part of the caller's request: `request` (once `repair_request`) offers `leave_callback` beside `send_request`, for a caller who wants someone to call them instead of a drop-off, or can't or won't set one up, and its `completes: []` keeps it working after either change, so a caller may send a request and also leave a note, each read back and confirmed on its own. `dealer_booking` offers `leave_callback` too (a dealership may want a person), with `completes: [book_dealer]`, so a note leaves the booking open; its `when` names a call back as well, so a recognised dealership asking for one is not read as unclear.

| Where | What is there |
|---|---|
| Base (every journey sees it) | Who, where and hours; services, ballpark prices and the caveat; what is not offered; the damage and diamond cut facts; the drop-off windows and turnaround; dealerships send trade-ins here; no live transfer, and that a callback note exists; the answers on turnaround, what to bring, discounts, price by car, being late, pick-up, someone else dropping off, parking and the job's status. |

Instructions: collect what `send_request` needs and look the day up before offering a window; a callback note, where the caller wants a call rather than a drop-off, carries only the name, the number, what it is about and notes; never say when someone will call back; do not discuss dealership accounts or their rates. |

Instructions: a callback note for the dealership is fine when the caller wants one; never say when someone will call back. |

The prevention fact that a request and a callback note each need a number the caller gives was one line in the base, then worded twice, once in each of the two journeys that are now one; it is one line again in `request` ("A request or a callback note needs a number..."). The original line carried no assumption marker; this one is marked in `agent.yaml` for the owner to correct, like the journey's other facts.

One judgment to check: the answer to "Do I get a confirmation number or a written quote?" used to describe both a request and a dealership booking. Its dealership half became a `dealer_booking` fact, so a repair request's answer never mentions dealership accounts.

## Quote and confirm

Carried over from the old agent-mode app's DESIGN.md, which this app's shop page, quotes and texts were copied from; its paths and the texts' home (`src/shopTexts.ts`, where it said business.yaml's `texts`) are adjusted, and a mention of the agent model's availability lookup is the old app's. `src/quotes.test.ts` holds the behaviour.

The maintainer's decisions (2026-11-08; the agent-mode design's calls 52 and 53): a retail request becomes a booking only after the customer accepts the shop's quote. `src/quotes.ts` is the flow; the shop page is where it starts.

1. **The form.** Each request card on the shop page has a "Quote and book" form until the request is booked: the price (an amount: "180", "$180", "180.50"), the drop-off day and window, and Send. The window is one select of the windows with room in the next two weeks, on the days the shop opens, in the calendar the dealerships book into (`Systems.roomIn`: capacity less the standing drop-off, the bookings and the holds), leaving out any day whose 6 pm the day before has passed; the caller's own choice is picked where it has room ("(their choice)"), a re-quote's own window where it is quoted again. A request whose caller said no to texts has no form: the card says to call them, with the number.
2. **Sending** (`POST /wheelshop-agent/shop/quote`) checks the form's Origin and its token, the request (on file, not booked, its `textOk` a yes), the form's version, the price and the window, then keeps the quote (the shop's log, `quote`), which holds one car of room in its window, and texts the customer `SHOP_TEXTS.quote` in `src/shopTexts.ts`'s words at `+1` and the request's callback number, then sends the browser back to the card (303). A newer quote for the same request supersedes the one waiting, its hold moved, in the same line of the log. The card shows "Quoted, waiting" with the price, the window, the hold's end and the text's status; a quote whose text did not go (`limited`, `failed`, `refused` by the page's sender, or `printed` with no sender) says so, with why and the number to call (call 53). A waiting quote has a **Withdraw** button (`POST /wheelshop-agent/shop/withdraw`, its own token): the hold is released, the quote withdrawn (a line of the log), the card "Withdrawn", and the request may be quoted again. The form asks whether to text the customer ("Text them that it is withdrawn", `SHOP_TEXTS.quoteWithdrawn`), unticked by default: the shop calls if it wants (call 53).
3. **The reply** (`onText`): a text from the number a quote was sent to, matched exactly (a `+1` number, the engine's `sameNumber` against `+1` and the quote's ten digits: "+445555550177" is not a reply, call 53), while the quote is its request's latest and is waiting, is read by the decision model (`ctx.decide`, [the guide's 13.17](../../docs/guide/13-channels.md#1317-texts-sending-and-receiving)), never by matching the word. Two typed questions: `acceptsQuote`, the goal only (does the reply accept the quote as offered and ask to book it?), and `asksChange` (does it ask for another day, time or price, ask a question, or decline), sent with the quote as texted and the reply word as context (`about`), with no instruction to match the word (call 53). A yes is `acceptsQuote` at the read-back's bar (`CONFIRM_YES`, 0.7), above `asksChange`, and `asksChange` under 0.5; so "confirm", "CONFIRM!", "yes confirm" and "sounds good, book it" book, and "can we do Wednesday instead?" does not. The reply word is `SHOP_TEXTS.confirmWord` (CONFIRM where it is left out; not YES, which Twilio takes as an opt-in keyword).
   - **The quote is looked at again** once the model has answered, on the clock of that moment (call 53): if meanwhile the shop sent a newer quote, withdrew it, or the request was booked, the reply goes to the shop as `SHOP_TEXTS.quoteReply`; nothing is booked. A quote that is not waiting, not its request's latest, or whose request has a booking is never booked (`bookQuote`), so two yeses read at once book once.
   - **A yes** books the held window (a booking of one car, the request's car, with the quote it came from: the hold becomes the booking in one line of the log), texts `SHOP_TEXTS.booked` to the number that consented (the request's callback number, never the number the reply came from), and texts the shop `SHOP_TEXTS.quoteBooked`, which adds `SHOP_TEXTS.customerNotTexted` ("Customer not texted (limited): call them.") where the customer's text did not go (call 53). The card shows "Booked"; the booking joins "Bookings by day and window" under the customer's name.
   - **Anything else**, or no reading (the model failed), goes to the shop's phone as `SHOP_TEXTS.quoteReply` and the customer's words ("Reply from Jordan Lee, ...0177: can we do Wednesday instead?"), with any photos; the hold stays. The shop sends a new quote from the page if a change is wanted.
   - A photo with no words is sent on to the shop as before, and the "We'll text you a price soon." thank-you goes only to a request not yet quoted.
4. **The hold lapses** at 6 pm the day before the drop-off, or 24 hours after the quote, whichever comes first (`holdUntil`, worked out as the quote is sent and kept with it). 6 pm is in the server's time zone (`TIMEZONE`, the one the shop page shows its times in): the shop's calendar has its own, agent.yaml's. A lapse is a line of the shop's log (`quote_state`, `lapsed`); the window is free again, the shop's phone is told (`SHOP_TEXTS.quoteLapsed`) and the card shows "Lapsed". The check runs on the server's minute sweep (the shop page's route's `sweep`), as the page is drawn, as the server starts (holds whose time ran out while it was stopped), and before a reply is read.
   - **A lapsed quote never books** (call 53). A reply to it (in the week after the lapse) is not read by the model: it goes to the shop as `SHOP_TEXTS.quoteLate` ("..., so call them. They wrote: ..."), and the customer gets no text. A yes the calendar refuses while the hold is still on (a window over full, as a capacity lowered under the hold would leave it) tells the shop to call (`SHOP_TEXTS.quoteFull`).
5. **Each card shows its state:** New, Photo in (a photo texted back), Quoted, waiting (the price, the window, the hold's end), Booked, or Lapsed; the page's header counts each.
6. **The forms are protected** as the page is: a console page (`auth: 'console'`: local only, or behind the console's sign-in with `CONSOLE_AUTH=token`, where a POST without a session is a 401), POST only (a GET is a 405), a form's body only (`application/x-www-form-urlencoded`, at most 4 KB), the browser's word that it came from the page (the engine's `formFromOwnPage`, the check the console's sign-in makes: Sec-Fetch-Site, when sent, same-origin; the Origin absent, `null` or the server's own; call 53), and a token of its own: an HMAC of what the form does (quote or withdraw), the request and its version under a key the server makes as it starts, so a page drawn before a restart is refreshed before it sends (403, saying so). A double submit is once: the quote's write key is the request and the form's version, so the second submit returns the first quote and sends no text; a form drawn before another quote was sent (an older version) is refused (409: refresh).
7. **The shop's texts** go to `SHOP_PHONE`, as every summary does; put it in `TEXT_LIMIT_EXEMPT`. A customer may get four texts in a day (the photo request, the thank-you, the quote and the booking), one more for each new quote the shop sends and one for a withdrawal it chooses to text. The engine's default `TEXT_LIMIT_PER_NUMBER` is 3 (kept), so the booking's text may be refused (`limited`), and the shop is then told to call: the wheelshop-agent's `.env.example` suggests 5, and the server notes at start when the limit is under 5.
8. **The page's sender is held to its numbers** (call 53): the shop page texts through the app's sender (`AppPageDeps.texts`) wrapped in an allow-list (`allowListTexts`): the shop's phone, and a request's callback number whose caller said yes to texts. Any other number is refused (`refused`, never sent) and logged by its last four. The page's texts' keys (`quote R101 0`, `lapsed Q101`, `withdrawn Q101`) are the audit's ids, so they hold references only, no number and no name.
9. **The decision model is asked only for a waiting quote**: every asking is a model call (the audit's `text_decision` row counts its tokens), and the engine puts no limit on how often a hook asks, so a text from any other number is never sent to the model.

## What changed from the old app, and why

- **One file of the business, and the model talks.** The old app built intents, forms and slots from `business.yaml` and checked each value the model understood by its slot type; the model never called a tool. Here the model calls the actions itself, the runtime checks each value by its param's type, and the read-back and the gate are the guard.
- **Prices as ranges.** The old line never said a figure (it quoted from a photo); the design gives the business ranges with its caveat, so a caller who asks hears a ballpark. The caveat promises the photo only once the request is in, as the old app's price line did: the text asking for one is sent by the request's yes.
- **A request books its window.** The old request took no room (the shop confirmed the window with its quote). Here `send_request` takes a place in the window the caller picked, as the brief asks, until the shop quotes it: the quote's hold then stands for it (a quote may move it to another window), so the calendar never counts one car twice. The shop page shows a request waiting for its quote among the window's holds.
- **The dealer booking takes the account (from `recognise_caller`), the number of cars, which cars and wheels (`vehicles`, added in fix round 1: dealers gave the list and the booking had nowhere to keep it) and the window**; the shop's summary says them all.
- **Opening hours, an after-hours greeting and no callback times** (2026-11-13, the maintainer's direction). `business.open` gives the line the shop's hours as agent.yaml's text already gives them (Monday to Friday, 7:30 am to 5:30 pm, closed at weekends; no holiday dates, the owner adds them under `closed`), so the model is told whether the shop is open now and when it closes or next opens. A call that starts while the shop is closed hears `lines.greetingAfterHours`, also a dealership's. It leads with what the line can do and says the shop is closed after it ("We can price a repair, set up a drop-off, or take a note for the shop to call you back; the shop itself is closed right now. How can we help?"), since terse callers left after the first greeting, which led with "We're closed right now". The line never promises a callback at a time, nor ties one to the shop's opening (rule 1 counts that as a time): "when will someone call me back" is answered "As soon as the shop can; we don't give a set time", and both journeys' instructions say never to say when.
- **The chat says "messaging"** (2026-11-13, the maintainer's direction). The greeting, the after-hours greeting and the unclear line each have a chat wording (`chat:`), so the web chat no longer thanks a customer for calling. The model writes its own goodbye with `end_call`, which ends the chat; the closing line ("Thanks for getting in touch", which fits both) is said only where it writes none.
- **The short-notice reply deadline** (2026-11-13, an assumption the owner must confirm). At night a caller can pick one of the next morning's windows, and the business information's rule for replying to the price text ("within 24 hours, or by 6 pm the day before drop-off") has already passed for it, as it has for a drop-off the same day. The answer to "When do I need to send the photo?" now adds: where the drop-off is too soon for that (the same day, or the next morning when it is already past 6 pm), reply to the price text before the window's drop-off time. Marked in `agent.yaml` for the owner to confirm. The owner corrects the hours, the greeting and the answer; each carries its assumption marker.
- **The callback note takes a name and what it is about**, beside the number; the shop's text says all three.
- **The finish is required, in the caller's words**: diamond cut (two-tone included), painted, or not sure, so the read-back always says it. The first fix said "painted all over", and a caller with a mostly painted face was coerced into it; "painted" covers any face with no bare machined metal (fix round 1). The shop page still shows whether it is diamond cut. It is asked plainly (bright metal coloured, painted such as grey or black, or a combination of both) since 2026-11-25: "fine machined lines" confused a caller on a live call.
- **The Friday-morning standing drop-off stays** in the shop's fake calendar, so the demo has a full window to meet.

## The chat and the number calling

A chat has no calling number, and `app.yaml` says `chat.callerNumberFromContext: false`: a chat that names a number in its start's context is not taken as calling from it, so no page can claim a dealership's account. The simulated callers' own server (`pnpm eval`, eval/server.ts) turns it on for itself alone, so the dealership persona, which names its account's number (`callingFrom` in `eval/personas.yaml`), is recognized as it would be on the phone. That override is the harness's, given to the server it starts in its own process, never a setting a deployment can have.

## Settings and the shop page

Nothing is read from the process as the app is made (`src/index.ts`). agent.yaml's `env.DEALER_TEST_LINE` is read by the server from the app's own settings as it starts (on a host, its own settings file), and the shop page is made then, with the app's settings (`SHOP_DATA_DIR`, `SHOP_PHONE`), the server's sender (so a quote sent from the page is texted), its clock and its log. The actions read their settings at call time (`ctx.env`). Each simulated call runs on the app made again (`app.fresh`), its records in memory alone whatever `SHOP_DATA_DIR` says, so no call sees what another booked and none reads or writes the shop's log.

## Findings

What building the app and its simulated-caller runs on Mercury 3 found (2026-11-11, twelve personas, three calls each): [the first run](../../docs/designs/eval/2026-11-wheelshop-agent-mercury/before-fixes.md), [the second](../../docs/designs/eval/2026-11-wheelshop-agent-mercury/run-2.md) after the first wording fixes, and [the third](../../docs/designs/eval/2026-11-wheelshop-agent-mercury/summary.md) after fix round 1, which fixed every runtime and harness defect below. Each shortfall is a wording fixed here, a defect fixed in the runtime or the harness, or left to the model comparison (Task 10). No persona was changed to pass.

### Runtime and harness defects, fixed in fix round 1

1. **Mercury was sent no reasoning effort, and a reply cut by the token limit was spoken as it was.** Its reasoning counted toward `maxTokens` (a probe at 400 spent 389 tokens and wrote no text): the first run heard 17 recovery lines (empty replies) and several replies cut mid-sentence ("For the photo to"), said as if whole. *Fixed:* agent.yaml's `model.effort` (minimal, low, medium, high), sent in each provider's form (Inception's `reasoning_effort`, where minimal is its `instant`; OpenRouter's `reasoning.effort`; none to Anthropic); a reply stopped by the token limit is a failed turn, its unfinished words never said (asked once more where time allows, else the recovery line). This app runs at `effort: minimal`, `maxTokens: 600`. The third run heard 2 recovery lines and no reply cut short, and its first word came sooner.
2. **The model said things were done that were not.** At a dealer's hedged yes nothing ran, the model was told "The caller did not confirm", and still said "That's booked"; on the second run a retail caller heard "Your request is in" with no request sent. *Fixed:* a read-back that is not confirmed tells the model "Not done: nothing was changed, booked or sent", and the system prompt's third rule says nothing is booked, sent, changed or done unless an action's result on the call says so. On the third run no call claimed a change that did not run (every "it's in" and "you're booked" followed the action's yes and result).
3. **No rule held a dealer's account to the number calling**, so `book_dealer` refused other accounts in its own code, after the read-back and the yes. *Fixed:* the account is a lookup's value: `recognise_caller` returns the account the number calling is on file for, and `book_dealer`'s `account` is a slot from it, so the runtime refuses any other before anything is read back. The check in code is gone.
4. **A converse app's pages got nothing from the server** (settings read from the process at import, no sender). *Fixed:* `ConverseApp.pages` may be made by the server as it starts (`ConversePageDeps`: the app's settings, its sender, the clock, the log), and agent.yaml's `env.<NAME>` references are read from the app's own settings then, never from the process at load.
5. **The harness's judge was not shown what an action returned.** It counted every availability stated from a lookup, the references the actions returned (R101, B101) and the date from the system prompt as unsupported: 42 of the second run's 61 "unsupported claims" were these. *Fixed:* a converse action's result is recorded in the audit (`tool_result`, beside its gate row, with the call's masked values masked), the judge pairs each action with it, and it is told the day, where the business is.
6. **The phone read-back said only the last four digits**, and a caller who gave their number asked five times to hear it whole. *Fixed:* a number the caller gave is read back whole, in groups ("555 555 0114"); the number they call from is "the number you're calling from, ending in 0114".
7. **Follow-on actions were offered to the model as tools.** *Fixed:* an action named in another's `then` is never offered, and a call to one is refused; it runs only after its parent's yes.
8. **One server's systems lasted the whole run**, so later calls found windows filled by earlier ones (a dealer was offered "room for just one car"). *Fixed:* each simulated call runs on a server of its own, on the app made fresh (`ConverseApp.fresh`).

### The second run's shortfalls, triaged

- **Claims of "it's in" or "booked" with nothing done** (both runtime, item 2): the dealer told "That's booked" after a hedged yes that ran nothing (`dealership-regular` 3), and a retail caller told "Your request is in" with no request sent (`retail-out-of-order` 3). A third, "you're booked" said of a request (`discount-pusher` 1), was a request that had run, misnamed. None on the third run.
- **Invented policies and process facts:** 19 of the judge's 61 unsupported claims were not artefacts of item 5. Of those, 2 were the done-claims above. 3 were about photos ("we're not able to receive photos through this chat", "there isn't a way to send just a photo", "they'll want a photo" to a caller who had declined texts), where the caveat invited a photo before any request: *wording*, the caveat now promises the photo once the request is in, and a new fact says the line takes none, one comes by reply to our text. 1 was the request's standing ("you're booked" for a request): *wording*, a new fact says a request holds its window and the shop confirms the drop-off with the price. 3 were a painted finish's handling (a category "painted all over", a price for "a painted finish", missing paint "covered by the refinishing"), from the finish coercion: *wording*, `finish` reworded. 10 were the model's own reach: no drop-off fee, the only charge being the work, the price the same for any car, a crew that "will expect" the details (twice), a confirmation text, texts as well as a call back, no warranty program, a waiting area, and "Saturday has no openings" (true of the calendar, though no lookup said it). Those are left to the model comparison (Task 10), which measures each candidate's rate.
- **The finish coercion:** the read-back's options had "painted all over", and callers with a painted face (some partly) were told their wheel was "painted all over", or argued it; now "painted".

### The third run's shortfalls, left to the model comparison

Completed and correct rose sharply on the third run, and its judge counted 20 unsupported claims. None is a lookup or reference artefact, and none claims a change that did not run. They are the model's reach: promises with no source (no charge or commitment until a price is approved, the price likely at the low end, a few minutes late "shouldn't be an issue", "you should hear from us soon", nothing to bring, a text as well as a callback, calling during the windows to reach a person), the same-day fact said of all curb rash, a paint match, and one request sent under the placeholder name "Caller" after the model never asked for a name. Each is a measure for Task 10's comparison (unsupported claims per call by model); the wording here already says what the business says, and adding facts to deny each invention is not the way to fix a model's reach.

### Seen, not changed

- "scuffed rim edge" was quoted the curb rash range; scratch and scuff repair has no price in the file. A fair reading of the damage, or a price the owner should add.
- The decision model did not read "yeah that's all right i think" as a yes; a hedge at a read-back is meant to fail safe.
- A caller who refused a callback note heard that calling during the windows reaches a person: the business says nothing of a phone answered by people.
