# Example Wheel Works on the converse runtime

Example Wheel Works is invented: a small wheel repair and refinishing shop inside a detailing shop at 400 Commerce Drive, Rivergate. This app is the shop's line rebuilt on the converse runtime ([the design](../../docs/designs/2026-11-11-agent-runtime.md)): a model holds the conversation from the shop's file, `agent.yaml`, and the runtime owns what is done. It replaces the agent-mode app (`apps/wheelshop`), which stays until plan 3 removes it; its business facts, its assumptions and its shop code are copied from there.

No real business, place, person or number is in this folder: the dealerships are invented and the numbers are in the 555 range. The shop's phone and the number texts come from are settings (`SHOP_PHONE`, `TEXT_FROM`), never in the folder, and so is a tester's phone recognized as Rivergate Motors (`env.DEALER_TEST_LINE` among the account's numbers, set privately in the app's settings; unset, left out).

## The calls it takes

- **A dealership regular**, recognized by the number it calls from (+15555550142 is Rivergate Motors), books its cars into a drop-off window. The model asks who the number is on file for (`recognise_caller`), looks up the day (`check_availability`, each window with how many cars it can still take), and calls `book_dealer` with the account `recognise_caller` returned, the number of cars, which cars and wheels, and a window the lookup returned; the runtime refuses any other account before anything is read back, reads the booking back ("So that's Rivergate Motors, three in all: a 2021 BMW 3 Series, ..., dropping off Thursday, November 12, morning, by 8. Shall we book it?") and books it on the yes, the shop texted a summary (`notify_shop_booking`, a follow-on). A window holds three cars at most, so `cars` offers one to three; more go in another window as a booking of their own. The calendar still refuses a booking whose window filled between the read-back and the yes.
- **A retail caller** says what happened to which car, hears the shop's ballpark for the work with its caveat when they ask, picks a drop-off window from the lookup, gives a number and says whether we may text them there. The runtime reads the request back in the shop's words; on the yes it is sent (`send_request`, taking a place in the window), the caller is texted asking for a photo (`send_photo_request`, only where they said yes to texts: the gate's `oneOf` rule, `no-consent`), and the shop gets a summary (`notify_shop_request`). A photo the caller texts back is sent on to the shop's phone (`onText`).
- **A caller who wants a person** leaves a callback note (`leave_callback`: their name, a number, what it is about), read back and texted to the shop. There is no live transfer: the handoff line, said only when the model has failed past the call's budget, asks the caller to get in touch again.

## The voice

The line speaks as the business ("we", "the shop", "Example Wheel Works"), never as a named person, as the old app does (its call 44). The persona is "a friendly, plain-spoken auto-shop voice; brief on the phone; speaks for the shop as 'we'": it shapes how the model sounds, never what it claims. Asked whether it is a person, the model says it is an automated assistant (the runtime's own rule, not the business's).

## The demo's assumptions, for the owner to correct

The demo needs answers only the owner can give. As with a real client, `agent.yaml` makes a reasonable assumption for each, marked in the file with `# assumption: the owner corrects these`:

The shop's own texts (the photo request, the summaries, the quote) are in `src/shopTexts.ts`: agent.yaml describes the business the model speaks for and has no place for the texts the code sends, and a converse folder holds only app.yaml and agent.yaml.

## What changed from the old app, and why

- **One file of the business, and the model talks.** The old app built intents, forms and slots from `business.yaml` and checked each value the model understood by its slot type; the model never called a tool. Here the model calls the actions itself, the runtime checks each value by its param's type, and the read-back and the gate are the guard.
- **Prices as ranges.** The old line never said a figure (it quoted from a photo); the design gives the business ranges with its caveat, so a caller who asks hears a ballpark. The caveat promises the photo only once the request is in, as the old app's price line did: the text asking for one is sent by the request's yes.
- **A request books its window.** The old request took no room (the shop confirmed the window with its quote). Here `send_request` takes a place in the window the caller picked, as the brief asks, until the shop quotes it: the quote's hold then stands for it (a quote may move it to another window), so the calendar never counts one car twice. The shop page shows a request waiting for its quote among the window's holds.
- **The dealer booking takes the account (from `recognise_caller`), the number of cars, which cars and wheels (`vehicles`, added in fix round 1: dealers gave the list and the booking had nowhere to keep it) and the window**; the shop's summary says them all.
- **The callback note takes a name and what it is about**, beside the number; the shop's text says all three.
- **The finish is required, in the caller's words**: diamond cut (two-tone included), painted, or not sure, so the read-back always says it. The first fix said "painted all over", and a caller with a mostly painted face was coerced into it; "painted" covers any face with no bare machined metal (fix round 1). The shop page still shows whether it is diamond cut.
- **The Friday-morning standing drop-off stays** in the shop's fake calendar, so the demo has a full window to meet.

## The chat and the number calling

A chat has no calling number, and `app.yaml` says `chat.callerNumberFromContext: false`: a chat that names a number in its start's context is not taken as calling from it, so no page can claim a dealership's account. The simulated callers' own server (`pnpm eval`, eval/server.ts) turns it on for itself alone, so the dealership persona, which names its account's number (`callingFrom` in `eval/personas.yaml`), is recognized as it would be on the phone. That override is the harness's, given to the server it starts in its own process, never a setting a deployment can have.

## Settings and the shop page

Nothing is read from the process as the app is made (`src/index.ts`). agent.yaml's `env.DEALER_TEST_LINE` is read by the server from the app's own settings as it starts (on a host, its own settings file), and the shop page is made then, with the app's settings (`SHOP_DATA_DIR`, `SHOP_PHONE`), the server's sender (so a quote sent from the page is texted), its clock and its log. The actions read their settings at call time (`ctx.env`). Each simulated call runs on the app made again (`app.fresh`), its records in memory alone whatever `SHOP_DATA_DIR` says, so no call sees what another booked and none reads or writes the shop's log.

## Findings

What building the app and its simulated-caller runs on Mercury 3 found (2026-11-11, twelve personas, three calls each): [the first run](../../docs/designs/eval/2026-11-wheelshop-agent-mercury/before-fixes.md), [the second](../../docs/designs/eval/2026-11-wheelshop-agent-mercury/run-2.md) after the first wording fixes, and [the third](../../docs/designs/eval/2026-11-wheelshop-agent-mercury/summary.md) after fix round 1, which fixed every runtime and harness defect below. Each shortfall is a wording fixed here, a defect fixed in the runtime or the harness, or left to the model comparison (Task 10). No persona was changed to pass.

### Runtime and harness defects, fixed in fix round 1

1. **Mercury was sent no reasoning effort, and a reply cut by the token limit was spoken as it was.** Its reasoning counted toward `maxTokens` (a probe at 400 spent 389 tokens and wrote no text): the first run heard 17 recovery lines (empty replies) and several replies cut mid-sentence ("For the photo to"), said as if whole. *Fixed:* agent.yaml's `model.effort` (minimal, low, medium, high), sent in each provider's form (Inception's `reasoning_effort`, where minimal is its `instant`; OpenRouter's `reasoning.effort`; none to Anthropic); a reply stopped by the token limit is a failed turn, its unfinished words never said (asked once more where time allows, else the recovery line). This app runs at `effort: minimal`, `maxTokens: 600`. The third run heard 2 recovery lines and no reply cut short, and its first word came sooner.
2. **The model said things were done that were not.** At a dealer's hedged yes nothing ran, the model was told "The caller did not confirm", and still said "That's booked"; on the second run a retail caller heard "Your request is in" with no request sent. *Fixed:* a read-back that is not confirmed tells the model "Not done: nothing was changed, booked or sent", and the system prompt's third rule says nothing is booked, sent, changed or done unless an action's result on the call says so. On the third run no call claimed a change that did not run (every "it's in" and "you're booked" followed the action's yes and result).
3. **No rule held a dealer's account to the number calling**, so `book_dealer` refused other accounts in its own code, after the read-back and the yes. *Fixed:* the account is a lookup's value: `recognise_caller` returns the account the number calling is on file for, and `book_dealer`'s `account` is a slot from it, so the runtime refuses any other before anything is read back. The check in code is gone.
4. **A converse app's pages got nothing from the server** (settings read from the process at import, no sender). *Fixed:* `ConverseApp.pages` may be made by the server as it starts (`ConversePageDeps`: the app's settings, its sender, the clock, the log), and agent.yaml's `env.<NAME>` references are read from the app's own settings then, never from the process at load.
5. **The harness's judge was not shown what an action returned.** It counted every availability stated from a lookup, the references the actions returned (R101, B101) and the date from the system prompt as unsupported: 42 of the second run's 61 "unsupported claims" were these. *Fixed:* a converse action's result is recorded in the audit (`tool_result`, beside its gate row, with the call's masked values masked), the judge pairs each action with it, and it is told the day, where the business is.
6. **The phone read-back said only the last four digits**, and a caller who gave their number asked five times to hear it whole. *Fixed:* a number the caller gave is read back whole, in groups ("555 555 0114"); the number they call from is "the number you're calling from, ending in 0114".
7. **Follow-on actions were offered to the model as tools.** *Fixed:* an action named in another's `then` is never offered, and a call to one is refused; it runs only after its parent's yes.
8. **One server's systems lasted the whole run**, so later calls found windows filled by earlier ones (a dealer was offered "room for just one car"). *Fixed:* each simulated call runs on a server of its own, on the app made fresh (`ConverseApp.fresh`).

### The second run's shortfalls, triaged

- **Claims of "it's in" or "booked" with nothing done** (both runtime, item 2): the dealer told "That's booked" after a hedged yes that ran nothing (`dealership-regular` 3), and a retail caller told "Your request is in" with no request sent (`retail-out-of-order` 3). A third, "you're booked" said of a request (`discount-pusher` 1), was a request that had run, misnamed. None on the third run.
- **Invented policies and process facts:** 19 of the judge's 61 unsupported claims were not artefacts of item 5. Of those, 2 were the done-claims above. 3 were about photos ("we're not able to receive photos through this chat", "there isn't a way to send just a photo", "they'll want a photo" to a caller who had declined texts), where the caveat invited a photo before any request: *wording*, the caveat now promises the photo once the request is in, and a new fact says the line takes none, one comes by reply to our text. 1 was the request's standing ("you're booked" for a request): *wording*, a new fact says a request holds its window and the shop confirms the drop-off with the price. 3 were a painted finish's handling (a category "painted all over", a price for "a painted finish", missing paint "covered by the refinishing"), from the finish coercion: *wording*, `finish` reworded. 10 were the model's own reach: no drop-off fee, the only charge being the work, the price the same for any car, a crew that "will expect" the details (twice), a confirmation text, texts as well as a call back, no warranty program, a waiting area, and "Saturday has no openings" (true of the calendar, though no lookup said it). Those are left to the model comparison (Task 10), which measures each candidate's rate.
- **The finish coercion:** the read-back's options had "painted all over", and callers with a painted face (some partly) were told their wheel was "painted all over", or argued it; now "painted".

### The third run's shortfalls, left to the model comparison

Completed and correct rose sharply on the third run, and its judge counted 20 unsupported claims. None is a lookup or reference artefact, and none claims a change that did not run. They are the model's reach: promises with no source (no charge or commitment until a price is approved, the price likely at the low end, a few minutes late "shouldn't be an issue", "you should hear from us soon", nothing to bring, a text as well as a callback, calling during the windows to reach a person), the same-day fact said of all curb rash, a paint match, and one request sent under the placeholder name "Caller" after the model never asked for a name. Each is a measure for Task 10's comparison (unsupported claims per call by model); the wording here already says what the business says, and adding facts to deny each invention is not the way to fix a model's reach.

### Seen, not changed

- "scuffed rim edge" was quoted the curb rash range; scratch and scuff repair has no price in the file. A fair reading of the damage, or a price the owner should add.
- The decision model did not read "yeah that's all right i think" as a yes; a hedge at a read-back is meant to fail safe.
- A caller who refused a callback note heard that calling during the windows reaches a person: the business says nothing of a phone answered by people.
