# Example Wheel Works: the agent-mode demo

Example Wheel Works is invented: a small wheel repair and refinishing shop inside a detailing shop at 400 Commerce Drive, Rivergate. Its line is the demo of [agent mode](../../docs/designs/2026-11-08-agent-mode.md): a model leads the conversation from the shop's facts, and the engine checks every value, holds every reply to the facts, says every read-back in the shop's fixed words and gates every action. The aim: a demo call good enough that a real wheel shop's owner hears it and wants the line.

No real business, place, person or number is in this folder: the dealerships are invented and the numbers are in the 555 range. The shop's real number and phone are settings (`TEXT_FROM`, `SHOP_PHONE`), never in the folder, and so is a tester's phone recognized as Rivergate Motors (`DEALER_TEST_LINE`, `env.DEALER_TEST_LINE` among the account's numbers: set privately on a host so the dealer flow can be heard on a real call; unset, left out).

## The two demo calls

- **A dealership regular**, recognized by the number it calls from (+15555550142 is Rivergate Motors), books three trade-ins into tomorrow morning's drop-off window. Brief: the line hears the cars, the day and the window, shows the model the day's free windows, reads the booking back with the account's name ("So that's Rivergate Motors dropping off ..., three in all, on Wednesday, November 11 in the morning. Shall I book it?") and books it on the yes. The shop gets a summary text. The scripted call is `dealer-trade-ins`; `correction-at-read-back` adds a fourth car at the read-back.
- **A retail caller** with curb rash on a BMW gets a warm welcome and reassurance from the claims ("Most curb rash can be fixed, and we see it every day"), help telling whether the wheel is diamond cut (from the services' own words), the price line when they ask (no figure, ever), a day and a window, the number they're calling from offered, and whether the shop may text them there. The request is read back; on the yes the line sends it to the shop, texts the caller (with their consent) asking them to reply with a photo of the damage, and texts the shop a summary. The photo they reply with is sent on to the shop's phone. The shop confirms the window when it sends the quote. The scripted call is `retail-bmw`; `consent-declined` is a caller who says no to texts and gets none.

## The line's voice

The line speaks as the business: "we", "the shop", "Example Wheel Works", like the front desk of a well-run shop, never as a named person (the maintainer's call 44, 2026-11-08). The first build named the owner (`business.owner: Sam`, `oneOwner: true`, and a `background` line that he works alone), and every line spoke of him: "send Sam a request", "I'll have Sam call you back as soon as he's free." The maintainer wanted a line that sounds like a professional operation, so `business.yaml` has no owner's name, no `oneOwner` and no background line (each pushed the model toward talking about him), and `voice` describes a friendly, professional front desk that speaks for the shop as "we". The claims, the price line, the read-backs, the done lines, the handoff, the callback note, the labels, the tool descriptions and the sample replies all say "we" or "the shop":

| Line | Before (call 43) | Now |
|---|---|---|
| a claim | Sam sees this kind of damage every day. | We see this kind of damage every day. |
| the price line | Sam quotes every job from a photo, ... Once I've sent him your request, you'll get a text with a link to send one, and he'll get back to you ... | We quote every job from a photo, ... Once your request is in, you'll get a text with a link to send one, and we'll get back to you ... |
| the request's read-back | Here's what I'll send Sam: ... and he'll text you at {callbackNumber}. | Here's your request: ... and we'll text you at {callbackNumber}. |
| the request's done line | I'm texting you a link to send Sam a photo. He'll text you a price ... | We're texting you a link to send us a photo. We'll text you a price ... |
| the handoff | I'll have Sam call you back as soon as he's free. | Someone from the shop will call you back as soon as they can. |

**While the model is slow** (call 60, the maintainer's decision; business.yaml's `waiting`): on a call, a turn whose answer has not come 1.2 s in hears "One sec.", "Okay, one moment." or "Let me see." (in turn, never the same twice in a row), and waits for the answer up to 4 s in all before it asks its question again. They speak as the shop, naming no one; the owner may word them their own way.

The price line still promises no text before the request is confirmed (the design's decision 25; since texting, "Texting" below): "Once your request is in" says what happens after the read-back's yes. Since a later live call (the maintainer's, with call 55) it stops at the photo, "We quote every job from a photo, because the price depends on the damage. Once your request is in, we'll ask you for one.": asked at the read-back, the line had said the steps (text, photo, price, drop-off) in the price line and again in the done line straight after. The request's done line drops the caller's name ("Thanks," with the full name sounded stiff on that call): "Thanks, we have your request." then the consent's own part, the steps by text ("Reply to our text with a photo of the damage, and we'll text you a price and confirm your drop-off.") or by a call ("We'll call you about a price and to confirm your drop-off."). The owner's notices are for the shop, so their summary ("New request from ...") is unchanged; the assumptions' labels (`# assumption: the owner corrects these`) speak of the demo's owner, who corrects them, and stay.

## The demo's assumptions, for the owner to correct

The demo needs answers only the owner can give. As with a real client, `business.yaml` makes a reasonable assumption for each, marked in the file with `# assumption: the owner corrects these`. The demo is up front about them: when the owner hears something that isn't how the shop works, that is the prompt for the owner to say how it does, or to leave it out.

| Assumption | In business.yaml | How the owner corrects it |
|---|---|---|
| Curb rash on a diamond cut wheel is usually a same-day fix. | `claims` | Change the claim's words, or delete it: a claim is the only place a turnaround may come from, so without it the line promises none. |
| Cars are dropped off in a window: by 8 in the morning, ready by about noon; or by noon, ready by about 5. | `windows` (`says`) | Change each window's words, rename one, or add or remove a window. A field with `options: windows` takes them as they are. |
| Each window takes up to three cars. | `windows` (`capacity`) | Change the number; the stand-in calendar reads it. Never said by the line. |
| The shop works Monday to Friday. | `days` | List the days he works; the calendar has no window on any other. |
| The work is done at the shop; no mobile service. | `where.mobile` | `true` if he also comes to the customer. |
| The shop doesn't do cracked wheels or straightening. | `notOffered` | Add or remove what the shop doesn't do. |
| The shop quotes every retail job from a photo, and confirms the window with the quote; the price line is for a retail request only. | `prices` (`forms: [request]`), `request` | Change the price line's words (`prices.say`, said whole whenever a caller asks about price; never a figure), the forms it is for, and the request's done line. |
| A quote is texted from the shop page and booked by the customer's reply word, CONFIRM; its words are the shop's. | `texts.quote`, `texts.confirmWord`, `texts.booked` and the shop's `quote*` texts | Change the words or the reply word ("Quote and confirm"). The hold's rule (24 hours, or 6 pm the day before) is the maintainer's decision, in `src/quotes.ts`. |
| Dealership work is billed at the rates agreed with the shop. | `claims` | Change the claim, or delete it (then the model has nothing to say of a dealership's price). |
| Dealerships are booked straight into a window. | `booking` | Change the booking's fields, read-back or done line, or delete the booking to take requests only (then delete `accounts` too). |
| The owner wants a text summary of every request and booking, and each photo a caller texts in. | `owner`, `texts`, the setting `SHOP_PHONE` | Change the shop's phone (`SHOP_PHONE` in the app's settings), the summary's words (`{kind}`, `{who}`, `{details}` and the fields are filled by the tools), or the texts' words (`texts`). |
| A caller is texted only if they say yes when asked, and asked for a photo by reply, not a link. | `request.fields` (`textOk`), `request.then` (`when`), `texts.photoRequest` | Change the question's or the text's words; delete the field and the `when` to text no caller. |
| The first text says "Reply STOP to opt out." | `texts.photoRequest` | Change or drop the line (Twilio answers STOP itself). |

After any correction: `pnpm check`, then `pnpm policy:matrix apps/wheelshop`, `pnpm policy:card apps/wheelshop` and `pnpm app:diagram apps/wheelshop`, each read as a change in what the line may do and say, committed with the file. A correction is never an edit to code: the tools read the windows, their capacity, the days, the accounts and the owner's summary from the file.

## What changed from the design's appendix, and why

The appendix was written before the format was built. The file here is the appendix with what the build decided (the design's call 27, and the reviews):

- **Neutral keys:** `where.place` for `where.shop`, and a window's `capacity` for `cars`. The engine's vocabulary stays neutral.
- **The request has its own write.** A form's write is the first tool of its `then`, so the request is `then: [request, send_photo_link, notify_owner_request]`: the request first, then the photo link and the shop's summary. In the appendix the photo link came first and would have been the request's write.
- **A tool follows one form**, since a call carries its form's values, confirmed at that form's read-back: the booking has its own owner's notice (`notify_owner_booking`), and the request its own (`notify_owner_request`).
- **The handoff is a statement** ("Someone from the shop will call you back as soon as they can."): it is said where nothing waits for an answer. A `callback:` form collects the number, offering the number the caller is calling from, reads it back and leaves the shop a note (`callback`).
- **`countryCode: "1"`** on each digits field that offers the number calling (the engine's default, written out).
- **Every read-back says every required field**, so the caller confirms nothing they never heard. The appendix's request read-back said "{wheels} wheels" ("one wheels"); it now says "on {wheels} of its wheels". The booking's said "{cars} cars"; it now says "{cars} in all". The optional `diamondCut` is in no read-back (a value that may be empty can't be read).
- **Each text field is framed so any words read** (calls 57 to 59): the request says "your {vehicle}, damage: {damage}", and the booking "{cars} in all: {vehicles}". The frame before, "a {vehicle} with {damage}", needed an article and a noun, and call 58 heard "a Infinity with scraped up"; "dropping off {vehicles}" needed a noun phrase the same way.
- **The text fields' figures are free** (the text slot's `figures`, default `free`, the maintainer's decision of 2026-11-10): `vehicle`, `damage` and `vehicles` are each read back whole before anything is sent, so a figure the model wrote down differently from the caller's words ("Infiniti G80" for "Infinity two eighty") is heard and can be corrected, and refusing it had refused near-right cars until the call fell to a callback (call 59: "Porsche 911" for "Porsche nine eleven", refused three times). None needs `figures: cited`, which is for a value acted on without being read back. The line still never says such a figure itself: a reply holds only the figures of a text value that its cited words have too ("An Infiniti G80, nice." is replaced by the field's question).
- **The price line promises no link before the request is confirmed.** The photo link is texted only by the request's read-back's yes, so the price line said before it would promise what the line had not done (the design's call 32). It now says what happens once the request is sent: "We quote every job from a photo, because the price depends on the damage. Once your request is in, you'll get a text with a link to send one, and we'll get back to you with a price and confirm your drop-off."
- **The owner's sample replies** (`business.examples`) are the bake-off's four, sent as examples of tone (measured: with them, no reply held a figure the facts don't).
- **`tools:`** names what each tool does, for the policy card, the console and the audit. **`lines:`** words the two account lines in the shop's terms ("Bookings are for our dealership accounts, but we can take a request instead.").
- **The reply's word budget on a call is 30** (style.yaml), over the engine's 25: the warm opening the demo wants, as the bake-off's cases give it, runs to 26 words.

## The tools (src/app.ts)

Each is a stand-in until the owner says what he uses:

- `availability`: the free drop-off windows on a day, from a calendar held in memory per call: two windows each weekday, each with `capacity` cars, less the cars booked into it, and Friday morning full from a standing dealership drop-off, so the demo has a full window to meet. Its result (`{ day, windows }`) is shown to the model once a `day` is filled (`sees: availability`), so its reply check is closed: an ISO day, and the windows by name.
- `request`, `book`, `callback`: the writes, each done once for its idempotency key, kept in memory and, where `SHOP_DATA_DIR` names a folder, in the shop's log there ("The shop page" below). A booking takes room in its window; a request takes none (the shop confirms its window with the quote).
- `send_photo_link`, `notify_owner_request`, `notify_owner_booking` (and the note `callback` texts the shop): the texts, sent through the server's sender (`ctx.texts`, `TEXT_FROM`) and printed by the server and the text CLI where there is none, or where `SHOP_PHONE` is unset for the shop's (the tests and the regression stay quiet). Each is kept whole on the systems; the trace and the audit show it by its summary and the engine's `text_sent` row, the caller's number by its last four. The owner's summary is `owner.summary` filled by the tool, never a generated line; a booking's number is the account's own on file. The words of every text are business.yaml's `texts`.
- `onText`: a text that comes in (below), and a reply to a quote ("Quote and confirm").
- `src/quotes.ts`: the quote the shop sends from its page, the customer's reply, and the hold's lapse ("Quote and confirm").

## Texting

The maintainer's decisions (2026-11-08; [the texting design](../../docs/designs/2026-11-08-texting.md)): the shop texts from its own Twilio number; the caller gives consent at the request; once the request is confirmed, the caller is texted to reply with a photo of the damage (not sent a link); the photo they reply with goes to the shop's phone with the request it is for.

- **Consent.** The request asks, after the callback number, "Is it OK if we text you at that number?" (`textOk`, yes or no, required). The read-back says it in the shop's words ("..., and we'll text you at 555..." or "..., and we'll call you at 555...", `says`), and so does the done line ("Reply to our text with a photo of the damage, ..." or "We'll call you about a price ..."). `send_photo_link` is held to the yes by the gate (`when: { field: textOk, is: "yes", reason: no-consent }`, a `oneOf` rule): with a no it is refused, audited as `no-consent`, and the request goes on without it. This code never checks the consent; the policy card lists it under "Done only on the caller's answer".
- **The text.** "Thanks for your request with Example Wheel Works. Reply to this text with a photo of the damaged wheel and we'll send you a price. Reply STOP to opt out." (`texts.photoRequest`; the opt-out line is a demo assumption the owner may change, and Twilio answers STOP itself), sent only by the read-back's yes, so the price line ("Once your request is in, we can text you, and you just reply to our text with a photo.") still promises nothing before it (decision 25).
- **The shop's phone** is the app's own setting, `SHOP_PHONE`, never in the folder; unset, the shop's texts are printed. A text to the business is not a text to a caller, so no consent applies to it.
- **A text that comes in** (`onText`, `POST /messages/twilio`): from a number with a request on file in the last week, its photos go to the shop's phone with "Photo from Jordan Lee, ...0177, for their 2020 BMW 3 Series request.", and, where that request said yes to texts (its `textOk` kept on file), the caller gets "Got it, thanks. We'll text you a price soon." (only before the request is quoted: "Quote and confirm" below); a text with no photo is sent on ("Text from ..., about their ... request."), unanswered; from any other number, "Text from ...0133 (no request on file)." with what it brought. The caller's own words follow the line. Twilio's media URLs are passed on as they came: nothing is downloaded.
- **The limits** (the texting design's call 46): the server's sender texts one number at most `TEXT_LIMIT_PER_NUMBER` times a day (default 3) and the app at most `TEXT_LIMIT_PER_HOUR` times an hour (default 20); a text over either is refused, recorded `limited`, reported in the tool's summary and never retried. Put `SHOP_PHONE` in `TEXT_LIMIT_EXEMPT`, so the shop's own phone gets every summary and photo. A chat caller is texted too (they give the number), under the same limits. A text forwarded to the shop is cut to 1,500 characters.
- **Requests on file** (`Systems.onFile`, by the ten digits of the callback number) are read back from the shop's log when the server starts, where `SHOP_DATA_DIR` is set ("The shop page" below), so a photo texted in after a restart still finds its request. With it unset (the tests, the regressions, a server with no folder) they are in memory, and a server restarted forgets them: a photo is then sent on as from a number with no request on file, which still reaches the shop.

## The shop page

The maintainer's ask (2026-11-08): a page where the shop sees what the line took in without opening each call in the console, the first piece of an owner's view. `src/shopPage.ts` serves it at `/wheelshop/shop`, one page the server renders on each request (no script, no build step), plain and readable on a phone, in the brand app.yaml gives (the name and the mark; the chat page's accent where the brand gives no colour):

- **Requests**, newest first: when, the caller's name, their callback number whole (a `tel:` link: the shop calls back), the car, the wheels, the damage, diamond cut, the drop-off day and window, whether they may be texted, and the photos they texted back, each a thumbnail linking to the full image at Twilio's media URL with when it came and any words with it. The browser fetches each photo from Twilio with no referrer; the server downloads nothing.
- **Bookings by day and window**, soonest first: the dealership, the cars, and the window's room used ("3 of 3 cars · full", the standing Friday drop-off counted and said).
- **Texts sent and received**, newest first: when, to or from (the number whole), the words, the photos, and the status. A sent text's status is what the sender answered when it was sent (`queued`: Twilio took it; `failed`, `limited`, or `printed` where there was no sender or no `SHOP_PHONE`): Twilio's delivery reports are not read yet, and the page says so.
- **The callback notes**, newest first: the number to call, when, and the note's reference.

**Never public.** The page shows whole numbers, callers' words and their photos, so its route is a console page (`auth: 'console'`, an engine capability added for it: [the guide's 14.9](../../docs/guide/14-running-it.md#149-looking-at-calls-from-your-phone)). With `CONSOLE_AUTH=local`, the default, it answers only a request made on the server's machine (a 404 through the tunnel), as the console does. With `CONSOLE_AUTH=token` it is served through the tunnel, only to a browser signed in to the console: the owner opens a sign-in link on a phone (`pnpm console:link --next /wheelshop/shop` prints one that opens the page), or the console's Shop button (app.yaml's `console.links`). Each session's first visit is in the audit (`console_access`, `page`). Where the server has no console sign-in it is local only whatever `CONSOLE_LOCAL_ONLY` says (the maintainer's decision: a console page is never public), and a request counts as made on the machine only when it is addressed to `localhost`, `127.0.0.1` or `[::1]` (the engine's fix for DNS rebinding, guide 14.9). Behind the sign-in the page has Sign out. Its answer carries its own policy (its style by nonce, images from https, its one form, Sign out, to this server, no framing, no referrer, never cached), and it shows a photo only at Twilio's media URL (`https://api.twilio.com/`, the engine's `TWILIO_MEDIA_PREFIX`, the only media a text that comes in keeps), whatever else a record holds. The chat page is public; this page never is.

**Kept on file.** Before it, the tools kept everything in memory, so a restart lost it. Now, where the app's setting `SHOP_DATA_DIR` names a folder, every record is also appended to `shop.jsonl` there (`src/shopLog.ts`): one JSON line for each request, booking, callback note, text sent and text received (whole: the number, the words, the photos' URLs, the request a photo was matched to), and one for each write's idempotency key, each line with its version and the time it was written. The server reads it back as it mounts the page, so after a restart the page, the windows' room, the requests a photo is matched to and each write's key (a write repeated after the restart is still done once) are as they were. The folder is made mode 700, and one already there that others may read or write is tightened to 700 where the server owns it, or warned of where it does not; the file is mode 600, opened with `O_NOFOLLOW` (a symbolic link there stops the server, naming it) and written only through that one descriptor. A line cut short by a crash, or one whose fields are not of their types, is skipped, counted in the startup log, and the next line written on a line of its own.

A write's key goes on file only once the write is done; while it is under way the key is held in memory, so a repeat in the same turn is still caught. A server that stops with a text in flight has no key on file for it, so after the restart a repeat of that write sends the text again, as a restart always did before the file store (the engine's own text keys are in memory too).

What the store is, and is not:

- **The shop's own system.** It keeps the callers' numbers and words whole, as the shop needs them to call back and quote. app.yaml's `records` (the caller's number by its last four in the audit, the trace and the console) is the engine's rule for its own records and does not apply to it, as it does not apply to the texts the shop's phone receives.
- **Nothing is deleted yet.** The log only grows. To rotate it or delete it: stop the server, move `shop.jsonl` out of the folder (keep or delete it as the shop's own retention says), and start the server, which begins a new one. Records moved out are gone from the page and from matching a photo to its request.
- **One server's.** Each server reads the folder once, as it starts, and keeps the rest in memory: two servers on one folder would not see each other's records. A line served by several servers keeps its requests where it keeps everything else (a database), not here. Unset, the default, the records are in memory as before (so the tests and the regressions keep their behaviour; a scripted call's settings never name it), and the server warns that a restart loses them. Nothing the model is sent changes.

Set on the host: `SHOP_DATA_DIR` (a folder on the server's own disk, outside the repository), and, for the phone, `CONSOLE_AUTH=token` with `CONSOLE_SESSION_KEY` (so a sign-in survives a restart).

## Quote and confirm

The maintainer's decisions (2026-11-08; the agent-mode design's calls 52 and 53): a retail request becomes a booking only after the customer accepts the shop's quote. `src/quotes.ts` is the flow; the shop page is where it starts.

1. **The form.** Each request card on the shop page has a "Quote and book" form until the request is booked: the price (an amount: "180", "$180", "180.50"), the drop-off day and window, and Send. The window is one select of the windows with room in the next two weeks, on the days the shop opens, in the calendar the dealerships book into (`Systems.roomIn`: capacity less the standing drop-off, the bookings and the holds), leaving out any day whose 6 pm the day before has passed; the caller's own choice is picked where it has room ("(their choice)"), a re-quote's own window where it is quoted again. A request whose caller said no to texts has no form: the card says to call them, with the number.
2. **Sending** (`POST /wheelshop/shop/quote`) checks the form's Origin and its token, the request (on file, not booked, its `textOk` a yes), the form's version, the price and the window, then keeps the quote (the shop's log, `quote`), which holds one car of room in its window, and texts the customer `texts.quote` in business.yaml's words at `+1` and the request's callback number, then sends the browser back to the card (303). A newer quote for the same request supersedes the one waiting, its hold moved, in the same line of the log. The card shows "Quoted, waiting" with the price, the window, the hold's end and the text's status; a quote whose text did not go (`limited`, `failed`, `refused` by the page's sender, or `printed` with no sender) says so, with why and the number to call (call 53). A waiting quote has a **Withdraw** button (`POST /wheelshop/shop/withdraw`, its own token): the hold is released, the quote withdrawn (a line of the log), the card "Withdrawn", and the request may be quoted again. The form asks whether to text the customer ("Text them that it is withdrawn", `texts.quoteWithdrawn`), unticked by default: the shop calls if it wants (call 53).
3. **The reply** (`onText`): a text from the number a quote was sent to, matched exactly (a `+1` number, the engine's `sameNumber` against `+1` and the quote's ten digits: "+445555550177" is not a reply, call 53), while the quote is its request's latest and is waiting, is read by the decision model (`ctx.decide`, [the guide's 13.17](../../docs/guide/13-channels.md#1317-texts-sending-and-receiving)), never by matching the word. Two typed questions: `acceptsQuote`, the goal only (does the reply accept the quote as offered and ask to book it?), and `asksChange` (does it ask for another day, time or price, ask a question, or decline), sent with the quote as texted and the reply word as context (`about`), with no instruction to match the word (call 53). A yes is `acceptsQuote` at the read-back's bar (`CONFIRM_YES`, 0.7), above `asksChange`, and `asksChange` under 0.5; so "confirm", "CONFIRM!", "yes confirm" and "sounds good, book it" book, and "can we do Wednesday instead?" does not. The reply word is business.yaml's `texts.confirmWord` (CONFIRM where it is left out; not YES, which Twilio takes as an opt-in keyword).
   - **The quote is looked at again** once the model has answered, on the clock of that moment (call 53): if meanwhile the shop sent a newer quote, withdrew it, or the request was booked, the reply goes to the shop as `texts.quoteReply`; nothing is booked. A quote that is not waiting, not its request's latest, or whose request has a booking is never booked (`bookQuote`), so two yeses read at once book once.
   - **A yes** books the held window (a booking of one car, the request's car, with the quote it came from: the hold becomes the booking in one line of the log), texts `texts.booked` to the number that consented (the request's callback number, never the number the reply came from), and texts the shop `texts.quoteBooked`, which adds `texts.customerNotTexted` ("Customer not texted (limited): call them.") where the customer's text did not go (call 53). The card shows "Booked"; the booking joins "Bookings by day and window" under the customer's name.
   - **Anything else**, or no reading (the model failed), goes to the shop's phone as `texts.quoteReply` and the customer's words ("Reply from Jordan Lee, ...0177: can we do Wednesday instead?"), with any photos; the hold stays. The shop sends a new quote from the page if a change is wanted.
   - A photo with no words is sent on to the shop as before, and the "We'll text you a price soon." thank-you goes only to a request not yet quoted.
4. **The hold lapses** at 6 pm the day before the drop-off, or 24 hours after the quote, whichever comes first (`holdUntil`, worked out as the quote is sent and kept with it). 6 pm is in the server's time zone (`TIMEZONE`, the one the shop page shows its times in): neither app.yaml nor business.yaml has a time zone of its own (app.yaml's `hours` has one, and the wheelshop has no hours). A lapse is a line of the shop's log (`quote_state`, `lapsed`); the window is free again, the shop's phone is told (`texts.quoteLapsed`) and the card shows "Lapsed". The check runs on the server's minute sweep (the shop page's route's `sweep`), as the page is drawn, as the server starts (holds whose time ran out while it was stopped), and before a reply is read.
   - **A lapsed quote never books** (call 53). A reply to it (in the week after the lapse) is not read by the model: it goes to the shop as `texts.quoteLate` ("..., so call them. They wrote: ..."), and the customer gets no text. A yes the calendar refuses while the hold is still on (a window over full, as a capacity lowered under the hold would leave it) tells the shop to call (`texts.quoteFull`).
5. **Each card shows its state:** New, Photo in (a photo texted back), Quoted, waiting (the price, the window, the hold's end), Booked, or Lapsed; the page's header counts each.
6. **The forms are protected** as the page is: a console page (`auth: 'console'`: local only, or behind the console's sign-in with `CONSOLE_AUTH=token`, where a POST without a session is a 401), POST only (a GET is a 405), a form's body only (`application/x-www-form-urlencoded`, at most 4 KB), the browser's word that it came from the page (the engine's `formFromOwnPage`, the check the console's sign-in makes: Sec-Fetch-Site, when sent, same-origin; the Origin absent, `null` or the server's own; call 53), and a token of its own: an HMAC of what the form does (quote or withdraw), the request and its version under a key the server makes as it starts, so a page drawn before a restart is refreshed before it sends (403, saying so). A double submit is once: the quote's write key is the request and the form's version, so the second submit returns the first quote and sends no text; a form drawn before another quote was sent (an older version) is refused (409: refresh).
7. **The shop's texts** go to `SHOP_PHONE`, as every summary does; put it in `TEXT_LIMIT_EXEMPT`. A customer may get four texts in a day (the photo request, the thank-you, the quote and the booking), one more for each new quote the shop sends and one for a withdrawal it chooses to text. The engine's default `TEXT_LIMIT_PER_NUMBER` is 3 (kept), so the booking's text may be refused (`limited`), and the shop is then told to call: the wheelshop's `.env.example` suggests 5, and the server notes at start when the limit is under 5.
8. **The page's sender is held to its numbers** (call 53): the shop page texts through the app's sender (`AppPageDeps.texts`) wrapped in an allow-list (`allowListTexts`): the shop's phone, and a request's callback number whose caller said yes to texts. Any other number is refused (`refused`, never sent) and logged by its last four. The page's texts' keys (`quote R101 0`, `lapsed Q101`, `withdrawn Q101`) are the audit's ids, so they hold references only, no number and no name.
9. **The decision model is asked only for a waiting quote**: every asking is a model call (the audit's `text_decision` row counts its tokens), and the engine puts no limit on how often a hook asks, so a text from any other number is never sent to the model.
